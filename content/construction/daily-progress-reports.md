## What is a daily progress report (DPR)?

A daily progress report is the record of one site for one day: the work done against each BOQ item, the labour and
machines on site, the material used, the weather, any hindrance, and photos. It is the contractor's own record, kept
whether or not the client asks for it, because it's what later proves progress for a bill, cost for the margin, and
the cause of a delay for an extension of time.

## What should a daily progress report record?

Everything that happened on the site that day that someone may later have to prove:

- **Work done**, per BOQ item and location, in the item's unit.
- **Labour**, by trade, from that day's attendance, with absentees and the reason.
- **Machines** on site and the hours each ran.
- **Material** used from site stock, per item.
- **Weather**, issues and hindrances, with who caused them and since when.
- **Instructions** received from the client's engineer.
- **Photos**, dated, of the work done and of any problem.

## Which site records does CPWD require?

On CPWD works the Works Manual names the site documents: the site order book, a physical inspection register where
applicable, the cement register and the dismantle register, kept under its standard operating procedure SOP 5/14.
For projects, the inspection register is online, through CPWD's project management system, for senior officers to
record their observations from the start of work to completion.

## What is the site order book, and why does it matter?

It's the book on site where the client's engineer writes instructions to the contractor. Under CPWD's general
conditions, the contractor's technical representative notes down instructions conveyed by the Engineer-in-Charge in
the site order book and signs it, and the Engineer-in-Charge's decision as recorded there is final and binding on the
contractor. Anything the engineer orders on site should therefore be in the site order book and, on the contractor's
side, in that day's DPR.

## How are hindrances recorded on CPWD works?

In the site order book. The physical hindrance register was dropped for works whose tenders were uploaded on or after
19 February 2019; now, when a hindrance causing delay comes to the junior or assistant engineer's notice, he writes it
in the site order book and reports it to the Engineer-in-Charge at once. A contractor should record the same hindrance
in its DPR the same day, with the date it started and photos, because extensions of time are argued from these dates.

## Who should approve a daily progress report?

The project manager, before it counts. A report filed by the site engineer and approved by the PM is a checked
record: only approved work should move the site's progress, feed the next measurement and bill, or count toward the
weekly report to the client. A report sent back with a note is corrected and filed again.

## Why file the DPR from a phone?

Because the work is on site and the evidence is on site. A report written in the evening from memory misses
quantities and photos; one filed on a phone from the site, with attendance marked there, carries the time, the place
and the pictures. Officers also inspect works often to check quality and specifications, and a dated photo record
answers their questions quickly.

## How do daily reports become the weekly report and the bill?

By adding them up rather than rewriting them. The week's approved reports give work done per BOQ item, labour
man-days by trade, material used, machine hours, issues and photos for the client's weekly progress report; the
same approved work feeds the [measurement book](/construction/measurement-book) and the material check against BOQ
norms in [material consumption](/construction/material-consumption-vs-boq).

## What does the construction demo do?

In the [construction demo](/industries/construction-company), the site engineer files one report per site per day
from a phone: quantity done against each BOQ line, labour by trade filled from that day's attendance, material used
from the site's own stock, machine hours, weather, issues and up to six photos, shrunk on the phone before upload.
Attendance is marked per worker on site, and a mark made away from the site's map pin is flagged. The project manager
approves the report or returns it with a note, and only approved work counts toward progress. The weekly report for
the client is built from the week's approved reports as a PDF, with sections chosen per client, and an approved
report is posted to the site's Telegram group where one is set up. Datum AI answers "which sites haven't sent
today's report?". It doesn't keep the site order book or the cement register in CPWD's format, or record hindrances
as their own entries for an extension of time claim; those are parts we'd build.
