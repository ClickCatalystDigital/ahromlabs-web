## What is a works contract under GST?

A works contract is a contract for building, construction, fabrication, completion, erection, installation, fitting
out, improvement, modification, repair, maintenance, renovation, alteration or commissioning of any **immovable
property**, where property in goods passes in the course of the work (section 2(119) of the CGST Act, 2017). The GST
Council's note stresses the limit: only work on immovable property qualifies. Painting a car or fabricating a
machine with your own material is a composite supply, but not a works contract.

## Is a works contract a supply of goods or of services?

Of services. Schedule II, paragraph 6(a) of the CGST Act treats a works contract as a supply of services, so the
whole contract value, materials included, is taxed as one service, not split into goods and labour as it was under
VAT and service tax.

## What is the GST rate on a works contract?

Generally 18%: 9% CGST plus 9% SGST (or 18% IGST), with lower rates only for the works a rate notification names.
The rates are in serial number 3 of Notification 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate), as amended. The GST Council's 2017 note
listed the general entry at 9% + 9% and lower entries at 6% + 6% for works such as roads and bridges for public use,
low-cost housing and some government works. Those lower entries have been changed several times since; from 22
September 2025, Notification 15/2025-Central Tax (Rate) moved items (vii), (viii) and (x) of serial 3 to 9% CGST.
Take the rate for your contract from the notification as it stands on the date of supply, or from your tax adviser,
not from an old article.

## Can the client claim input tax credit on my bill?

Not when the client uses your works contract to build its own immovable property, such as an office or a factory
shed: section 17(5)(c) blocks that credit, except for plant and machinery. A main contractor that buys a
subcontractor's works contract to supply its own works contract can take the credit.

| Who receives the works contract service | Input tax credit |
|---|---|
| A business building its own office, factory shed or other immovable property | Blocked (section 17(5)(c)) |
| The same, for plant and machinery | Allowed: plant and machinery is excluded from the block |
| A main contractor buying a subcontractor's works contract to supply its own works contract | Allowed |

## Can a contractor claim ITC on cement, steel and other inputs?

Yes, when they go into a works contract you supply to a client: you charge GST on that supply and set the credit
against it. Section 17(5)(d) blocks credit only for goods and services used to construct immovable property on your
own account, such as your own office or godown (other than plant or machinery).

## What is GST TDS, and when does the client deduct it?

Tax deducted at source under section 51 of the CGST Act: government departments, local authorities, government
agencies and other notified persons deduct 1% CGST plus 1% SGST from payments for
a contract whose taxable value exceeds ₹2.5 lakh. It's worked out on the value excluding GST, and the deductor pays it
to the government within ten days after the end of the month. It isn't deducted when the supplier's location and the
place of supply are in a state other than the one where the recipient is registered. It is separate from
[income-tax TDS](/construction/tds-on-contractor-payments); both can come off the same bill.

On a ₹18,50,000 RA bill to a state department, GST TDS is ₹18,500 CGST plus ₹18,500 SGST: ₹37,000.

## Which state's GST applies to a works contract?

The state where the property is. The GST Council's note says the place of supply "would be where the immovable
property is located" when supplier and recipient are both in India. So a Gujarat contractor working on a site in
Maharashtra charges IGST to a client registered in Gujarat; CGST plus SGST apply where the contractor and the site are in
the same state.

## When is GST due on an RA bill?

At the time of supply: for services, the earlier of the invoice date (if the invoice is issued in time) and the date
payment is received (section 13). Each [running account bill](/construction/ra-bills) is a supply, so the contractor
issues a tax invoice for the bill's value with GST on top.

| Line | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Gross value of RA bill #3 | 18,50,000 |
| CGST 9% | 1,66,500 |
| SGST 9% | 1,66,500 |
| **Invoice value** | **21,83,000** |

The figures are made up.

## Is GST payable on a mobilisation advance or on security deposit?

Ask your adviser before you decide. For services, receiving a payment can fix the time of supply, but whether an
interest-bearing mobilisation advance is a payment for the supply is argued both ways. Security deposit is deducted
from a bill whose full value has already been invoiced; how a deposit that is later forfeited is taxed is a separate
question, and practitioners disagree on it.

## What should a contractor's system do for GST?

- Classify each contract as a works contract or not, with the place of supply (where the property is).
- Issue a GST invoice for each RA bill from the [measured](/construction/measurement-book) value.
- Keep input tax credit apart where it is blocked (your own buildings) and where it is allowed.
- Record the GST TDS each government client deducts against the bill it belongs to.
- Reconcile GST invoices with RA bills and payments, so nothing is billed or credited twice.

In the [construction demo](/industries/construction-company), RA bills and trading invoices carry HSN or SAC codes
and the CGST and SGST or IGST split, and the month's GST checklist shows output tax and the GSTR-1 summary by
customer and HSN, with what's missing and the due dates. It doesn't yet record GST TDS deducted by clients or tag
input tax credit as blocked or allowed; those are parts we'd build.
