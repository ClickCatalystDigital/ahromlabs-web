## What is certified payroll?

Certified payroll is a weekly payroll report, signed under a statement of compliance, that a contractor files on a
construction job covered by the Davis-Bacon and Related Acts. It shows every laborer and mechanic who worked on the
contract that week, the work they did, their hours and what they were paid, so the agency can check that each got at
least the prevailing wage set for the job. The law behind it, the Copeland Act, says each contractor and subcontractor
"each week must furnish a statement on the wages paid each employee during the prior week".

## Which jobs need certified payroll?

Federal and federally funded or assisted contracts over $2,000 for construction, alteration or repair, including
painting and decorating, of public buildings or public works. "Related Acts" are the laws under which federal
agencies help pay for construction through grants, loans, loan guarantees and insurance, so a state or city job
paid partly with federal money can be covered too. The contract says so: it carries the Davis-Bacon clauses and the
wage determination for the job.

## Who has to submit it?

Every contractor and subcontractor with laborers or mechanics on the covered work, weekly, for each week in which
covered work is done. Payrolls go to the federal agency when it is a party to the contract, or otherwise to the
applicant, sponsor or owner that keeps the records for the agency. The prime contractor is responsible for getting
every sub's certified payroll in, so a late or wrong payroll from a sub is the prime's problem too.

## Do I have to use Form WH-347?

No, but you must report the same information weekly. DOL's WH-347 is optional; a covered contractor can use its own
format or an electronic system the agency or prime allows, as long as it has every required field and the signed
Statement of Compliance (page 2 of WH-347, or a document with identical wording). Each weekly payroll is numbered,
starting at 1 for the project, and the last one is marked final.

## What goes on a certified payroll?

For each worker: name, Social Security number, last known address, telephone number and email, the classification of
the work actually done, the hourly rate paid including fringe benefits, the daily and weekly hours worked in total
and on each covered contract, deductions made, and the actual wages paid. The payroll also names the project, the
contract number, the wage determination numbers that apply, and the week-ending date.

## What does the Statement of Compliance certify?

That the payroll is accurate and complete, that the required records are kept, and that each laborer and mechanic,
including registered apprentices, was paid at least the required prevailing wage, including fringe benefits, for the
work done. Falsifying it can bring civil or criminal prosecution under 18 U.S.C. 1001 and the False Claims Act
(31 U.S.C. 3729).

## How are prevailing wages and fringe benefits paid?

At no less than the rate for each worker's classification on the job's wage determination, which the Department of
Labor sets from local prevailing wages. The fringe part can be paid into bona fide benefit plans, or as cash in lieu
of fringe benefits. A made-up example: a classification at $38.50 an hour plus $14.20 in fringe, paid entirely in
cash, for 40 hours in a week:

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base wages, 40 hours × $38.50 | $1,540.00 |
| Fringe paid in cash, 40 hours × $14.20 | $568.00 |
| **Gross for the week** | **$2,108.00** |

A worker doing work in more than one classification may be paid the rate for each for the time actually worked in
it, if the payroll records show that time accurately.

## Is overtime required on Davis-Bacon jobs?

On prime contracts over $100,000, yes: the Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards Act requires laborers and
mechanics, including guards and watchmen, to be paid at least one and a half times their regular rate for every hour
over 40 in a workweek. The Fair Labor Standards Act's overtime rules may also apply.

## How long must certified payroll records be kept?

During the work and for 3 years after all the work on the prime contract is completed. The underlying payroll records
must be kept for the same period, and an electronic system used to submit payrolls must give the contractor, the
agency and DOL access to them for at least 3 years.

## Did the Davis-Bacon rules change recently?

Yes. DOL's final rule updating the Davis-Bacon regulations took effect on 23 October 2023. On 24 June 2024 a federal
court in the Northern District of Texas enjoined three of its provisions nationwide (on material suppliers, on-site
time of delivery truck drivers, and applying the clauses by operation of law when an agency left them out of a
contract); the rest of the rule is in effect. Check DOL's page for the current status before relying on those three.

## What should a contractor's system do for certified payroll?

Keep, per worker and per day, the job, the classification of the work done and the hours, so the weekly payroll can
be built rather than retyped:

- Each job flagged as covered, with its wage determination and the rate per classification.
- Daily hours per worker per classification and per job, from the field.
- Rates paid, fringe paid in cash or into plans, deductions and net pay, per week.
- A weekly payroll per job in WH-347 layout, numbered, with the Statement of Compliance for signature.
- Subcontractors' weekly payrolls received and checked by the prime.

The same jobs, crews and hours feed each job's [pay applications](/construction/pay-applications) and the payments to
subs that [prompt payment acts](/construction/prompt-payment-acts) time, so they belong in one system.

The [construction demo](/industries/construction-company)'s US build has daily attendance per worker and job and a
monthly pay register with crew totals; payroll taxes stay with the payroll provider. It doesn't do certified payroll:
no wage determinations, classifications per hour of work, weekly WH-347 reports or subcontractor payroll tracking.
That would be a part we'd build for a contractor on federal work.
