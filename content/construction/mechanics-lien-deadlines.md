## What is a mechanics lien?

A mechanics lien is a claim against the property itself for unpaid work or materials on it: if the bill isn't paid,
the claimant can ask a court to have the property sold to pay it. Every state has its own lien statute, and each has
deadlines. Miss one and the lien is lost, even if the money is owed. Many states also require some claimants to send
a notice early in the job (a preliminary notice, notice to owner or notice of claim) to keep the right to a lien.

## What is a preliminary notice, and who has to send one?

A preliminary notice tells the owner, and usually the general contractor and lender, that you're working on the
project and may claim a lien if you aren't paid. Subcontractors and suppliers usually have to send it; a general
contractor with a contract directly with the owner often doesn't. It isn't a lien and it isn't a
[lien waiver](/construction/lien-waivers); it only keeps the right open.

## What are the deadlines in California, Texas, Florida and New York?

| State | Preliminary notice | Lien deadline | Suit to enforce |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | Within 20 days of first furnishing work (Civil Code 8204) | Direct contractor: after completing its contract, before the earlier of 90 days after completion of the project and 60 days after a notice of completion (8412). Others: before the earlier of 90 days after completion and 30 days after a notice of completion (8414) | Within 90 days of recording the lien (8460) |
| Texas | Subs and suppliers: notice of claim by the 15th day of the third month after the month the work or materials were provided (2nd month on residential projects) (Property Code 53.056) | Lien affidavit by the 15th day of the fourth month after the month work was completed (third month on residential projects) (53.052) | Within one year of the last day to file the affidavit (53.158) |
| Florida | Notice to owner before starting or within 45 days of starting, for those not in contract with the owner (Statutes 713.06) | Within 90 days after final furnishing of labor, services or materials (713.08) | As the statute sets |
| New York | Not required by Lien Law 10 | Within 8 months after completion or final furnishing; 4 months for a single-family home; 90 days after retainage was due for a retainage lien (Lien Law 10) | As the statute sets |

These are the main deadlines only. Each statute has its own form, service rules and exceptions, so read the state's
text, or ask a construction lawyer, before relying on a date.

## How do you count a California lien deadline?

From completion, or from the owner's notice of completion if one is recorded, whichever gives the earlier date. A
subcontractor on a project completed on 1 June 2026, where the owner records a notice of completion on 10 June, must
record its lien by 10 July 2026: 30 days after the notice, which is earlier than 30 August, 90 days after completion.
The general contractor has until 9 August, 60 days after the notice. Each then has 90 days after recording to sue.

## What happens if a California preliminary notice is late?

You don't lose everything, but you lose the work before it. A late notice still protects work done within the 20 days
before it was served and after. A sub that started on 2 March 2026 should have served notice by 22 March; serving on
1 April protects only work from 12 March onward.

## How do you count a Texas lien deadline?

By months, not days: to the 15th day of a month after the month the work was done. A subcontractor on a commercial
job that provided labor in March 2026 and wasn't paid must send its notice of claim by 15 June 2026 (the third month
after March) and, if work ended that month, file its lien affidavit by 15 July 2026 (the fourth month). A suit to
foreclose must follow by 15 July 2027.

## How long do you have in Florida and New York?

Florida: 90 days after you last furnished labor, services or materials, with the notice to owner served within 45
days of starting. Work that started on 2 March and ended on 1 June 2026 needs notice by 16 April and the claim of
lien recorded by 30 August 2026. New York: 8 months after completion or your last work, so work last done on 1 June
2026 can be liened until 1 February 2027 (1 October 2026 for a single-family home).

## Can you put a lien on a federal project?

The remedy on a federal job is the prime contractor's payment bond, under the Miller Act, rather than a lien on the
property. A sub or supplier with a contract directly with the prime can sue on the bond once it hasn't
been paid in full 90 days after its last work. A second-tier sub or supplier, in contract with a sub but not the prime,
must first give the prime written notice within 90 days of its last work. Any suit must be brought within one year of
the last labor or material.

## Can a lien be filed for unpaid retainage?

Yes, where the statute allows it, often on its own clock. In New York a lien for [retainage](/construction/retainage)
can be filed within 90 days after the date the retainage was due to be released; in Texas a sub claiming a lien for
retainage has its own affidavit deadline, counted from the month the original contract was completed, terminated or
abandoned (Property Code 53.052(d)).

## How should a contractor track lien deadlines?

From the dates the system already has: the day each job and each sub started, the last day each worked or delivered,
completion, any notice of completion, and the state of the project. From them, every deadline can be worked out per
job and per party:

- Preliminary notices due and sent, per sub and supplier, with proof of service.
- Lien deadlines per job, counted under that state's rule, with a reminder well before each.
- Lien waivers received against each payment, so nobody is paid without one.
- Retainage still held, with its release date and any lien deadline for it.

The [construction demo](/industries/construction-company)'s US build tracks lien waivers per pay application, ours
and our subs' and suppliers', conditional or unconditional. It doesn't yet send preliminary notices or work out lien
deadlines by state; those are parts we'd build.
