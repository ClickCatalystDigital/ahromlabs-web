## Which law governs contract labour in construction now?

The Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (the OSH Code). It repeals the Contract Labour
(Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970, and the OSH (Central) Rules, 2026, published on 8 May 2026, replace the Contract
Labour Central Rules, 1971. PF, ESIC and the building workers' cess now sit in the Code on Social Security, 2020. The
Central Rules apply where the Central Government is the appropriate government; states make their own rules for the
rest, so check the state's rules for your site.

## Does a construction contractor need a contract labour licence?

Yes, once it employs 50 or more contract workers. The contract labour part of the OSH Code applies to every
establishment that employed 50 or more contract workers through contract on any day of the preceding twelve months,
and to every manpower supply contractor that employed 50 or more. A contractor it applies to can't supply or engage
contract labour, or execute work through it, without a licence. Under the Central Rules no licence is needed up to 49
contract workers, and a licence is valid for five years, applied for online.

It doesn't apply where the work is only intermittent or casual, but work done for more than 120 days in the preceding
twelve months, or seasonal work for more than 60 days a year, doesn't count as intermittent.

## Who pays contract workers, and what if the contractor doesn't?

The contractor pays the wages of each worker it employs, through a bank transfer or other electronic mode, and tells
the principal employer electronically what it paid. If the contractor doesn't pay in time, or pays short, the
principal employer must pay the wages in full or the unpaid balance, and can recover it from the contractor. Under the
Central Rules, when wages aren't paid within seven days of the end of the wage period, the principal employer pays
within fifteen days and recovers the amount from the contractor.

| | Contractor | Principal employer |
|---|---|---|
| Licence (50 or more contract workers) | Holds it | Engages only licensed contractors |
| Wages | Pays, by bank transfer, and reports to the principal employer | Pays if the contractor doesn't, and recovers |
| Work order | Reports it within 15 days of receiving it | |
| Returns | Half-yearly, in Form XVIII | |

## Can contract labour be used for core work?

Not in an establishment's core activities, with exceptions. The Code prohibits contract labour in the core activity of
any establishment, but allows it where that work is ordinarily done through contractors, where it doesn't need
full-time workers for most of the day, or for a sudden increase in work that has to be finished in a set time. Civil
and other construction work, including maintenance, isn't treated as a core activity of an establishment that isn't
set up for it, so a factory or office hiring a contractor to build or repair is not affected.

## When do PF and ESIC apply to a contractor?

PF applies to every establishment with twenty or more employees, and ESIC to every establishment with ten or more
persons (other than a seasonal factory), under the First Schedule of the Code on Social Security. The Code counts a
contractor as an employer, and a worker hired through a contractor for an establishment's work is treated as employed
in it. So a contractor's site gangs and office staff can bring it over these thresholds, and its compliance matters to
the principal employer too.

## What records should a contractor keep for contract labour?

- Each worker's identity and KYC, with the site they work on.
- Attendance per day per site, which sets the days paid.
- Wages, deductions and the bank transfer that paid them, per wage period.
- PF and ESIC contributions, where the thresholds are crossed.
- The licence number and validity, work orders reported, and the half-yearly returns.

A made-up gang of 12 workers at ₹700 a day for 26 days earns ₹2,18,400 for the month; paid by bank transfer within
seven days of the end of the wage period, with the amount reported to the principal employer, the contractor has met
its wage duty for that period.

## How does this connect to labour cess and TDS?

Separately. [Labour cess](/construction/labour-cess) is 1% of the cost of construction paid to the building workers'
welfare board; income-tax [TDS on payments to contractors](/construction/tds-on-contractor-payments) is deducted
from what a principal employer pays the contractor. Neither replaces the contractor's duty to pay its workers.
