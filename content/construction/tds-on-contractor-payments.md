## What replaced section 194C?

Section 393(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025: TDS on payments to contractors is now serial number 6 of the table in
that section, "Payments to contractors, fees for professional and technical services, etc." The 2025 Act replaced
the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026, so payments made from that date are reported under section 393, not
194C. Older documents, such as the CPWD Works Manual 2022, still refer to section 194C.

## What is the TDS rate on payments to contractors?

1% when the contractor is an individual or a Hindu undivided family (HUF), and 2% for any other contractor (a
company, firm or LLP), under Table serial 6(i). The contractor's status, from its PAN, sets the rate.

| Paid to | Rate | On a ₹5,00,000 bill |
|---|---|---|
| A proprietor (individual) or HUF | 1% | ₹5,000 |
| A company, firm or LLP | 2% | ₹10,000 |

## When does TDS apply to a contractor payment?

When a single payment exceeds ₹30,000, or the payments to that contractor in the year add up to more than ₹1,00,000.
Payments to a subcontractor of ₹25,000, ₹28,000, ₹32,000 and ₹20,000 run to ₹25,000, ₹53,000, ₹85,000 and ₹1,05,000:
the third payment is over ₹30,000 on its own, and the fourth takes the year past ₹1,00,000. How the earlier payments
are treated once the total crosses the limit is a question for your adviser.

## Who has to deduct TDS from a contractor?

A "designated person", as section 402 of the Act defines it for serial 6(i): the Central or a State Government, local
authorities, corporations set up by law, companies, co-operative societies, housing and development authorities,
registered societies, trusts, universities, foreign governments and enterprises, firms, and individuals, HUFs and
others whose sales, gross receipts or turnover in the previous year was over ₹1 crore (business) or ₹50 lakh
(profession).

Other individuals and HUFs deduct under serial 6(ii) instead: 2% on payments for work, but only once their payments
in the year pass ₹50 lakh.

| Payer | Contractor | Rate | Deduct when |
|---|---|---|---|
| A designated person | Individual or HUF | 1% | One payment over ₹30,000, or the year's total over ₹1,00,000 |
| A designated person | Anyone else | 2% | Same limits |
| An individual or HUF not otherwise required to deduct | Any contractor | 2% | Payments for work over ₹50 lakh in the year |

## What counts as "work" for contractor TDS?

Any work under a contract, including supplying labour to carry it out. The Act's definition also brings in
advertising, broadcasting and telecasting, carriage of goods or passengers by any mode other than rail, catering, and
making or supplying a product to a customer's specification with material bought from that customer. Making a product
to specification with material bought from someone else isn't work for this purpose; that is a purchase of goods.

## Does a main contractor deduct TDS from its subcontractors?

Yes, the same way, if it is a designated person: 1% or 2% on each subcontractor's bills once the limits are crossed.
The main contractor suffers TDS on its own bills from the client and deducts it from what it pays its subs; each claims
credit for what was deducted from it.

## Is TDS deducted on the GST part of the bill?

It depends on the guidance under the Act, so confirm with your adviser whether to deduct on the bill's value with or
without the GST shown separately on the invoice, and set your system to match.

## How does TDS sit on an RA bill?

It's deducted from each [running account bill](/construction/ra-bills) at the rate in the table, on the bill's value.
On a ₹18,50,000 RA bill to a company contractor, income-tax TDS at 2% is ₹37,000. A government client may also deduct
GST TDS of ₹37,000 (1% CGST + 1% SGST) from the same bill; that is a separate deduction under the GST law, covered in
[GST on works contracts](/construction/gst-on-works-contracts).

## What happens after TDS is deducted?

The payer deposits it, reports it in its TDS statement and gives the contractor a certificate; the contractor claims
credit for it against its own tax. Check the deposit and filing dates for the year in the rules under the 2025 Act.

## Did the rates change in 2026?

The rates and limits above are read from the table in section 393 as enacted. Rates can be changed by a Finance Act,
so check the table as amended for the year you pay in.

## What should a contractor's system do for TDS?

- Hold each contractor's PAN and status (individual or HUF, or other), which sets the rate.
- Track payments to each contractor through the year, so the ₹1,00,000 total is caught on the bill that crosses it.
- Deduct on the base your adviser confirms, and show TDS as its own line on the
  [measurement-based bill](/construction/measurement-book).
- Record TDS clients deduct from your own bills, so the credit can be matched before you file.
- Produce the deposit and return data per contractor, and the certificate each one needs.

In the [construction demo](/industries/construction-company), the monthly wage register deducts 1% TDS from
subcontractors' wages once they pass the limits, and the TDS register lists deductions by quarter with deposit and
return dates; the return itself stays in Tally. The demo still labels these deductions "194C", and it doesn't yet
record TDS that clients deduct from the contractor's own RA bills; those are changes we'd make.
