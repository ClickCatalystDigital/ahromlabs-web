## What is the holdback in Ontario?

The Canadian equivalent of US [retainage](/construction/retainage), but set by statute rather than by contract. Under
section 22(1) of Ontario's Construction Act, "Each payer upon a contract or subcontract under which a lien may arise
shall retain a holdback equal to 10 per cent of the price of the services or materials as they are actually supplied
under the contract or subcontract", until the liens that could be claimed against it have expired or been dealt with.
It protects subcontractors and suppliers down the chain, which is why it applies at every tier, not just between owner
and general contractor.

## Does it apply if the contract says otherwise?

Yes. Section 22(3): the obligation "applies irrespective of whether the contract or subcontract provides for partial
payments or payment on completion". A contract can't waive the statutory holdback.

## What is the finishing holdback?

A second, separate 10% on work done after the contract is certified or declared substantially performed: the payer
retains "a separate holdback equal to 10 per cent of the price of the remaining services or materials" supplied after
that date. It is released on its own timetable, once the liens for the finishing work have expired.

## When is the holdback paid out?

When the lien period for the work it covers has passed with no liens preserved, or the liens are dealt with. In
practice the trigger is the publication of the certificate or declaration of substantial performance, followed by the
lien period (60 days in most cases under the current Act). The Act also allows release in phases or annually on some
contracts. Check the Act and your contract for the exact rule that applies to your job.

## What are Ontario's prompt payment deadlines?

| Who pays | Deadline | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Owner pays a proper invoice | No later than 28 days after receiving it | s. 6.4(1) |
| Owner disputes it | Notice of non-payment no later than 14 days after receiving it, with reasons | s. 6.4(2) |
| Contractor pays its subcontractors | No later than 7 days after being paid | s. 6.5(1) |

A "proper invoice" is one that meets the Act's content requirements; the clock only starts when one is received. If
the owner gives notice of non-payment, the contractor in turn has to give its subs notice to withhold their share.
Compare the US rules on [prompt payment acts](/construction/prompt-payment-acts).

## Can you show the holdback on a worked invoice?

A subcontractor bills $120,000 for work done in March on an Ontario job (figures made up, before HST):

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Work done this period | $120,000 |
| Basic holdback, 10% | −$12,000 |
| Payable this invoice (before HST and any set-off) | $108,000 |
| Holdback retained on the job to date | $46,500 |

The general contractor holds back the same 10% from its own subs, at every tier. The holdback retained to date is
the figure everyone watches at substantial performance: it is released only when the lien period has run.

## What is a proper invoice?

The invoice that starts Ontario's payment clock. The Act lists what it must contain, including the contractor's name
and address, the date and period covered, what was supplied, the contract or authority under which it was supplied,
the amount payable and the payment terms, and the name and address of the person to send payment to. Contracts can
add requirements, such as a certified progress estimate. If an invoice is missing a required item, it isn't a proper
invoice and the 28 days don't start, so build the required fields into your invoice template rather than relying on
memory.

## How does Ontario compare with US retainage?

| Point | Ontario holdback | Typical US retainage |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Statute (Construction Act) | Contract, capped or regulated by some states |
| Rate | 10% basic plus 10% finishing | Often 5% to 10%, varies by state and contract |
| Who must hold it | Every payer at every tier | Whoever the contract says |
| Released | When the lien period expires | On completion terms in the contract or state law |
| Prompt payment | 28 days owner, 7 days to subs | Varies by state; see prompt payment acts |

Other Canadian provinces have their own holdback statutes and periods; check the province where the work is, not
where you are based.

## What happens if the holdback isn't retained?

An owner or contractor who pays out without retaining the holdback can end up paying twice: liens can be claimed
against the holdback that should have been held, and a payer who released it early may still be liable for it. That
is why the holdback is retained even when the sub is trusted, and why release waits for the lien period, not for a
friendly phone call.

## How should a contractor track holdback?

On every invoice: the 10% held, the cumulative holdback by contract and by subcontractor, the date substantial
performance was certified and published, the lien expiry date, and the release. Track what you hold back from subs
separately from what the owner holds from you, because they are released at different times. Our US construction
demo tracks retainage on each [pay application](/construction/pay-applications) and the amounts held from each sub; a
Canadian setup would carry the 10% holdback, the finishing holdback and Ontario's invoice clocks. See
[software for subcontractors and specialty contractors](/industries/construction-company).
