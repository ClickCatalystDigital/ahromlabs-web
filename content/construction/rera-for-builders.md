## Which projects must be registered with RERA?

Real estate projects where plots, apartments or buildings are sold, above the size thresholds in the Act (generally
land over 500 square metres or more than eight apartments, which states may lower). The promoter registers the
project with the state's RERA authority before advertising or selling, and the registration carries the project's
plans, approvals, schedule and the bank account below. States run their own portals and rules; check yours.

## What is the 70% separate account?

Section 4(2)(l)(D) of the Act requires the promoter to deposit 70% of the amounts realised from buyers, from time to
time, in a separate account in a scheduled bank, to be used only for the cost of land and construction of that
project. Withdrawals must be in proportion to the percentage of completion, certified by an engineer, an architect
and a practising chartered accountant, and the account is audited within six months of the end of each financial
year, with the accountant verifying that the money was used for the project.

## What must be updated during the project?

Quarterly updates on the RERA website: bookings, approvals received, and the status of construction against the
schedule, in the format your state's rules set. Delays and changes to plans have their own rules, including buyer
consent for changes to sanctioned plans in many cases.

## What is the five-year defect liability?

Section 14(3) makes the promoter responsible for rectifying structural defects, workmanship, quality or services
defects that buyers report within five years of handing over possession, at no charge, within 30 days. Builders
usually carry this down to their contractors through retention and [bank guarantees](/construction/bank-guarantee-defect-liability).

## How does a RERA withdrawal work in practice?

A worked example, with made-up figures. A project's estimated cost is ₹40 crore (land ₹10 crore, construction
₹30 crore). By 31 December the promoter has collected ₹18 crore from buyers and deposited 70%, ₹12.6 crore, in the
project's RERA account. The engineer certifies construction at 35% complete, the architect certifies the same stage,
and the chartered accountant certifies the cost incurred: land ₹10 crore paid, construction ₹10.5 crore spent.

| Line | Amount (₹ crore) |
|---|---|
| Land cost incurred | 10.0 |
| Construction cost incurred (35% of ₹30 crore) | 10.5 |
| Total cost incurred | 20.5 |
| Proportion of estimated cost incurred | 51.25% |
| Balance in the RERA account | 12.6 |

The promoter may withdraw against the certified cost incurred, in proportion to completion; here the account holds
less than the certified cost, so the whole balance can be drawn against these certificates, and the rest of the cost
was funded from the promoter's own money.

The certificates go with each withdrawal request to the bank, and the same figures appear in the annual audit. The
weak point is the cost certificate: if site costs are in spreadsheets, the chartered accountant has to rebuild them
every quarter. If costs are booked to the project as they happen, the certificate is a report.

## What usually goes wrong?

- **Collections deposited late or short.** Money received in a general account and transferred later breaks the
  trail; deposit the 70% as each payment is received.
- **Common costs not allocated.** Head office, plant and shared staff costs used across projects need an allocation
  basis the accountant can certify; agree it at the start.
- **Cost to complete not updated.** The estimate in the registration is a baseline; when it changes, the proportion
  of completion changes with it.
- **Quarterly updates missed.** Most state portals show the last update publicly, and buyers check.
- **Contractor bills not tied to measured work.** Progress certified by the engineer should match the running bills
  paid to contractors for the same period.

## What are the penalties?

The Act sets them by contravention. Advertising or selling a project that should have been registered and wasn't can
cost up to 10% of the estimated project cost (section 59); other contraventions by a promoter, up to 5% (section 61);
and failing to comply with an order of the authority can lead to further penalties. Buyers can also claim interest
for delayed possession. State authorities publish their orders, and buyers read them.

## Does RERA apply to a contractor?

Not directly: RERA obligations sit with the promoter (the developer). A contractor building for a developer feels it
through the contract: progress evidence for the engineer's certificate, quality records for the five-year defect
liability, and retention or guarantees held against defects. A contractor whose bills, measurements and site records
are in order makes the developer's certificates easier, which is a selling point when bidding.

## What records make RERA compliance easier?

- Collections per project and per buyer, with the 70% deposited and the date.
- Costs per project, land and construction separately, so the percentage of completion and the withdrawal can be
  certified from the books, not estimated. See [job cost per site](/construction/job-cost-per-site).
- Contractor bills certified against measured work, so progress is evidenced. See [RA bills](/construction/ra-bills).
- Labour cess paid per project; see [labour cess](/construction/labour-cess).
