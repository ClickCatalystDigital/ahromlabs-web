## What is labour cess on construction?

Labour cess is a levy of 1% of the cost of construction, paid by whoever carries out building or other construction
work, to fund the social security and welfare of building workers through each state's Building Workers' Welfare
Board. It's often called BOCW cess, after the Building and Other Construction Workers' Welfare Cess Act, 1996, which
first levied it.

## What law levies labour cess now?

Section 100 of the Code on Social Security, 2020, which repeals the 1996 Cess Act. On 8 May 2026 the Ministry of Labour notified the rate under section 100(1) as one per
cent of the cost of construction, replacing the 1996 notification (S.O. 2899) that had set the same 1%. The Code allows
a rate between 1% and 2%; the notified rate is the minimum.

## Who pays labour cess?

The employer carrying out the building or other construction work, which under the Code includes a contractor. In
practice the owner who gets the building approved pays it in advance, and on government and PSU works the client
deducts it from the contractor's bills. Who bears the cost between owner and contractor is a matter for the contract,
so read the tender's terms before you price.

## Which works are covered, and which aren't?

Building or other construction work is defined widely: buildings, streets, roads, railways, airfields, irrigation,
drainage, power lines, water works, pipelines, towers, dams, bridges, tunnels and more, including construction,
alteration, repair, maintenance and demolition. It doesn't include:

- Work related to a factory or a mine.
- Work that employed fewer than ten workers in the preceding twelve months.
- An individual's (or group's) own house costing no more than ₹50 lakh, or a higher amount notified, within the
  worker numbers the government notifies.

## How is the cost of construction worked out?

All the cost of construction incurred by the employer, except the cost of land and any compensation paid to workers
or their families under the Code's employee compensation chapter. The Social Security (Central) Rules, 2026 have the
employer self-assess it using the state PWD's or CPWD's schedule of rates, another applicable schedule, or the figures
filed with RERA for a building, for the year the work started, certified by a chartered engineer.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total project cost | ₹2,60,00,000 |
| Less cost of land | ₹60,00,000 |
| Cost of construction | ₹2,00,00,000 |
| **Cess at 1%** | **₹2,00,000** |

The figures are made up.

## When is labour cess paid?

In advance, at approval or before work starts, on a self-assessment certified by a chartered engineer. Where a local
authority approves the building, the approval application must carry proof that the cess has been paid online to
the Welfare Board; a project expected to run more than a year can pay for the first year's construction cost and the
rest as it falls due. Within sixty days of completing the work, the employer pays the balance on its final
self-assessment and files a return.

## How is labour cess deducted from a contractor's bills?

On works for a government or a public sector undertaking, the client deducts the cess at the notified rate from the
bills it pays and deposits it with the state's Welfare Board within thirty days. On a ₹18,50,000
[RA bill](/construction/ra-bills), 1% is ₹18,500, deducted alongside TDS and security deposit. Keep each deduction
against its bill, so the final self-assessment counts what was already paid.

## What if labour cess is paid late or short?

The employer owes interest at 1% a month, or part of a month, from the due date until it's paid. If the amount in an
assessment order isn't paid by its date, the cess is treated as in arrears and the authority can impose a penalty of
up to the amount of the cess, after giving the employer a hearing.

## Is labour cess the same as GST or TDS?

No. It's a separate levy under labour law, paid to the Building Workers' Welfare Board, not a tax under the GST or
income-tax laws. A government client may take all three off the same bill: income-tax TDS, GST TDS and labour cess.

## What should a contractor's system track for labour cess?

- Each job's cost of construction, excluding land, and who bears the cess under the contract.
- Cess paid in advance, deducted from each RA bill, and still due on completion.
- Deposit and return dates, so nothing attracts interest.
- The state Welfare Board each job's cess goes to.

The [construction demo](/industries/construction-company) doesn't track labour cess: RA bills carry GST, and payroll
handles PF, ESIC and TDS for subcontractors, but cess paid or deducted per bill isn't recorded. That would be a part
we'd build. For the workers themselves, see [contract labour compliance](/construction/contract-labour-compliance).
