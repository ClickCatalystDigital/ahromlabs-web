## Why lien waivers exist

On a US private job, anyone who supplied labor or material and wasn't paid can usually file a mechanic's lien
against the property, even if the owner paid the general contractor in full. So owners and lenders ask for a waiver
from the contractor, and from each subcontractor and supplier, for every payment: proof that the money reached them
and that they give up the lien for what they've been paid.

## The four kinds

| | Progress payment | Final payment |
|---|---|---|
| **Conditional** | Releases lien rights through a date, once this payment clears | Releases all lien rights for the job, once the final payment clears |
| **Unconditional** | Releases lien rights through a date; effective when signed | Releases all lien rights for the job; effective when signed |

Arizona's statute uses exactly these four forms and makes a waiver unenforceable unless it follows them
substantially. The conditional forms take effect only when the check has been endorsed and paid by the bank. The
unconditional forms carry a notice in the statute: they are enforceable even if you haven't been paid, so if you
haven't been, use a conditional form.

Florida takes another approach: lien rights can't be waived in advance, only for work already furnished; its
statutory progress form says it doesn't cover retention or later work; and nobody may require a waiver different
from the statutory forms.

## When to sign or ask for which

- **Sending a pay application?** Give a conditional progress waiver with it, for the amount billed, through the
  billing date.
- **Payment received and cleared?** Give the unconditional progress waiver for that amount when asked.
- **Final payment, including [retainage](/construction/retainage)?** Conditional final with the request;
  unconditional final once paid.
- **Paying subcontractors?** Collect their waivers for the previous payment before releasing the next one. The
  owner's lender will ask for them with your next [pay application](/construction/pay-applications).

Each state has its own lien law, deadlines and forms. Use the statutory form where the project's state has one,
and read any waiver for words that waive more than the payment it's for, such as claims for pending
[change orders](/construction/change-orders).

## What your system should keep

Per job and per payment: who owes a waiver, which kind, the amount and through-date, whether it came back signed,
and the file. A subcontractor's next payment should show as blocked until the waiver for the previous one is in.
