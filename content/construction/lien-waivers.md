## Why do lien waivers exist?

A lien waiver is a signed release of mechanic's lien rights for work that has been paid for. On a US private job,
anyone who supplied labor or material and wasn't paid can usually file a mechanic's lien against the property, even
if the owner paid the general contractor in full. So owners and lenders ask for a waiver from the contractor, and
from each subcontractor and supplier, for every payment: proof that the money reached them and that they give up the
lien for what they've been paid.

## What are the four types of lien waiver?

There are four: conditional or unconditional, each for a progress payment or the final payment.

| | Progress payment | Final payment |
|---|---|---|
| **Conditional** | Releases lien rights through a date, once this payment clears | Releases all lien rights for the job, once the final payment clears |
| **Unconditional** | Releases lien rights through a date; effective when signed | Releases all lien rights for the job; effective when signed |

[Arizona's statute](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/33/01008.htm) uses exactly these four forms and makes a waiver unenforceable unless it follows them
substantially. Its conditional forms take effect only when the check has been endorsed and paid by the bank; its
unconditional forms carry a notice that they are enforceable even if you haven't been paid, so if you haven't been,
use a conditional form. California and Texas use the same four forms, with a similar notice on the unconditional ones.

## Which states have statutory lien waiver forms?

Arizona, California, Florida, Massachusetts, Michigan, Texas and Wyoming are among the states whose statutes set
the wording of lien waivers; in each, a waiver on the wrong form can fail to release the lien. Several other states also prescribe forms by statute,
and most states set none, leaving the wording to the contract. Before using any form, check the statute in the state
where the project is.

| State | Statute | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Arizona | [ARS 33-1008](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/33/01008.htm) | Four forms; a waiver is unenforceable unless it substantially follows them |
| California | [Civil Code 8132–8138](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=8132), with [8124](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=8124) and [8126](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=8126) | Four forms; a waiver given before payment, or for a single or joint payee check, is void unless it is in substantially the statutory form. Any other statement waiving lien rights is void unless it follows the statute or the claimant has been paid in full |
| Florida | [713.20](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.20.html) | Progress and final forms; lien rights can't be waived in advance, and nobody may require a waiver different from the statutory forms |
| Massachusetts | [G.L. c.254 §32](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIII/TitleIV/Chapter254/Section32) | A contract term barring a lien or a notice of contract is void, except listed waivers. One statutory form, a partial waiver and subordination given against a payment application, "with no material deviation"; it carves out retainage, unpaid or pending change orders and disputed claims, and is signed under the penalties of perjury, not notarized |
| Michigan | [MCL 570.1115](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-570-1115) | Four forms (partial and full, conditional and unconditional), used "in substantially the following format"; a conditional waiver takes effect on payment. Requiring a waiver in advance of work is void except to the extent of payment actually made. No notary block |
| Texas | [Property Code 53.281–53.287](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.53.htm) | Four forms; a waiver is unenforceable unless it substantially complies with 53.284. The current forms have no notary block |
| Wyoming | [29-10-101](https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title29.pdf) | One waiver form, completed in substantially the statutory wording; it reserves retainage and amounts still unpaid, and ends with a notary's acknowledgment. The same section sets the preliminary notice to the owner, which attaches a waiver form |

"Statutory form" in practice means: copy the statute's form word for word, fill in the blanks, and don't add terms.
Extra releases in a custom form, such as a waiver of claims for pending changes, are the usual reason a form is
challenged.

## What happens if you sign the wrong lien waiver?

Signing an unconditional waiver before the money arrives is the expensive mistake: in states with statutory forms it
is enforceable against you even if you are never paid. California's unconditional forms say so in a notice that must
be in type at least as large as anything else on the form, and Texas prohibits anyone from requiring an
unconditional waiver for a payment the signer hasn't received in good funds.

The other mistakes:

- **A conditional waiver for the wrong amount or through-date.** It releases what it says once paid, so check both
  against the payment.
- **A final waiver for a progress payment.** It gives up everything, including [retainage](/construction/retainage)
  and unbilled work.
- **Missing exceptions.** California's progress forms release rights for change orders fully signed before the
  waiver unless they are listed as exceptions; retention and unpaid extras stay outside. List anything still owed.
- **A non-statutory form in a statutory state.** It may not release the lien at all, which the owner's lender will
  find before you do.

## How do lien waivers work with joint checks?

When the owner or general contractor pays a subcontractor and its supplier with one check payable to both, the
waiver given for it should be conditional. California's and Texas's statutes both require the conditional form when
a single or joint payee check is given in exchange for the waiver, and California treats the payee's endorsement on
a check paid by the bank as evidence of payment. Florida lets a lienor condition a waiver on the check clearing.

In practice: name the maker, amount and payees of the check on the conditional waiver, and give the unconditional
waiver only after the check has cleared and the supplier has been paid its share.

## What is the difference between a progress and a final lien waiver?

A progress waiver releases lien rights for work through a stated date, for one payment; a final waiver releases all
lien rights for the job. Florida's statutory progress form says it doesn't cover retention or work after the date
specified, and California's progress forms leave out retention and unpaid extras. The final forms leave room only
for disputed claims, so anything not listed is given up.

| | Progress waiver | Final waiver |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | Work through the through-date, for this payment | The whole job |
| Retainage | Usually excluded | Included: the final payment normally is the retainage |
| When given | With each [pay application](/construction/pay-applications) and payment | At closeout, with the final payment |
| Exceptions to list | Earlier unpaid progress payments, retention, unpaid extras | Disputed claims for extras |

## Is a preliminary notice the same as a lien waiver?

No. A preliminary notice protects lien rights at the start of the work; a lien waiver gives them up once paid. In
many states a subcontractor or supplier must send a notice to the owner to keep the right to file a lien at all, and
missing the deadline can lose it:

| State | Notice | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| California | Preliminary notice ([Civil Code 8204](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=8204)) | Within 20 days of first furnishing work; a late notice covers only work from 20 days before it was served |
| Florida | Notice to owner ([713.06](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.06.html)) | Before starting, or within 45 days of starting; missing it is a complete defense to the lien |
| Texas | Notice of claim for unpaid labor or materials ([53.056](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.53.htm)) | By the 15th day of the third month after the month the work was done (second month on residential projects) |

So the same subcontractor sends a notice when it starts, then a conditional waiver with each payment request and an
unconditional one once paid.

## How do you track lien waivers per pay application?

Track one row per payer, payee and payment: who owes a waiver, which of the four types, the amount and through-date,
and whether the signed copy is in. A made-up example for pay application 3 on a job paying $63,000:

| From | Type | Through | Amount | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General contractor | Conditional progress | Mar 31 | $63,000 | Sent with pay app 3 |
| General contractor | Unconditional progress | Feb 28 | $45,000 | Signed after the pay app 2 payment cleared |
| Steel subcontractor | Unconditional progress | Feb 28 | $16,200 | Received |
| Electrical subcontractor | Unconditional progress | Feb 28 | $5,400 | **Missing: hold its next payment** |

The lender's draw usually asks for the conditional waiver for this application and the unconditional waivers for
the last one, from the contractor and every subcontractor paid from it.
