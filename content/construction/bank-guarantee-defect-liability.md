## What is a performance guarantee?

A performance guarantee is security the contractor gives the client, at the start of the contract, that it will
perform the contract; the client can claim it if the contractor doesn't. On CPWD works, Clause 1 of the General
Conditions of Contract 2023 requires an irrevocable performance guarantee at the percentage in Schedule E of the
tendered amount, on top of the other deposits in the contract, within the period Schedule F allows after the letter
of acceptance. Schedule E of GCC 2023 sets it at 5% of the tendered value.

## In what form can a performance guarantee be given?

As an insurance surety bond, an account payee demand draft, a fixed deposit receipt, or a bank guarantee from any
commercial bank, under Clause 1. If a bank can't pay against a fixed deposit receipt given as part of the guarantee,
the loss falls on the contractor, who must make it good on demand.

## How long must a performance guarantee be valid?

Up to the stipulated date of completion plus at least 6 months, and longer if the time for completion is extended:
the contractor must get its validity extended to cover the extended time. If it doesn't, the Engineer-in-Charge may
claim the full amount. The CPWD Works Manual 2022 put it differently (valid at least sixty days beyond completion of
all contractual obligations), and CPWD amended Clause 1 and Schedule E by a circular on 27 February 2026; check the
version of the GCC your contract names.

## When is the performance guarantee returned or forfeited?

It's returned, without interest, once the competent authority records the completion certificate. The client can
claim it only for amounts it's entitled to under the contract: the full amount if the contractor fails to extend its
validity, or an amount the contractor owes and hasn't paid within 30 days of a notice. If the contract is determined
or rescinded, the whole guarantee is forfeited. On contracts that include maintenance after construction, half of it
is kept as security deposit and returned year by year.

## What is the difference between a performance guarantee and a security deposit?

The performance guarantee is given once, up front, and returned at completion; the security deposit is built up from
the bills and kept until the defect liability period has run. On CPWD works, 2.5% of the gross amount of each running
and final bill is deducted until the deposit reaches 2.5% of the tendered amount (Clause 1A). Private contracts call
it retention money and set their own rate; see [RA bills](/construction/ra-bills) for how it comes off each bill.

| | Performance guarantee | Security deposit |
|---|---|---|
| Amount (CPWD) | 5% of tendered value | 2.5% of tendered value |
| How given | Up front: bank guarantee, surety bond, FDR or DD | Deducted from each running and final bill |
| Returned | After the completion certificate | After the defect liability period, or when the final bill is passed, whichever is later |

## Can the security deposit be released against a bank guarantee?

Yes. Under Clause 1A, the security deposit deducted from bills can be released against a bank guarantee from a
scheduled bank once at least ₹5 lakh has built up; each guarantee except the last must be for at least ₹5 lakh, and
its validity must follow Clause 17 and any extension of time. That frees cash during the job, using the contractor's
bank guarantee limits instead.

## What is the defect liability period?

The time after completion during which the contractor must make good, at its own cost, defects that appear in the
work from faulty materials or workmanship: twelve months after the completion certificate, or six months for works
costing ₹10 lakh or less (except road works), under Clause 17. If the contractor doesn't fix them after written
notice, the Engineer-in-Charge can have them fixed by others and recover the cost from any money due to the
contractor or from the security deposit.

## When is the security deposit refunded?

Not before the defect liability period ends, or until the final bill has been prepared and passed, whichever is
later. For road works, half may be refunded after six months and the rest after twelve, if the Engineer-in-Charge
thinks half is enough to cover the contractor's liabilities. CPWD issued an advisory on releasing security deposit on
18 November 2025; ask the division for the current procedure.

## What does this look like on a ₹2 crore work?

A made-up CPWD work with a tendered amount of ₹2,00,00,000, stipulated completion 31 December 2026, actual
completion certificate 15 January 2027 (within an extension granted):

| Item | Amount | Held until |
|---|---|---|
| Performance guarantee, 5% (bank guarantee) | ₹10,00,000 | Completion certificate (15 January 2027); validity at least 6 months beyond the extended completion date |
| Security deposit, 2.5%, from bills | ₹5,00,000 | 15 January 2028, or when the final bill is passed, if later |
| Security deposit released against a bank guarantee | ₹5,00,000 | The guarantee then stays in place instead of the cash |

## How should a contractor track guarantees and deposits?

- Every bank guarantee with its amount, purpose (performance, security deposit, mobilisation advance), bank, expiry
  and the date it must be extended to.
- Security deposit deducted per bill, the running total against the cap, and any release against a guarantee.
- The completion certificate date, the defect liability period, defects notified and fixed, and the refund date.
- Bank guarantee commission and limits used, per job.

The CPWD Works Manual itself asks divisions to monitor bank guarantees given for earnest money, performance,
security deposit, mobilisation advance and milestones; a contractor needs the same list from its side. The
[construction demo](/industries/construction-company) doesn't track bank guarantees, security deposits or the defect
liability period yet; those are parts we'd build, from the same RA bills and completion dates. For the price side of
a long contract, see the [price escalation clause](/construction/price-escalation-clause).
