## How does retainage work?

Each [pay application](/construction/pay-applications) shows the work completed to date and holds back a percentage
of it. The held amount builds up over the job; it is paid when the work is substantially complete (sometimes half
at that point and the rest at final completion), less anything needed for unfinished or defective work.

| Month | Billed to date | Retained at 10% | Paid to date |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $50,000 | $5,000 | $45,000 |
| 2 | $120,000 | $12,000 | $108,000 |
| 3 | $160,000 | $16,000 | $144,000 |

The figures are made up. On a $400,000 job, $40,000 waits for the end, often months after the work it covers.

## What are the rules on federal jobs?

FAR 52.232-5 starts from full payment: where progress is satisfactory, the contracting officer authorizes payment in
full. Only when progress isn't satisfactory may the officer retain, up to 10% of the payment, until it is. At
substantial completion the officer keeps only what protects the Government and releases the rest. Retained amounts
are due as the contract says or, if it's silent, 30 days after the officer approves their release (FAR 52.232-27).

The same clause makes prime contractors pass payment down: subcontractors are paid for satisfactory work within 7
days of the prime receiving payment, and the subcontract may permit retainage on the subcontractor as agreed.

## And on private and state jobs?

The contract sets the rate and the release, within the state's law: many states have their own retainage and prompt
payment statutes, for public works and often private jobs too, with caps and release deadlines. Check the law of
the state where the project is, and read the contract's retainage clause before signing it.

## Why it matters for cash and records

- **It's money owed to you, in the balance sheet.** Retainage receivable is earned revenue not yet billable. Track it
  per job, not as one figure.
- **You hold it too.** Retainage you withhold from subcontractors is a payable that must be released on the same
  terms.
- **Release needs paperwork.** Final [lien waivers](/construction/lien-waivers), closeout documents and the punch
  list usually come before the last payment.
- **It moves the WIP schedule.** Billing includes retainage; cash doesn't. See the [WIP schedule](/construction/wip-schedule).
