## What is a punch list?

The list of items that are incomplete or don't meet the contract documents when the work is substantially complete:
a damaged ceiling tile, a missing cover plate, paint touch-up, a door that doesn't latch. It is made on a walk-through
by the owner's representative or architect with the contractor, and each item is assigned to the trade responsible.

## What is substantial completion?

The point where the work, or a part of it, is complete enough that the owner can occupy or use it for its intended
purpose, with only minor items left. It usually matters for three things: warranties start, the owner takes over
utilities and insurance, and many contracts (and some state laws) tie a reduction or release of retainage to it.
The certificate of substantial completion attaches the punch list. See [retainage](/construction/retainage).

## How do you run a punch list?

- Walk early with your own crew and fix what you find before the owner's walk; a short official list is closed
  faster.
- Number each item, with its location (room or gridline), the trade, a photo, and the date found.
- Send each trade only its own items, with a due date.
- Close each item with a photo and the date, and get the owner's representative to sign it off.
- Keep a separate list for items the owner damaged or added: those are not punch items, they are change orders.

## What documents are needed at closeout?

Usually listed in the specifications' closeout section (Division 01):

| Document | From |
|---|---|
| Final punch list, signed off | Owner's representative |
| Warranties and guarantees, with start dates | Each trade and manufacturer |
| Operation and maintenance manuals | Equipment suppliers |
| As-built drawings | Each trade |
| Attic stock and spare parts | As specified |
| Test, balance and commissioning reports | Specialty contractors |
| Final lien waivers, unconditional on final payment as required | Contractor, subs and suppliers |
| Consent of surety to final payment | Bonded jobs |
| Certificates of occupancy and inspections | Authority having jurisdiction |

## How is retainage released at the end?

With the final pay application, once the punch list is complete and the closeout documents are accepted. Final lien
waivers usually go conditional with the final application and unconditional once paid; see
[lien waivers](/construction/lien-waivers). State prompt payment laws often set deadlines for releasing retainage
after completion; check yours on [prompt payment acts](/construction/prompt-payment-acts) and
[retainage](/construction/retainage).

## Why does retainage get stuck?

- One trade's punch items stay open and hold everyone's release.
- O&M manuals or warranties from a supplier never arrive.
- A sub-tier supplier's final lien waiver is missing.
- The final pay application doesn't reconcile to the schedule of values and approved change orders.
- Nobody owns closeout: the project manager has moved to the next job.

Track closeout like a job of its own, with an owner and a date.

## What does a punch list item look like?

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Number | P-0147 |
| Location | Level 2, room 214, east wall |
| Description | Outlet cover plate missing |
| Trade responsible | Electrical |
| Found by and date | Owner's representative, 3 March |
| Photo | Before and after |
| Due date | 10 March |
| Status | Open, fixed (awaiting check), closed |
| Closed by and date | Owner's representative, 9 March |

One line per item, one item per line. "Paint touch-up throughout" is not a punch item; "Touch up paint at door
frame 214, scuffed" is.

## How long should closeout take?

It depends on the job, but the contract usually sets the deadlines: how soon after substantial completion the punch
list must be finished, when closeout documents are due, and how long the owner has to pay the final application.
Track them like any other deadline. On a mid-size commercial job, 30 to 60 days from substantial completion to final
payment is a reasonable target; many contractors wait far longer because closeout starts only when someone asks for
the money.

## What is a closeout checklist for a subcontractor?

1. Your own punch walk done and items fixed before the owner's walk.
2. Owner's punch list items for your trade closed, with photos.
3. Warranties issued, with the start date matching substantial completion.
4. O&M manuals and as-builts submitted and accepted.
5. Attic stock delivered, with a signed receipt.
6. Your sub-tier suppliers' and subs' final lien waivers in hand.
7. Final pay application with all approved change orders and the retainage line.
8. Your conditional final waiver with the application; unconditional once paid.
9. Equipment, materials and trailers off site.
10. Job cost closed and reviewed: what went over, and what to change in the next estimate.

The last step is the one most often skipped, and it is the one that makes the next bid better.
