## What is a change order in construction?

A change order is a written agreement between owner and contractor that changes the work, the contract price, the
contract time, or all three. It is signed by both (and usually the architect), and once signed its amount is added to
the contract sum. AIA's standard form is G701; many owners use their own form with the same content: what changes,
the price, the days, and the signatures.

Most disputes on a job are about changes: extra work done on a verbal OK, priced after the fact, argued about at
closeout. The fix is procedural: no change is real until it is written, priced and approved, and every one is tracked
until it is billed.

## What's the difference between a change order and a construction change directive?

A change order is agreed by both sides; a construction change directive orders the work to go ahead before the price
or time is agreed. AIA describes its G714 directive as for use where the owner and contractor "have not agreed on
proposed changes in the contract sum or contract time", and under AIA's general conditions the owner can issue one
without the contractor's signature. You do the work, keep the cost records, and the price is settled later, by a
change order once it's agreed.

| | Change order (G701) | Change directive (G714) |
|---|---|---|
| Price and time | Agreed | Not yet agreed |
| Contractor signs | Yes | No |
| Work starts | After signing | On the directive |
| Billed | Once signed, as its own line | Once agreed and turned into a change order (or as the contract says) |

## Do I have to do extra work without a signed change order?

It depends on the contract and the state, but in Texas you may refuse once unsigned work gets too big. Since
1 September 2023, a Texas contractor or sub may decline owner-directed additional work without a written, fully
executed change order once that work, plus earlier unsigned owner-directed work, exceeds 10% of the original contract
amount, and isn't liable for damages for declining (Property Code 28.0091). On a $400,000 contract the line is
$40,000: with $36,000 of unsigned work already done, a new $6,000 directive takes it to $42,000, and the contractor may
decline it.

On federal jobs, a written or oral order from the contracting officer that changes the work counts as a change order
only if you give written notice saying you regard it as one.

## Will I get paid for work done on a verbal OK?

Often not without a fight. A verbal approval is hard to prove at closeout, and contracts usually say changes must be
in writing. Get the scope and price in writing before you start, even as an email, and keep dated photos, daily
reports and costs for any work done before the paperwork caught up.

## How do I price a change order?

Build it from quantities, labor hours at your rates, material quotes and equipment, then add overhead and profit as
the contract allows, with the backup attached:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Labor: 40 hours × $65 | $2,600 |
| Material (supplier quote attached) | $3,400 |
| Equipment rental | $500 |
| Subtotal | $6,500 |
| Overhead and profit, 15% | $975 |
| **Change order** | **$7,475**, 3 days added |

The figures are made up. Ask for the time as well as the money: a change that adds work but no days can leave you
paying liquidated damages for a delay the owner caused.

## How long do I have to claim a change?

As the contract says; on federal jobs under FAR 52.243-4, you must assert your right to an adjustment within 30 days
of the change order or your notice. Costs incurred more than 20 days before your written notice aren't adjusted
(except for defective specifications), and nothing can be claimed after final payment. Under that rule, notice given
on 1 March 2026 covers costs from 9 February 2026 onward. Private contracts, such as AIA's general conditions, have
their own deadlines: read the changes article before the job starts.

## What about hidden or different site conditions?

Give written notice promptly, before you disturb them. On federal jobs FAR 52.236-2 requires notice "before the
conditions are disturbed" of subsurface or latent conditions that differ materially from the contract, or unusual
unknown ones; if they do differ, the contract is adjusted in writing. No request is allowed after final payment.

## Can I bill a pending change order?

No: bill only approved changes, each as its own line on the schedule of values, so the
[pay application](/construction/pay-applications) shows the original contract sum, net change by change orders and
the revised sum. Pending changes are tracked, not billed, and they belong on the
[WIP schedule](/construction/wip-schedule), because their costs are already in the job.

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Original contract sum | $400,000 |
| CO 1, approved | $12,000 |
| CO 2, pending | $8,500 (tracked, not billed) |
| CO 3, rejected | $4,000 (kept for the record) |
| **Revised contract sum** | **$412,000** |

## Can signing a lien waiver give up a pending change order?

It can. A waiver that releases all claims through a date may release the extra work you haven't been paid for yet;
read what each [lien waiver](/construction/lien-waivers) covers, and list pending changes as an exception where the
form allows.

## What should a contractor's system keep for change orders?

- A change log per job: number, description, who asked, date of notice, status, price and days.
- The backup for each price, linked to the job's cost codes, so actual cost can be compared later.
- Approved changes added to the contract value and the schedule of values, once.
- Pending exposure: the total of unapproved changes per job, reviewed weekly.

In the [construction demo](/industries/construction-company)'s US build, a change order is raised as its own estimate
against the accepted contract estimate; once accepted, its lines join the schedule of values labeled CO 1, CO 2 and
are billed apart on the pay application. On the sample tower job, change order 1 adds $6,504 to a $245,856 schedule
of values. It doesn't yet keep a change log with notice dates and days, or change directives billed before a price is
agreed; those are parts we'd build.
