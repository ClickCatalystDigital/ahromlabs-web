## What a variation order is

Once a client accepts a quotation built on a BOQ, every later change is a variation: an extra item, a line dropped, a material swapped, a quantity that grew on site. A [variation order](/systems/variation-order) writes that change down with its price and its effect on time before the work is done, so the final bill is the BOQ plus a short list of approved changes, not an argument.

In interior work most variations are substitutions (veneer instead of laminate, a different tile), additions (a loft nobody drew) and quantities that move once walls are measured.

## The model: CPWD's clause 12

Private interior contracts aren't bound by government rules, but the Central Public Works Department's General Conditions of Contract give a tested structure for changes. Clause 12 says, in short:

- **Changes are made in writing.** The engineer in charge may alter, omit, add or substitute work by a signed written instruction, and the changed work becomes part of the contract.
- **A changed item keeps its contract price.** Altered or substituted work is done on the same conditions, including price, as the main work, unless the rules below apply.
- **A new item gets a market rate, claimed quickly.** For an item not in the contract, the contractor claims a market rate within 15 days, backed by a rate analysis and invoices; the engineer decides the rate within 45 days.
- **Quantities far above the BOQ can be re-rated.** Where a contract item exceeds the limit set in the contract's schedule, the rate for the excess quantity can be revised the same way.
- **Time moves with cost.** The completion time is extended in proportion to the added cost against the original contract value, plus 25% of that extra time.

## What to write on a variation order

1. **Which line it changes**: the BOQ line number and its description, or "new item".
2. **What changes**: added, omitted, substituted or quantity revised, with the new description written as fully as a BOQ line (board and grade, thickness, finish, hardware, unit and measurement basis).
3. **The rate and where it comes from**: the BOQ rate if the item is already in the BOQ; a new rate with its rate analysis if it isn't.
4. **The amount, plus or minus**, and GST on it.
5. **Effect on time**: the extra days, worked out the same way as clause 12 or as agreed.
6. **Effect on payment**: which payment stage it is billed with.
7. **Signatures and date**, before the work starts.

## Pricing a change from the BOQ

Most of the price is already in the BOQ. A wardrobe that grows from 60 to 72 sq ft by front elevation is 12 more sq ft at the agreed rate. A swap from laminate to veneer shutters is the difference between two rates, each built from material, labour and wastage. An omission is a credit at the BOQ rate.

This only works if the BOQ line said what was priced. "Wardrobe, per sq ft" gives nothing to price a change against; a line that names the board, thickness, finish, hardware and [measurement basis](/systems/measurement-basis) does. The [measurement rules](/boq/measurement-rules) and [material specifications](/boq/material-specifications) are where those lines are settled.

## Agree the rules before the first change

Put the variation rules in the quotation's terms: changes only in writing, the BOQ rate for listed items, how a new item is priced, and how much quantity can grow before its rate is reopened. Ahrom Labs' own default terms for a design studio's quotations include "work not listed here, or changed after acceptance, is quoted separately as a variation".

## Boundary

The clause 12 summary is from the CPWD General Conditions of Contract for construction works, April 2026 edition; it binds government works under that contract, not a private interior job. Use it as a model and put your own rules in your contract. The schedule limits it refers to vary by contract and are not given here.
