## What should an interior quotation include?

Everything the client needs to compare it and everything you need to bill it without an argument:

| Part | What it says |
|---|---|
| Header | Firm, GSTIN, client, site address, quotation number, date and revision |
| Scope summary | Rooms and trades covered, drawings or design version it is based on |
| BOQ | Each item by room: description with materials and brands, unit, quantity, rate, amount |
| Design fee | Percentage, per square foot or lump sum, and what it covers |
| Taxes | GST on each part at its rate, shown separately from the amounts |
| Payment schedule | Stages and the percentage due at each, see below |
| Exclusions | What is not included: civil changes, appliances, loose furniture, lights, society charges |
| Validity | How long the rates hold, often 15 to 30 days, because material prices move |
| Timeline | Start, key stages, handover, and what the client must do (approvals, site access) |
| Changes | How additions and substitutions are priced and approved, see [variation orders](/boq/variation-orders) |
| Warranty | Period and what it covers, by trade or brand |
| Signatures | Acceptance by the client, with the date |

## How should the BOQ part be laid out?

Room by room, trade by trade inside each room, with the same item described the same way every time: "Wardrobe
carcass in 18 mm BWP plywood with 1 mm laminate outside, 0.8 mm inside, soft-close hinges, brand X" rather than
"Wardrobe". Give the unit (sq ft of shutter area, running ft, number) and the measurement rule. See
[how to make an interior BOQ](/boq/how-to-make-an-interior-boq), the [measurement rules](/boq/measurement-rules) and
the free [BOQ template](/boq/template).

## How is the design fee shown?

As its own section, separate from execution: a percentage of the project cost (and of which cost), a rate per square
foot of carpet area, or a lump sum by stage (concept, drawings, site supervision). Say how many design revisions are
included and what a further revision costs.

## What payment schedule is usual?

Stage-wise, with each stage something the client can see. A common pattern for a turnkey project:

| Stage | Share |
|---|---|
| Booking, on signing | 10% |
| Design approved, before material is ordered | 40% |
| Carcass and civil work complete | 30% |
| Finishing complete, before handover | 15% |
| After handover and snag list closed | 5% |

Large contracts borrow the public works model: CPWD's contract allows mobilization advance with conditions,
"Mobilization advances not exceeding 10% of the tendered amount may be given, if requested by the contractor in
writing", and holds a security deposit that "shall be collected from the running bills and the final bill of the
contractor". On a residential project the last 5% does the same job: it is released after the snag list is closed.

## How should GST be shown?

Separately, on each part at its rate, with the totals before and after tax. Design services and the execution of
interior work may attract GST differently depending on how the contract is structured (a works contract for the
whole job, or separate supplies), so confirm the treatment with your CA before you fix the format.

## What causes disputes after a quotation is accepted?

- Lump sums per room with no item list, so nobody can say what was included.
- Brands and thicknesses not written, so a cheaper board is a "substitution" only after the fact.
- No measurement rule, so the final bill's areas don't match the quote's.
- Changes agreed on the phone with no price, settled at the last payment.
- Validity not stated, so a quote accepted four months later is expected at the old rates.
