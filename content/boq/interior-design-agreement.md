## What should an interior design agreement cover?

Two jobs are often in one agreement: design (drawings, selections, supervision) and execution (building and
installing the interiors). Whether you do both or only design, the agreement should say plainly which, because the
risks are different. A design-only firm is paid for drawings and advice; a turnkey firm also carries the cost of
materials, labour and mistakes on site.

| Clause | What it should say |
|---|---|
| Parties and site | The client, the firm, the GSTINs, the site address and the area covered |
| Scope | Rooms and trades included; drawings and design version the scope is based on |
| Exclusions | What is not included: civil and structural changes, appliances, loose furniture, lights, society and permit charges |
| BOQ and rates | The BOQ as an annexure, with items, specifications, units, quantities and rates |
| Design fee | Basis (percentage, per square foot or lump sum), what it covers, number of revisions included |
| Payment schedule | Stages, share at each, and that payment is due within a set number of days of the invoice |
| Changes | How additions, omissions and substitutions are priced, approved in writing and billed |
| Timeline | Start date, key stages, handover date, and what the client must do and by when |
| Client responsibilities | Site access, power and water, society permissions, timely approvals and payments |
| Materials and brands | Specified brands, and the rule for equivalents if a brand isn't available |
| Measurement | How quantities are measured for billing; see [measurement rules](/boq/measurement-rules) |
| Warranty and snags | Warranty by trade and brand, the snag list process and its timeline |
| Retention | A share of each bill or the last payment held until snags are closed |
| Delays | What happens when either side delays, and any extension of time |
| Termination | How either side can end it, and payment for work done to that date |
| Disputes | Discussion first, then mediation or arbitration, the seat and governing law |

## How should the scope be fixed?

By reference to specific documents: the approved layout and drawing list with revision numbers, the BOQ, and the
material and brand schedule. "Complete interiors of 3BHK" invites argument; "Interiors as per drawings ID-01 to
ID-24 revision B and BOQ annexure A" doesn't. When drawings change, the agreement's change clause applies, not a fresh
negotiation of the whole job.

## How should changes be handled?

In writing, priced before the work, using the BOQ's rates for items that exist and an agreed rate for new items, with
the effect on time stated. A change agreed on the phone and settled at the last payment is the most common
interior dispute. See [variation orders](/boq/variation-orders); the CPWD model, which prices changed items at BOQ
rates and new items at market rates, is a sensible pattern for private work too.

## What retention and warranty terms are usual?

Public works hold a security deposit from every bill; CPWD's contract says "The security deposit shall be collected
from the running bills and the final bill of the contractor". Private residential work usually holds the last 5% or
so until the snag list is closed. Warranty is set by trade: one year on workmanship is common, with brand warranties
on hardware, appliances and finishes passed to the client with their cards. State what the warranty doesn't cover
(misuse, water leakage from outside the scope, wear).

## What about delays?

Both sides cause them. The firm's delays are usually materials and labour; the client's are approvals, payments and
site access. Set out what each side's delay does: an extension of time for client delays, and for firm delays either
nothing beyond the obligation to finish or an agreed, modest deduction. Keep a record of each delay as it happens,
with the date and cause; at the end, nobody remembers who held up the kitchen.

## Should the agreement be stamped?

Agreements in India attract stamp duty under the state's stamp law, at rates and on terms that vary by state and
document type. An unstamped or under-stamped agreement may not be accepted as evidence until the duty and penalty are
paid. Check the duty for your state and document with your lawyer or the state's e-stamping service.
