## What is BIS CRS?

The Compulsory Registration Scheme run by the Bureau of Indian Standards for electronics and IT goods. The Ministry of
Electronics and Information Technology notifies the products by order; for each, the manufacturer has the product
tested at a BIS-recognised laboratory against its Indian Standard and registers it with BIS. A registered product
carries the Standard Mark with the registration number (an R-number) on the product or its packaging.

## Which products need it?

The products listed in the order and its amendments, each against an Indian Standard. The list has grown over the
years and includes items such as power adapters for IT equipment, LED drivers and lamps, power banks, mobile phones,
laptops, set-top boxes, and certain cells and batteries. Check the current list on the BIS CRS site for the exact
product description before importing; the order, not the product's common name, decides.

Bare electronic components (resistors, capacitors, ICs, connectors) are generally not on the list. Modules and
finished assemblies may be, if their description matches a listed product.

## Who registers, and what does the registration cover?

The manufacturer, for each factory and each brand and model series, through an Authorised Indian Representative if the
manufacturer is abroad. An importer or trader doesn't register in its own name for someone else's product; it must
make sure the product it brings in or sells is registered and marked. A registration is valid for a fixed period and
is renewed; check its status on the BIS site.

## What does a trader need to check?

- Before ordering a listed product: the supplier's BIS registration number, the manufacturer and factory it covers,
  the models included, and that it is valid.
- On arrival: the Standard Mark and R-number on the product or packaging match the registration.
- On the item record: the registration number and expiry, so stock isn't sold after it lapses and customs queries are
  answered from the record.

Customs can hold a consignment of a listed product without registration, which turns into storage charges and missed
orders.

## How long does registration take?

Testing at a recognised laboratory and BIS's processing both take time, typically weeks to a few months depending
on the product and the laboratory's queue. A trader planning to import a new listed product should start with the
manufacturer well before the first order and confirm the registration is granted, not applied for, before shipment.

## What does the marking look like?

The Standard Mark with the registration number, printed on the product, or on the packaging where the product is too
small, as the scheme's marking rules allow. Check that the R-number printed matches the registration and the model
number on the product; a mismatch is the first thing an inspector or a customs officer notices.

## What if a supplier's registration lapses?

Stock already imported and sold before the lapse is a different case from new imports and sales after it; ask the
supplier for the renewal and stop purchase orders for that model until it is in place. An inventory system that holds
the validity date on the item can warn buyers before an order is raised for a product whose registration is about to
expire.

## How should an inventory system support it?

By keeping compliance data on the item and the supplier: BIS registration number, validity and the models covered for
listed products, alongside the [HSN code](/erp/hsn-codes-electronic-components) and GST rate, with a warning when a
purchase order is raised for a listed item without a valid registration. See
[landed cost of imports](/erp/import-landed-cost) and
[software for electronics importers and traders](/industries/electronics-component-trading).
