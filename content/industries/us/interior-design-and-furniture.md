## Can I try it before talking to anyone?

Yes. A live demo runs at [interiors-erp-us.ahromlabs.com](https://interiors-erp-us.ahromlabs.com/demo) with two made-up sister companies in Austin, Texas: Acme Interiors, a design studio, and Acme Furniture, its furniture shop. It has a full house, a 40-seat office fit-out, a coffee bar and a kitchen remodel in progress, shop orders with weeks of work history, vendor orders with deliveries, freight checked against a carrier's rate card, invoices with Texas sales tax and a shared cash book. Leave a name and email, pick a login (owner, manager or office staff) and use the real screens. The data resets every night, so nothing you do reaches anyone.

## Why do design firms outgrow spreadsheets and texts?

A design-build job touches clients, site visits, vendors, crew, freight and payments at once, usually spread across a spreadsheet per project and a group text per site. The owners know how it all connects; nobody else does. The system puts all of it in one place: clients, projects and site visits on the design side, shop orders on the furniture side, and vendors, freight and money across both.

## Can a design studio and its furniture shop share one system?

Yes, without the weight of a multi-company ERP. Both companies share one client list and one combined book; a design job lives under Projects and a furniture order under Orders, and each invoice prints with its own company's letterhead and logo. It is one app modeling two business lines under the same owners, which is exactly as much structure as that business needs. See [Two companies, one book](/notes/two-companies-one-book).

## Does it work with QuickBooks Online?

Yes. QuickBooks Online stays the books. The studio connects its QuickBooks company once, in Settings → Integrations, and maps its accounts, with suggested matches to review. After that, an invoice goes to QuickBooks with its client when it leaves draft, each company's lines on its own income item ("Design studio sales" or "Furniture workshop sales"); a client payment goes when it is recorded, for the accountant to apply to an invoice in QuickBooks; and a vendor order goes as a bill once it is complete. Sending happens in the background, so saving never waits on QuickBooks, and a failure shows on the invoice with its reason and a Retry button. In the demo it is connected to an Intuit sandbox company. See [connecting QuickBooks Online to a shop system](/manufacturing/quickbooks-integration), and [QuickBooks for interior designers](/interiors/quickbooks-for-interior-designers) for which plan a design firm needs and what it can't track.

## How does sales tax work for design fees and furniture?

Each invoice line carries its own sales tax rate, and a line without one is not taxed. In the demo, Texas sales tax (8.25%) is charged on materials and furniture, while design fees and labor are not taxed. QuickBooks' automated sales tax works out its own figure from the address, and its total is shown beside ours. Whether a design fee is taxable depends on the state: [sales tax for interior designers](/sales-tax/interior-designers-sales-tax) compares New York, California and Texas, and [how an ERP should handle US sales tax](/sales-tax/sales-tax-in-erp) covers nexus, resale certificates and what to check in any system.

## Can a quote start from an item library?

Yes. Each studio keeps its own item library, and a quote is built room by room from it, with quantities worked out from site dimensions. Payment stages printed on the quote become draft invoices once the client accepts. Lines carry the sell price; a net cost and markup per line is what we'd add for a firm that resells product. [How to charge for interior design](/interiors/how-to-charge-for-interior-design) and [invoices, deposits and retainers](/interiors/invoices-deposits-and-retainers) work the numbers through.

## How is a design project tracked from brief to handover?

Each project has milestones with due dates, a log of site visits (who went, when and what they found), a material palette, and the shop orders and vendor orders that belong to it, on one page. Every conversation with a client is logged against the client and, when it is about a specific job, pinned to that project, so the history of a decision isn't buried in someone's texts. The home screen is a month calendar of what's due across both companies: tasks, milestones, site visits and delivery dates, with today's and overdue tasks beside it.

## How does the shop know who made what, and how is the crew paid?

A shop order can come from a design project or straight from a walk-in client, and moves from pending to in progress, done and delivered. The daily worksheet records each person's attendance and what they worked on: which order, which part, from when to when. Each order's work history then shows who worked on which part, and when. Pay is worked out from the same records: a salaried person's monthly pay, or a day-rate person's days worked times their rate, minus that month's advances, recorded with the gross, the deductions and the net. [Job costing for custom furniture and millwork shops](/interiors/custom-furniture-job-costing) works an order's materials and labor through.

## How are freight overcharges caught?

Each carrier has a rate card per route, from one location to another, with the amount expected. A delivery billed above its route's rate is flagged the moment it is entered, and each carrier shows its count of overcharges. It catches overcharges as long as the rates are kept current. [Purchase orders and procurement](/interiors/purchase-orders-and-procurement) covers the rest of the vendor side, from deposit to receiving, and [freight damage claims](/interiors/freight-damage-claims) what to do when a piece arrives broken.

## How do you stop staff seeing the whole cash book?

By filtering at the point the data is fetched, not by hiding buttons. Staff log day-to-day cash, but their view of the cash book contains only the entries they created. Bank data isn't shown to staff at all: every request for it from a staff account is refused before any query runs. Owners see everything.

## What should a design studio ask before choosing software?

1. **Does it hold the design side and the shop side together** (projects, site visits, shop orders and vendors) or only one of them?
2. **Does it work beside QuickBooks Online**, sending invoices and bills without double entry?
3. **Can it tax furniture and materials but not design fees** on the same invoice?
4. **If you run two companies, does each invoice carry the right company's name** from one system?
5. **Who can see the money?** Staff logging petty cash shouldn't see the bank account, enforced by the server, not by hiding a menu.

The [interior design firm guides](/interiors) cover each of these in depth, from trade accounts to the client agreement.

## Boundary

The system was first built for an interior design firm and its sister furniture workshop in India, [Savistar and Saag](/work#savistar-saag), who run on it every day; this North American version is the same code with US wording, dollars, sales tax and QuickBooks Online, shown here on sample companies. It has not yet been used by a US or Canadian studio. QuickBooks sending covers invoices, client payments and completed vendor orders; vendor payments are not sent yet, because the cash book names the vendor but not the bill. It is one shared app for two related businesses, not a multi-tenant platform, and there is no 3D design, mood board or client portal.
