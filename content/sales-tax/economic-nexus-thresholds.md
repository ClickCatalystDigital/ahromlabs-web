## Which sales count toward the threshold?

States measure different things, and the difference is large for a manufacturer or distributor, because so much of
what you sell goes to resellers or to buyers with an exemption certificate. The Streamlined Sales Tax Governing
Board explains the three bases with one example: a company sells $400,000 into a state in a year, made up of
$220,000 to wholesalers buying for resale, $75,000 to buyers claiming another exemption (such as use in
manufacturing), $10,000 of exempt products and $95,000 of taxable sales.

| The state counts | What's included | The example's figure |
|---|---|---|
| Gross sales | Everything, including resale and exempt sales | $400,000 |
| Retail sales | Everything except sales for resale | $180,000 |
| Taxable sales | Only sales that are (or should be) taxed | $95,000 |

The same company is over a $100,000 threshold in a state that counts gross or retail sales, and under it in a state
that counts taxable sales. Each state's rule in the table below names the basis it uses.

## Which period does each state use?

Most states look at the previous or the current calendar year: you are over as soon as either one passes. Others
use the last 12 months (Pennsylvania, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Mississippi) or the previous four sales tax
quarters (New York). Check monthly against a rolling 12 months and you catch all of them.

## Are transaction thresholds going away?

Yes. Many states first copied South Dakota's "$100,000 or 200 transactions" and have since dropped the transaction
count: North Dakota in 2019, Wisconsin in 2021, South Dakota in 2023, Indiana, North Carolina and Wyoming in 2024,
Alaska and Utah in 2025 and Illinois from January 1, 2026, all as listed in the Board's chart. Kentucky followed on
**August 1, 2026**: its 2026 revenue act, [HB 757](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/acts/26RS/documents/0161.pdf),
rewrote KRS 139.340 so a remote retailer must register once its sales into Kentucky in the previous or current
calendar year exceed $100,000, now counting services as well as goods, and must register and collect by the first day
of the month at most 60 days after it passes. The Board's chart, which calls itself a summary "as of January 11,
2023", still shows Kentucky's 200-transaction test, so the checker below quotes the act for Kentucky.

Where a count is left, a business with many small orders, such as spare parts, can pass it long before the dollar
amount. A made-up spare-parts seller with 260 orders into Kentucky averaging $300 ($78,000) had nexus there under the
200-order test until July 31, 2026, and doesn't from August 1.

## Which states need both a dollar amount and a number of sales?

Connecticut and New York, where both tests must be met. Connecticut counts $100,000 of gross receipts and 200 retail
transactions, per the Board's chart. New York's
[Tax Department](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/publications/sales/nexus.htm) counts the four preceding sales
tax quarters, in which the seller's gross receipts from goods delivered into the state exceeded $500,000 and it made
more than 100 sales. So a made-up distributor with $520,000 of sales into New York across 90 orders is under, and its
101st order in the window puts it over.

## How do you check your own sales against each state?

Enter each state you ship to with your sales and number of orders into it over the period that state uses. The
checker says whether you are over, within 20% of the threshold, or under, and quotes the state's rule beside the
answer. Physical presence in a state means you register there regardless; see
[what sales tax nexus is](/sales-tax/sales-tax-nexus). If you are over in several of the 24 member states, one
registration covers them: see [Streamlined Sales Tax](/sales-tax/streamlined-sales-tax).

The figures come from your own system: sales by ship-to state, split into resale, exempt and taxable, for any
period. If you can't get that report today, see [how an ERP should handle sales tax](/sales-tax/sales-tax-in-erp).
