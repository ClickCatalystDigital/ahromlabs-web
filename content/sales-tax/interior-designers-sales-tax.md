## Do interior designers charge sales tax?

On the product, yes: furniture, lighting, fabric, rugs and accessories you sell to a client are taxable goods in every
state with a sales tax, and the tax is on your selling price, markup included. On your design fee, it depends on the
state, and in two of the three states below it also depends on how the fee is written on the invoice. That is why a
design firm's invoice should put design fees, product, freight and installation on separate lines.

| | New York | California | Texas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furniture you resell | Taxable | Taxable, on the retail price with markup | Taxable |
| Design fee on its own | Taxable (state rate only in New York City) | Not taxable if not related to a sale of merchandise | Not among the 16 taxable service categories |
| Design fee tied to the product | Taxable at the full rate if not separately stated | Taxable (for example, showroom time for items you sell) | Not addressed in the pages read; ask the Comptroller |
| Drawings and plans | Taxable as design services | Taxable when you transfer finished drawings | Taxable when made to a customer's specifications |
| Installation labor | Depends on the work (see below) | Not taxable, if itemized | Depends on the property and the work |

## How does New York tax interior design?

As a taxable service in its own right. Tax Bulletin ST-400 says anyone who sells interior decorating or design services
must register if they deliver the services in New York State or have a place of business there, and lists what counts,
from layout drawings and furniture arranging to the selection, purchase and arrangement of furniture, draperies and
other decorations. A licensed architect or engineer doing interior design that isn't architecture or engineering is
taxed the same way. The rate is set by the point of delivery, so plans delivered to Albany are taxed at Albany's rate.

Two rules matter most for a firm that sells product:

- **New York City.** Design services are exempt from the city's local sales tax, so a separately stated, reasonable
  design fee is taxed at the state and MCTD rate of 4⅜%, while the goods on the same bill are taxed at 8⅞%. If the
  design service is not contracted for separately from the goods, the entire charge is taxed at 8⅞%.
- **Design plus implementation.** When plans are followed by the physical work, the bulletin treats the firm as a
  construction contractor: the job is then taxed as a service to real or personal property, or not at all if it is a
  capital improvement, and the firm pays tax on its materials. This is the rule a design-build firm needs to read in
  full.

## What does that look like on a New York City invoice?

A Manhattan apartment: a $12,000 design fee and $54,000 of furniture, delivered in the city.

| | Design fee stated separately | Design fee lumped into the furniture |
|---|---|---|
| Design fee | $12,000 × 4.375% = $525.00 | (included below) |
| Furniture | $54,000 × 8.875% = $4,792.50 | $66,000 × 8.875% = $5,857.50 |
| **Sales tax on the invoice** | **$5,317.50** | **$5,857.50** |

Writing the fee as its own contracted, reasonable charge saves the client $540 on this job, and it is the client's
money the firm is collecting, so getting it wrong in either direction is a problem.

## How does California tax design fees?

By what the fee is for. CDTFA's Publication 35 says a designer who sells merchandise, including samples or finished
drawings, generally needs a seller's permit, and owes tax on the retail selling price including markup and any related
taxable labor and service charges. Professional fees not directly related to a sale of merchandise are not taxable,
and CDTFA tells designers to list them separately on the invoice. Fees that are part of a sale are taxable: its example
is accompanying a client to a showroom to choose furniture the client is buying from you.

Labor is split too: tax generally applies to fabrication labor (making draperies, cutting and sewing upholstery) but
not to repair or installation labor, which should be itemized. Billing product at cost with a separate fee for
overhead and profit makes the whole fee taxable unless you can show part of it is for nontaxable professional
services; [how to charge for interior design](/interiors/how-to-charge-for-interior-design) works the markup through.

## How does Texas tax interior design?

Texas taxes goods and a closed list of services. The Comptroller's Publication 96-259 defines taxable services, under
Tax Code section 151.0101, as 16 broad categories; interior design is not one of them, so a consultation or design fee
billed on its own is not on that list. Two things are: furniture and other goods you sell, at the 6.25% state rate plus
up to 2% local, a maximum of 8.25%; and drawings, because Publication 94-143 says tax is due when a draftsman or designer
uses a customer's specifications to create drawings. Nonresidential repair and remodeling is also a taxable service
category, which matters to a firm that manages fit-outs. Texas rulings are fact-specific, so a firm whose fee includes
drawings should ask the Comptroller how its invoices are read.

## Can you buy furniture for clients without paying sales tax?

Yes, with a resale certificate, because you are buying it to resell. You give each vendor a certificate and charge the
client the tax instead. CDTFA spells out two limits worth following in any state: you may not give your permit number to
clients so they can shop tax-free at furniture marts, and if you use something you bought for resale (a sample you
keep, a lamp in your studio, a gift to a client) you owe use tax on it. [Resale certificates](/sales-tax/resale-certificates)
covers the forms and [use tax](/sales-tax/use-tax) what to report. Vendors' trade programs ask for a certificate for
each state you ship to: see [trade accounts and designer discounts](/interiors/trade-accounts-and-designer-discounts).

## Is freight on furniture taxable?

Often, and it depends on the route. In California, a delivery charge on a taxable sale is generally not taxable when a
carrier delivers directly to the client and you charge no more than the carrier charged you, but delivery from the
factory to your place of business or another intermediate point, such as a receiving warehouse, is generally taxable.
Bill freight, handling and storage as their own lines so each can be taxed by the state's rule;
[purchase orders and procurement](/interiors/purchase-orders-and-procurement) covers the warehouse leg.

## Do you have to collect in other states?

Only where you have nexus there. A firm that ships furniture into another state can reach that state's economic nexus
threshold through product sales alone; [economic nexus thresholds](/sales-tax/economic-nexus-thresholds) has every
state's figure and a checker. In New York, a design fee is taxed where it is delivered, so a plan delivered into New York
is taxable there even when the project is elsewhere.

## What should a design firm's system do for sales tax?

When comparing systems, check that it can:

- Hold a **tax rate per invoice line**, so design fees, goods, freight and installation are taxed separately.
- Work out the rate from the **delivery address** of the goods and, for New York design services, where the service is
  delivered.
- Keep design fees **separately stated and contracted**, so New York City's lower rate and California's exemption apply.
- Store each vendor's **resale certificates** and each client's exemption certificate, with states and expiry.
- Flag **use tax** on items bought for resale but kept or used.
- Report **sales by state** for the nexus check, and send invoices with tax codes to QuickBooks Online.
