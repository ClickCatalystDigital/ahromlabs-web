## What does nexus mean for sales tax?

Nexus is the connection between your business and a state that lets the state make you collect its tax. Once you
have it, you register with that state, charge its sales tax on taxable sales delivered there, file its returns and
keep the records it asks for. Without it, the buyer owes the state [use tax](/sales-tax/use-tax) instead, and
usually doesn't pay it.

There are two ways to have nexus:

| Kind | What creates it | Since |
|---|---|---|
| Physical nexus | An office, plant, warehouse, inventory, employees or representatives in the state | Always |
| Economic nexus | Sales into the state above its threshold, with no presence at all | South Dakota v. Wayfair, June 21, 2018 |

## What counts as a physical presence?

Each state lists its own. Texas, for example, counts a location you operate, an employee or representative who
sells, delivers or takes orders, services performed in the state, and delivering in your own or leased vehicles.
California's examples include keeping inventory or an office there, having representatives who take orders or
install, and leasing equipment such as a server in the state.

Two cases catch manufacturers and distributors out:

- **Stock in someone else's warehouse.** Inventory you keep in a state is usually a presence there, even in a
  rented or third-party warehouse: California's examples start with maintaining inventory in the state.
- **Installation and service crews.** A team that installs equipment or does warranty work in a state is presence,
  even if the sale was made from home.

A physical presence means registering whatever the amount of sales. The thresholds only apply to remote sellers.

## What changed with South Dakota v. Wayfair?

Before 2018, a state could only make a seller collect if it had a physical presence there. In South Dakota v.
Wayfair the Supreme Court ruled that a state can require collection on sales delivered into it regardless of
physical presence. Every state with a sales tax now has an economic nexus rule: most at $100,000 of sales a year,
some with a transaction count as well. See [every state's threshold, with a checker](/sales-tax/economic-nexus-thresholds).

## When do you have to start collecting?

Each state sets its own start. Texas gives an example: a remote seller whose Texas revenue passes $500,000 over
twelve months must get a permit and start collecting no later than the first day of the fourth month after the
month it passed. Other states expect registration from the day you pass, or from the start of the next month or
quarter. Look up the state's own page before the threshold is near, not after.

If you are already registered and collecting in a state, keep doing so: the Streamlined Sales Tax Governing Board's
guidance is to continue, and dropping a registration has its own procedure in each state.

## Do marketplace sales count?

Where a marketplace collects the tax for you, most states let you leave those sales out of your own filing, and
Texas does not require a permit for a seller that sells only through a marketplace that collects for it. Whether
marketplace sales count toward your own threshold differs by state; the state's rule says. Keep the marketplace's
reports either way: Texas asks sellers to keep records of all marketplace sales for at least four years.

## What to do once you know where you have nexus

1. List the states where you have a physical presence, including stock held by others and crews working there.
2. Run your last 12 months of sales by ship-to state through the [nexus checker](/sales-tax/economic-nexus-thresholds).
3. Register in each state you're over, directly or, in the 24 member states, once through the
   [Streamlined Sales Tax registration system](/sales-tax/streamlined-sales-tax).
4. Collect [resale and exemption certificates](/sales-tax/resale-certificates) from customers who buy tax-free,
   before you invoice them in a new state.
5. Make your order system report sales by state every month, so the next threshold doesn't arrive as a surprise:
   see [how an ERP should handle sales tax](/sales-tax/sales-tax-in-erp).
