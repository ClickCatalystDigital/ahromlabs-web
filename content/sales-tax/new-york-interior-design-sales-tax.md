## Is interior design taxable in New York?

Yes: New York taxes interior decorating and design services in their own right, not only the furniture sold with
them. Tax Bulletin ST-400 lists what counts (layout drawings, schedules and specifications,
furniture arranging, design of furniture, fixtures and cabinetry, staging, lighting and sound design, and the selection,
purchase and arrangement of furniture, draperies and other decorations) and who provides it, from interior consultants
and kitchen designers to home decorators and designers, renderers and event decorators. Anyone selling these services
must register if they deliver them in New York State or have a place of business there.

## What rate applies, inside and outside New York City?

Outside New York City, the 4% state rate plus the local rate where the service is delivered, plus the ⅜% MCTD rate in
the Metropolitan Commuter Transportation District. Inside the city, design services are exempt from the city's own
local tax, so they are taxed at 4% state plus ⅜% MCTD, 4⅜% in all. Goods sold in the city are taxed at the full 8⅞%.

| Where delivered | Design service | Furniture sold with it |
|---|---|---|
| New York City | 4⅜% (state and MCTD only), if contracted and stated separately | 8⅞% |
| Albany (the bulletin's example) | 8% | the local combined rate |
| Essex County (the bulletin's staging example) | 7¾% | the local combined rate |

The city's lower rate holds only when the design service is contracted for separately from the goods and its charge
is reasonable and separately stated on the invoice; if not, the bulletin says the entire charge is taxed at 8⅞%.
[Sales tax for interior designers](/sales-tax/interior-designers-sales-tax) works a $66,000 Manhattan invoice both ways:
$5,317.50 against $5,857.50.

## Where is a design service "delivered"?

It depends on what the client receives, and the bulletin sets out each case:

| What the firm provides | Taxed where |
|---|---|
| Renderings or plans only | Where the plans are delivered to the client, whatever the property's location |
| On-site advice only | Where the property is |
| Plans and on-site advice, one contract, one charge | Where the property is (an integrated, two-step transaction) |
| Plans and on-site advice, separate reasonable charges | Each part where it is delivered |
| Plans followed by the firm's own implementation | Not a design service: taxed as construction work (below) |

This is why a New York firm working on a project out of state can still owe New York tax: plans delivered to a client's
New York office are taxable there. In the bulletin's example, a Lake Placid law firm cancels a Vermont office after
receiving plans for a single fee covering plans and on-site advice; once the on-site work won't happen, Designer M must
collect New York tax on the $7,000 kept for the plans.

## What happens when the firm also does the work?

When plans are followed by the firm's physical implementation, the bulletin treats the firm as a construction
contractor, not a seller of design services. The charge is then taxed as a service to personal or real property: repair,
maintenance or installation work on property in New York is taxable, while installation that results in a capital
improvement to real property is not, and the firm pays tax on the materials it buys and installs. For a design-build
firm, how each job is classified (capital improvement or repair) matters more than the design fee; the bulletin's
restaurant example, a full gut renovation managed by the design firm, is a capital improvement and not taxable.

## Are millwork shop drawings taxable?

Yes, when they are for an interior. In advisory opinion TSB-A-08(30)S, the Department found that preparing millwork
shop drawings (custom staircases, mouldings, built-in cabinetry) and providing a manager to supervise the installation
in New York are interior decorating and design services, taxable under section 1105(c)(7), when the company isn't
practising architecture and isn't itself doing the construction. In New York City, those services escape only the
city's local tax. [Job costing for custom furniture and millwork shops](/interiors/custom-furniture-job-costing)
covers the shop side.

## Do licensed architects charge tax on interior design?

On design work that isn't architecture, yes. The bulletin says interior design doesn't include the practice of
architecture or engineering, but when a licensed architect or engineer performs interior decorating or design services
that don't constitute architecture or engineering, those services are taxable. An architect buying interior design
from a designer to use in its own plans can't buy it for resale.

## What should a New York design firm's invoices show?

At least: the design fee as its own contracted, reasonable line; goods, installation and freight as separate lines; and the place each service and each delivery happens, which sets the rate. Keep the agreement showing the design service was contracted separately; it is what supports the 4⅜%
rate in the city. [Invoices, deposits and retainers](/interiors/invoices-deposits-and-retainers) has the rest of the
invoice, and [resale certificates](/sales-tax/resale-certificates) covers buying the furniture tax-free for resale.
