## Which states are in Streamlined Sales Tax?

Twenty-four: 23 full members and Tennessee as an associate member, as the Governing Board lists them on 5 November
2026:

| Status | States |
|---|---|
| Full members (23) | Arkansas, Georgia, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Minnesota, Nebraska, Nevada, New Jersey, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Utah, Vermont, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming |
| Associate member (1) | Tennessee |
| Not members | Every other state, including California, Texas, New York, Florida, Illinois and Pennsylvania |

A full member's laws already follow the agreement; an associate has substantially complied. The states with the
most manufacturing and distribution sales, California, Texas and New York, are not members, so a growing seller
deals with them directly whatever it does in Streamlined states.

## What do member states have in common?

The agreement standardizes how sales tax works, not the rates. Member states share definitions of products, a
single state-level administration, simpler rate structures and other uniform rules. When the Supreme
Court decided South Dakota v. Wayfair, it pointed to South Dakota's membership as one reason its law did not burden
interstate sellers unduly.

For a seller, three things follow:

- **One registration.** The Streamlined Sales Tax Registration System registers you in any or all member states
  with one application, free. You still file a return with each state you register in.
- **One exemption certificate.** The Streamlined exemption certificate is accepted by all 24 member states, and you
  don't need to be registered through Streamlined to use it. See
  [resale and exemption certificates](/sales-tax/resale-certificates).
- **Published taxability and rates.** Each member state publishes a taxability matrix and rate and boundary files
  in a common format, which is what a tax engine loads.

## Can a remote seller get sales tax filing for free?

Yes, in member states, for remote sellers. The Board contracts with certified service providers (CSPs); on
5 November 2026 it lists Avalara, TaxCloud, Sovos, AccurateTax and Avior as offering the free service. A seller
that is a "CSP-compensated seller" in a member state gets, at the state's cost:

- setup and integration of the provider's system with yours
- tax calculated on each sale
- returns prepared and filed, and the tax remitted
- help with that state's notices and audits

To qualify in a state, you register through the Streamlined system, contract with a CSP under the Streamlined
contract, and in the 12 months before registering had no fixed place of business there for more than 30 days, less
than $50,000 of property and of payroll there, under 25% of your total property or payroll there, and weren't
collecting there to qualify as a state supplier. A remote seller that only has to collect because it passed the
economic threshold qualifies. The provider may charge for states where you don't qualify, for non-member states and
for extra services.

## Can a certified provider charge a distributor that sells mostly for resale?

Yes, in part. The Board's [free services page](https://www.streamlinedsalestax.org/for-businesses/free-sales-tax-services)
lets a provider charge to process non-taxable transactions when they exceed 30% of all the transactions it processes
for that seller in a member state in a year. A worked example: a made-up remote seller is over the threshold in Ohio,
Indiana, Michigan, Kentucky, Georgia and Washington, with no presence in any of them. One free registration covers all
six, and a provider files them at the states' cost. But if 70% of its Ohio transactions are sales for resale, the
provider may charge for processing those in Ohio. Ask each provider for its price on non-taxable volume before you
sign.

## Is Intuit a certified service provider?

Not for the free service. The Board lists Exactor, an Intuit subsidiary, among the providers whose systems are
certified but that don't currently offer free services under the Streamlined contract. QuickBooks Online's
automated sales tax works out the tax inside QuickBooks; it isn't one of the providers offering the free service.

## Is it worth registering through Streamlined?

For a manufacturer or distributor that has passed the threshold in several member states and has no presence
there, usually yes: one registration, and a provider files those states for free. It doesn't help with California,
Texas or New York. Check where you are over with the [nexus checker](/sales-tax/economic-nexus-thresholds), and
see [how an ERP should handle sales tax](/sales-tax/sales-tax-in-erp) for connecting a provider to your order
system.
