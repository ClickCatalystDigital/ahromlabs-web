## What does a design firm need from its software?

If the firm only bills design time, a time tracker and QuickBooks may be enough. Once it sells furniture, fabric and
lighting, it needs one record per item from selection to installation: the
[specification](/interiors/ffe-specifications), the client proposal and approval, the deposit, the
[purchase order](/interiors/purchase-orders-and-procurement) and vendor deposit, acknowledgement, freight, receiving
and the client invoice, with markup and sales tax right on each line. Then time billing against fee agreements,
retainers, and reports on margin by project.

## What are the main choices?

Two families, judged by where the books live:

| Approach | Examples, in their own words | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Design software with its own accounting | Design Manager calls itself "a flexible project management and accounting package"; Studio Designer argues "5 Reasons Designers Need a Purpose-Built Solution" for accounting | Projects, purchasing and the general ledger in one system; your accountant works in it |
| Design software that syncs to QuickBooks Online | Houzz Pro documents how to "Sync Houzz Pro With QuickBooks Online"; Programa offers a "Web Clipper", proposals and purchase orders | Projects and purchasing in the design tool, books in QuickBooks; check what syncs and in which direction |

Features change; check each vendor's current pages and pricing before choosing.

## How should you compare them?

Test with one real past project, not a demo script:

1. Clip or enter five items, put them on a proposal with your markup, and send it.
2. Take a client deposit, issue two purchase orders with vendor deposits, record a partial back-order.
3. Receive the items, add freight, invoice the client, and check sales tax on each line.
4. Bill a month of design time against a retainer.
5. Look at the project's margin and the client's balance, then the same numbers in your books.

Wherever you retype something, or the two sets of numbers disagree, that is daily work for your team.

## Which features matter most for a product-selling firm?

Ranked by how often they cause trouble when missing:

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| One item record from spec to invoice | Retyping an item at each step is where prices and finishes go wrong |
| Markup and sell price per item, with the rule visible | Clients see a price; the firm needs the cost and margin behind it |
| Client deposits and vendor deposits per item | Deposits are the cash flow of a product business; they must match orders |
| Purchase orders with acknowledgements and back-orders | The vendor's acknowledgement is when errors are caught cheaply |
| Receiving and freight, item by item | Freight claims have deadlines; see [freight damage claims](/interiors/freight-damage-claims) |
| Sales tax by line | Product and design fees are often taxed differently |
| Time tracking against fee agreements | Fixed fees need hours to show whether they paid |
| Client portal or approval links | Approvals by email get lost; an approval should be a date on the item |
| Reports: project margin, open orders, deposits held | The owner's weekly view |

## How long does it take to switch?

Plan on a few weeks for a small studio and longer for a firm with years of projects, mostly spent on three things:
the item and vendor lists, open projects (orders in flight, deposits held), and the chart of accounts if the books
move too. Start new projects in the new system and finish old ones in the old, rather than migrating every open
order halfway through.

## What does it cost?

Design platforms are priced per user per month, with tiers for features such as accounting or client portals;
QuickBooks Online is a separate subscription when the books stay there. Compare the total for your team size,
including the bookkeeper's seat, and the cost of the hours each option saves or adds. Vendors' prices change, so take
them from their current pricing pages.

## What questions should you ask a vendor?

- Where do client deposits sit until the order is placed, and how do they appear in the books?
- Can one item carry a different markup from the project default, and does the proposal show it?
- What happens to an approved item when the vendor changes the price before the order?
- Which records sync to QuickBooks Online, in which direction, and what happens when a sync fails?
- Can a workroom or installer see only their own orders?
- How do we export all our data if we leave, and in what format?

The last question is the one most firms forget; ask for an export of a test project before you sign.

## When does QuickBooks alone stop working?

When product lines need markup, deposits, purchase orders and receiving tracked per item; QuickBooks Online handles
the accounting but not the design workflow. See [QuickBooks for interior designers](/interiors/quickbooks-for-interior-designers).

## When does a custom system make sense?

When the firm also runs a workroom or furniture shop, has its own pricing and approval rules, or works across two
companies, and off-the-shelf tools force workarounds in each. We built one for Savistar & Saag, an interior design
studio and its sister furniture business: quotes from an item library, design projects from brief to handover, the
furniture shop's jobs and crew, freight checks and staff-limited views of the cash book. See
[software for interior design firms](/industries/interior-design-and-furniture) and, for India,
[ERP for interior design firms](/erp/interior-design-firms).
