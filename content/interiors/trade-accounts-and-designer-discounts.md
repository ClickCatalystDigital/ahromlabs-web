## What is a designer trade account?

An account with a vendor that sells to design professionals at a trade price, below what the public pays, and lets
them buy without sales tax when they are buying to resell. Retail brands run trade programs alongside their stores;
fabric, wallcovering and furniture houses that sell only through designers open trade accounts. Each sets its own
terms, so the same firm can hold forty accounts with forty sets of rules about discounts, deposits, returns and tax.

## Who qualifies for a trade program?

Credentialed design businesses, and the vendor decides what counts. Arhaus's terms open its program to individual
design professionals and related businesses only, and its FAQ asks for two forms of documentation from two different
categories, one of them a business license or a membership card from an approved professional organization. Expect
most programs to ask for some combination of a business license, a resale certificate, a design association
membership and proof of a working practice, and to check them again later.

## How much is the trade discount?

Whatever the vendor sets, and it is rarely the only term that matters. Arhaus's terms give members at least 30% off
regularly priced products, online and in stores, with no minimum purchase, but not on protection plans, floor samples,
shipping or delivery fees, or taxes, and with some products excluded. Returns are credited at the discount rate, so a
returned sofa refunds what you paid, not the retail price. Read every program for its exclusions, minimums, deposit
terms, restocking fees and whether the discount applies to sale items before building a price on it.

## How do you buy tax-free on a trade account?

With a resale certificate, filed with each vendor. CDTFA puts the rule simply: when you are the retailer of an item,
you may buy it for resale without paying sales tax to the vendor, and you collect it from the client instead. Vendors
add their own paperwork. Arhaus wants a resale certificate for each state where items are delivered, provided with each
order, and its FAQ says that if the order's billing information doesn't match the certificate, the tax can't be removed.
[Resale certificates](/sales-tax/resale-certificates) covers the forms and the multistate certificate, and
[sales tax for interior designers](/sales-tax/interior-designers-sales-tax) what you then charge the client.

## Can a client use the designer's trade account?

No. Arhaus's FAQ says clients are not permitted to use your trade ID for showroom purchases, and CDTFA says it is not
legal to give your permit number to anyone, clients included, so they can buy at retail stores or furniture marts
without paying sales tax. If a client wants the trade price, the firm buys the piece and sells it to them, with the tax
on the invoice.

## Should you pass the trade discount on to the client?

It is your call, and the client agreement should say which you do. Take $50,000 of furniture at list, bought on a 30%
trade discount for $35,000:

| Approach | Client pays (before tax) | Firm's gross profit | As a markup on net cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bill at list price | $50,000 | $15,000 | 42.9% |
| Share half the discount | $42,500 | $7,500 | 21.4% |
| Cost plus 20% | $42,000 | $7,000 | 20.0% |
| Pass it all through, purchasing fee 15% of list | $35,000 + $7,500 fee | $7,500 | (fee, not markup) |

Billing at list is easy to defend (the client pays what the store charges) and keeps the discount as the firm's
margin. Sharing it is a selling point. A purchasing fee on list price is one of the fee bases in ASID's contract forms.
[How to charge for interior design](/interiors/how-to-charge-for-interior-design) compares the fee structures, and
markup against margin.

## What should software do with trade accounts?

When comparing systems, check that it can:

- Hold **each vendor's trade terms**: discount, exclusions, minimums, deposit, returns and restocking fee.
- Store the **trade account number and resale certificates** per vendor, with the states each covers and expiry dates.
- Price a line from **list price and discount**, showing net cost, sell price and markup together.
- Warn when a **certificate is missing** for the delivery state before the PO goes.
- Track **returns and credits** at the price actually paid, not the list price.
- Report **spend by vendor**, which is what you take to a vendor when asking for better terms.
