## Can an interior design firm run on QuickBooks Online alone?

It can keep the books, but not run the projects. QuickBooks Online records invoices, bills, payments, bank feeds and
sales tax well, and your accountant already knows it. What a design firm needs on top is everything that happens
before a bill exists: the proposal by room, the client's approval of each item, purchase orders with acknowledgements
and ship dates, receiving, and deposits tied to the items they pay for. Firms that try to do that in QuickBooks end up
with a spreadsheet beside it, and the spreadsheet becomes the real system.

## Which QuickBooks Online plan does a design firm need?

Plus, if you buy product for clients. [Intuit's help](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/purchase-orders/create-send-purchase-orders-quickbooks-online/L2mVpjOoq_US_en_US) puts purchase orders and billable expenses (with an optional
markup rate) in QuickBooks Online Plus and Advanced only, and those are the two features a reselling firm uses most.
[Intuit's pricing page](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/pricing/) lists Plus with 5 users and features including budgets, project profitability, inventory and
classes and locations.

| Plan (list price a month, 6 Nov 2026) | Users | What matters for a design firm |
|---|---|---|
| Essentials, $85 | 3 | Invoices, bills and reports; no purchase orders, no billable expenses |
| Plus, $140 | 5 | Purchase orders, billable expenses with markup, project profitability, classes |
| Advanced, $340 | 25 | Plus, more users, custom permissions and workflow automation |

Intuit was offering 50% off for three months when the page was read; prices change, so check before you buy. A firm
that moves from QuickBooks Desktop should read [QuickBooks Online vs Desktop](/manufacturing/quickbooks-online-vs-desktop)
first, because design platforms that sync tend to sync with Online only (Houzz Pro says so in its help).

## What can't QuickBooks Online track for a design firm?

The product side of a project, before and after the bill:

| A design firm needs | QuickBooks Online |
|---|---|
| A proposal by room, with images, net cost, markup and sell price per item | Estimates list products and prices; markup is a billable-expense setting |
| Client approval per item, with a date | No approval step per line |
| A purchase order from the approved items, with sidemarks | Purchase orders (Plus and up), typed in or synced |
| Vendor acknowledgement, ship date, cutting for approval | Not held |
| Partial receipts, damage and returns per PO line | Not held; a PO closes when it is added to a bill |
| A client deposit tied to the items it pays for | A deposit on an estimate, held as a liability until invoiced |
| Budget per room against approved, on order and invoiced | Budgets (Plus and up) in the books, not tied to proposal lines |

[Studio Designer](https://www.studiodesigner.com/accounting/) makes the same point from its side: each item needs an accounting of the designer's actual cost, the
client markup and the room it goes in. That is a project record, not a ledger line.

## How do design platforms work with QuickBooks?

Some replace it, some sync to it. Studio Designer describes itself as integrated project management, time billing,
product sourcing and accounting, and [Design Manager](https://manuals.designmanager.com/manuals/dmpro/an_interior_design_business_model.htm) as a project management and accounting package: both can be the
books themselves. [Houzz Pro](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-help/r/syncing-houzz-pro-to-quickbooks-online-faqs) syncs to QuickBooks Online: its help says proposals, estimates, invoices, retainers,
change orders and payments sync as soon as they're approved, sent or marked paid, purchase orders sync if you have
Plus or above, and the sync is one way, from Houzz Pro to QuickBooks, so a change made in QuickBooks isn't sent back.
[Programa](https://programa.design/features/purchase-orders-for-interior-designers) builds purchase orders from a project's schedules.

| Product | Where the books live | Purchase orders | What its own pages say about QuickBooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| QuickBooks Online | QuickBooks | Plus and Advanced | (It is QuickBooks) |
| Houzz Pro | QuickBooks Online | Yes; sync needs Plus or above | One-way sync to Online; no Desktop |
| Studio Designer | Studio Designer | Product sourcing in the platform | Its accounting page argues for purpose-built accounting over QuickBooks |
| Design Manager | Design Manager | Yes, with acknowledgements and receiving | Its own accounting package |
| Programa | Not covered on the page we read | From schedule data | Not covered on the page we read |

This table holds only what each vendor's own pages say; check the current version with them before choosing.

## Who should own the data when two systems are connected?

Decide which system is the source for each record before you connect them, and never edit the same record in both. In
a one-way sync like Houzz Pro's, documents are made and changed in the design platform and QuickBooks only receives
them; an edit in QuickBooks may be overwritten by the next sync. The same rule applies to any connector we build:
[connecting QuickBooks Online](/manufacturing/quickbooks-integration) covers matching, retries and what to do when a
send fails.

## What should design software do with QuickBooks?

When comparing systems, check that it can:

- Send **invoices** to QuickBooks Online with the client and each line's income account and tax code.
- Send **client payments and deposits**, applied to the right invoice, or held as a liability until it is raised.
- Send **vendor bills** from completed purchase orders, with the vendor deposit taken off.
- Send **vendor payments** linked to their bills.
- Say **which system owns each record**, and show what was sent, when, and what failed with a Retry.
- Work with **QuickBooks Online Plus**, the plan a reselling firm needs anyway.
- Keep the **design data**: proposals, approvals, POs and receipts, which QuickBooks can't hold, in your own export.
- Mark **1099 vendors** and total what you paid each one: see [1099s for installers and workrooms](/interiors/1099s-for-installers-and-workrooms).

## Does your demo work with QuickBooks?

Yes. Our [design studio demo](https://interiors-erp-us.ahromlabs.com/demo) is connected to an Intuit sandbox company.
An invoice goes to QuickBooks Online with its client when it leaves draft, each company's lines on its own income item;
a client payment goes when it is recorded, unapplied, for the accountant to apply; and a vendor purchase order goes as a
bill when it is complete. Sending happens in the background, and a failure shows on the invoice with its reason and a
Retry button. Vendor payments are not sent yet, because the demo's cash book names the vendor but not the bill.
QuickBooks calculates its own sales tax from the address, so its total can differ from the demo's per-line tax.
[Purchase orders and procurement](/interiors/purchase-orders-and-procurement) and
[invoices, deposits and retainers](/interiors/invoices-deposits-and-retainers) list what else the demo does and
doesn't do.
