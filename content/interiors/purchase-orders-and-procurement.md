## What does procurement mean in interior design?

Procurement is buying what the client approved and getting it installed: ordering from each vendor, paying their
deposits, confirming ship dates, approving fabric, arranging freight, receiving and inspecting the goods, storing
them and delivering them. On a furnished house that can be a hundred items from thirty vendors, each on its own lead
time, and it is where most of a design firm's administrative hours go. A procurement fee, where a firm charges one,
pays for that work.

## What are the steps from approval to installation?

| Step | What happens | Date to record | Who chases |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Client approval and deposit | The client signs off the item and pays the product deposit | Approved on | Designer |
| 2 Purchase order | A PO per vendor and ship-to address: item, finish, quantity, net price, sidemark | Ordered on | Purchasing |
| 3 Vendor deposit | Paid with the PO if the vendor requires it | Deposit paid on | Bookkeeper |
| 4 Acknowledgement | The vendor confirms the order, price and an expected ship date | Acknowledged on, ship date | Purchasing |
| 5 Cutting for approval (CFA) | For fabric, a cutting of the actual dye lot is sent for approval before it is used | CFA sent, CFA approved | Designer |
| 6 Ship and freight | The vendor ships, often to a receiving warehouse, with tracking | Shipped on | Purchasing |
| 7 Receiving | Counted, inspected, damage noted, stored | Received on, quantity, condition | Warehouse |
| 8 Delivery and install | Scheduled with the client, white-glove crew places and unpacks | Delivered on | Project manager |
| 9 Vendor bill | Matched to the PO and the receipt before payment | Bill date, amount | Bookkeeper |

Design software records the same steps. Design Manager's manual describes recording acknowledgements, receiving
merchandise and tracking back-ordered products, with an acknowledgement date (when the vendor receives and accepts
the PO), an expected ship date, a cut for approval date, and a received date and quantity per item.

## What is a vendor acknowledgement, and why does it matter?

It is the vendor's confirmation that it has accepted your purchase order, usually with its own order number, the
price and an expected ship date. Until you have one, you don't know the order exists. Check every acknowledgement
against the PO the day it arrives: a wrong finish, a changed price or a ship date three months later than quoted is
cheap to fix before production and expensive after. An order with no acknowledgement after a few days is the first
thing to chase.

## How do vendor deposits work?

A vendor deposit is paid with the order so the vendor starts work. Design Manager's manual defines it that way and
notes deposits are normally less than 100% of the cost of the merchandise; where a vendor wants full payment, it
suggests recording a vendor invoice instead. Track each deposit against its PO, so the vendor's final bill is reduced
by it and the deposit isn't forgotten if an order is cancelled. Deposits paid to vendors and deposits taken from the
client should be compared per project: [invoices, deposits and retainers](/interiors/invoices-deposits-and-retainers)
works out how much client deposit covers them.

## How should back-orders and damaged pieces be tracked?

By quantity, not by a note. A PO line is open until everything ordered has arrived in good condition, so the
outstanding quantity is what was ordered, minus what was delivered, plus what was sent back:

| Item: dining chair, walnut, client's fabric | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ordered | 8 |
| Delivered 12 Oct | 6 |
| Returned 14 Oct (one with a split seat rail) | 1 |
| Delivered 30 Oct | 2 |
| **Outstanding** | 8 − 8 + 1 = **1** |

The PO stays open for one chair, with the vendor's replacement date beside it, and the client's installation date
is checked against it. Before the vendor's bill is paid, it is matched to the PO and to what arrived: a
[three-way match](/systems/three-way-match).

## Why use a receiving warehouse, and what does freight cost?

A receiving warehouse takes deliveries for the firm, inspects them, reports damage while the freight claim can still
be made, stores pieces until the whole room is ready, then delivers and places them in one trip. It adds a storage
and handling charge and a second freight leg; in return the client's home sees one delivery, not thirty.

Freight changes the sales tax, so bill it on its own line. In California, CDTFA says delivery charges on a taxable sale
are generally not taxable when a carrier delivers directly to the client and your charge does not exceed what you paid
the carrier; delivery from the factory or dealer to your place of business or another intermediate point, such as a
receiving warehouse, is generally taxable. Other states have their own rules:
[sales tax for interior designers](/sales-tax/interior-designers-sales-tax).

## Can QuickBooks Online handle interior design purchase orders?

It can hold them, on Plus or Advanced. Intuit's help says purchase orders are only available in QuickBooks Online Plus
and Advanced; a PO tells the vendor what you intend to buy, can ship straight to a customer, stays open until it is
added to a bill or expense, and then closes. Open POs can be listed in reports. What it doesn't hold is the design
firm's part: the acknowledgement, the ship date, the CFA, partial receipts against the line, damage and returns, and
which client approval the PO came from. Design platforms add those; Programa, for example, creates purchase orders
from a project's schedule data. More in [QuickBooks for interior designers](/interiors/quickbooks-for-interior-designers).

## What should procurement software do for a design firm?

When comparing systems, check that it can:

- Create **POs from the approved proposal**, grouped by vendor and ship-to address, with the sidemark (client and
  room) on every line.
- Record the **vendor deposit** per PO and deduct it from the vendor's bill.
- Hold the **acknowledgement**: number, date, confirmed price and expected ship date, and flag POs with none.
- Track **CFAs** and other approvals that hold an order.
- Record **partial receipts, damage and returns** per line, with the outstanding quantity worked out.
- Show **what is late**: past its ship date, back-ordered, or due after the client's installation date.
- Hold **freight per delivery**, checked against the carrier's rate, on its own invoice line.
- **Match the vendor's bill** to the PO and the receipt, and send the bill to QuickBooks Online.
- Show the client's **project budget** against POs placed: see [project budget tracking](/interiors/project-budget-tracking).
