## What goes into the cost of a custom furniture order?

Three kinds of cost, each tied to the order: materials bought for it, the labor of the people who built it, and work
sent out (upholstery, metalwork, finishing). Overhead (rent, tools, the office) is then spread across orders or left
for the monthly accounts. Most small shops know their material cost roughly and their labor cost not at all, because
time is recorded on a timesheet for payroll and never against the order.

## How is labor costed per order?

From a daily record of who worked on which order, for how long, priced at what each person costs. QuickBooks Online,
with projects (on Plus and up), offers two ways: payroll expenses, which appear after payroll is run, or estimated hourly
costs, which multiply the hours worked by a fixed rate per employee and can include overhead. Either way, the hours have
to be logged against the order at the time, not reconstructed at month-end.

For a shop that pays some people a salary and some a day rate, the day's cost of each person can be spread across the
orders they logged time to that day:

| Monday | Pay | Day's cost | Hours on the table | Hours on other orders | Cost to the table |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carlos (salary, $5,200 a month, 20 days worked) | $5,200 ÷ 20 | $260 | 6 | 2 | $195 |
| Jake (day rate) | $220 a day | $220 | 8 | 0 | $220 |
| **Monday's labor on the table** | | | | | **$415** |

Repeated over the days the table was in the shop, that comes to the $1,560 in the example below.

## How does a $6,500 dining table work out?

A made-up walnut dining table for a design client, quoted at $6,500 before tax:

| Cost | Detail | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Lumber | Vendor PO, walnut, 120 board feet | $1,400 |
| Hardware and finish | Vendor PO | $250 |
| Freight on the lumber | Carrier charge on the PO | $200 |
| **Materials** | | **$1,850** |
| Labor | Work logs over 9 days, priced as above | $1,560 |
| **Total cost** | | **$3,410** |
| **Margin** | $6,500 − $3,410 | **$3,090 (47.5%)** |

If one board is returned as defective and credited at $120, materials fall to $1,730 and the margin rises to $3,210.
If the shop sends the top out for a lacquer finish at $450, that is outside work on the same order, and the margin
falls by the same amount.

## How is custom furniture taxed?

As goods, design included, in the states quoted here. CDTFA's Publication 35 says tax applies to fabrication labor,
whether the materials are yours or the client's, and that when you contract to provide an item custom-made by a
subcontractor, your charge for the item and the labor to make it is taxable. New York's advisory opinion TSB-A-08(30)S
found that preparing millwork shop drawings, and supervising installation of the millwork, is a taxable interior design
service. British Columbia's PST 139 says that when you design custom furniture you make to the customer's
specifications, the design is taxable because the purpose of the transaction is buying the custom goods. Built-in
millwork installed in a building can follow real property rules instead: [California's interior design sales tax
rules](/sales-tax/california-interior-design-sales-tax) cover lump-sum and time-and-materials contracts.

## What if the shop and the design studio are one business, or two?

Then each order needs to say which company sold it and what the studio charged the client. Many design firms own a
workroom or furniture shop, sometimes as a separate company; the shop's job cost and the studio's markup on the same
piece are two different margins. [Two companies, one book](/notes/two-companies-one-book) is how our demo models a
studio and its furniture shop with one client list and separate letterheads, and [project budget
tracking](/interiors/project-budget-tracking) covers the studio's side.

## What should job costing software do for a furniture shop?

When comparing systems, check that it can:

- Log **each person's hours per order per day**, from the shop floor, with the part they worked on.
- Price labor from **each person's actual pay**, salary or day rate, not one shop-wide rate.
- Link **vendor POs, returns and freight** to the order they were bought for.
- Hold **outside work** (upholstery, finishing, metal) as its own cost line on the order.
- Show **quoted against actual** per order, while the order is still in the shop, not after delivery.
- Send invoices and vendor bills to **QuickBooks Online** without retyping.
