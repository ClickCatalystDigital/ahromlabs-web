## What does a design firm invoice for?

Two different things, on two different rhythms: design time, billed as a retainer and then by the hour or in flat-fee
instalments; and product, billed as a deposit when the client approves it and a balance before delivery. Keeping
them on separate invoices, or at least separate lines, matters for cash (product money is mostly owed to vendors) and
for tax (in several states design fees and product are taxed differently).

## What is a retainer, and how is it different from a deposit?

A retainer pays for design work before it starts; a deposit pays for product before you order it. [ASID's Minnesota
chapter](https://mn.asid.org/costs) defines the retainer as money paid by the client to the designer and applied to the balance due at the end
of the project, customarily paid when the agreement is signed. A product deposit is collected later, when the client
approves what to buy; [Studio Designer](https://www.studiodesigner.com/blog/proposal-confidence-vs-confusion-how-interior-designers-can-get-it-right/) calls the proposal the point where the first merchandise deposit is often
collected.

| | Retainer | Product deposit |
|---|---|---|
| Paid when | The agreement is signed | The client approves a proposal |
| Pays for | Design time | Furniture, lighting, fabric, workroom orders |
| Applied to | The final design invoice | The product invoice for the same items |
| Mostly owed to | The firm | Vendors, often within days |

## How much deposit should a client pay for product?

Enough to cover what you must pay vendors before the client pays the balance. Take a proposal of $54,000 for product
that costs you $40,000 net, and vendors that each want a deposit with the order:

| Vendors want with the order | You pay out | 50% client deposit ($27,000) | Deposit needed to break even |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50% of net cost | $20,000 | $7,000 left over | 37% ($20,000 ÷ $54,000) |
| 100% of net cost | $40,000 | **$13,000 short** | 74% ($40,000 ÷ $54,000) |

The rule of thumb is cost ÷ price: at a 35% markup, a deposit under 74% means the firm finances custom pieces with its
own cash whenever a vendor wants full payment up front. Asking for full payment on custom and non-returnable items
closes that gap; [purchase orders and procurement](/interiors/purchase-orders-and-procurement) covers
vendor deposits.

## What should an interior design invoice show?

At least the client and project, the invoice number and date, the terms and due date, each line with its description,
quantity and price, sales tax by line, the deposits and retainer applied, and the balance due. Design manuals put the
deposit step in the same place: [Design Manager's model workflow](https://manuals.designmanager.com/manuals/dmpro/an_interior_design_business_model.htm) is approval and a deposit, then purchase orders, then
receiving, then a final invoice that applies the deposit.

Show nontaxable charges as their own lines. In California, [CDTFA](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub35/designer-fees-and-charges-related-to-the-sale-of-merchandise.htm) says tax does not apply to professional fees that are
not directly related to a sale of merchandise, and tells designers to list those charges separately on the invoice;
[sales tax for interior designers](/sales-tax/interior-designers-sales-tax) has each state's rule.

## How does a payment schedule work on a $54,000 proposal?

Here is one project from contract to handover, with a $12,000 flat design fee and the product priced in
[how to charge for interior design](/interiors/how-to-charge-for-interior-design). Tax is left out to keep the
arithmetic visible.

| Invoice | When | Lines | Amount | Applied | Due |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Retainer | Agreement signed | Design retainer | $3,000 | | $3,000 |
| 2 Design | End of concept | Design fee, 1st half | $6,000 | | $6,000 |
| 3 Product deposit | Proposal approved | 50% of $54,000 | $27,000 | | $27,000 |
| 4 Product balance | Before delivery | Product $54,000, freight $1,200 | $55,200 | −$27,000 deposit | $28,200 |
| 5 Final design | Installation | Design fee, 2nd half | $6,000 | −$3,000 retainer | $3,000 |
| **Total** | | | **$97,200** | **−$30,000** | **$67,200** |

Check: $12,000 design + $54,000 product + $1,200 freight = $67,200 paid over five invoices.

## How does QuickBooks Online record a client deposit?

As a liability until the work is invoiced. [Intuit's help](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/bank-deposits/request-customer-deposit-estimate-quickbooks/L81Kln0bN_US_en_US) says QuickBooks Online can request a deposit on an estimate
as a dollar amount or a percentage, records the money in a liability account rather than accounts receivable, and
applies it as a partial payment when the estimate becomes an invoice. Intuit adds that you should check your state for
any further rules on customer deposits, which matters both for when sales tax is due and for refunds if a project is
cancelled. In California, for example, CDTFA says a deposit for future delivery isn't reported until the goods are
delivered or ownership passes: see [California sales tax for interior designers](/sales-tax/california-interior-design-sales-tax).

If your design system sends invoices to QuickBooks but not the deposit's link to them, the accountant applies the
payment by hand. That is what our demo does today (below), and it is a question to ask of any system:
[connecting QuickBooks Online](/manufacturing/quickbooks-integration) explains the trade-offs.

## What should invoicing software do for a design firm?

When comparing systems, check that it can:

- Raise a **retainer** at signing and apply it to the final design invoice automatically.
- Take a **product deposit** per proposal or per item, as a percentage or an amount, and apply it to that item's
  invoice, not to whatever is open.
- Show **deposit coverage**: client deposits held against vendor deposits paid, per project.
- Keep **design fees and product on separate lines** with their own tax treatment.
- Carry **freight, delivery and installation** as their own lines, so each can be taxed by the state's rule.
- Send invoices, deposits and payments to **QuickBooks Online**, with each payment applied to its invoice.
- Handle **refunds and cancellations**: what is returned, what the restocking fee was, and what is kept.
