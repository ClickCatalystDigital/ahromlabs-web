title: "OEE (overall equipment effectiveness)"
answer: >
  A measure of how much of a machine's planned time produces good parts: availability × performance × quality.
domain: [manufacturing]
order: 8
published: 2026-11-02
updated: 2026-11-02
---

- **Availability**: run time ÷ planned production time (stops and breakdowns count against it).
- **Performance**: (ideal cycle time × total parts) ÷ run time (slow cycles count against it).
- **Quality**: good parts ÷ total parts (scrap and rework count against it).

Example: a machine planned for 8 hours runs 6.4 (availability 80%), makes 300 parts at an ideal 1 minute each in those 6.4 hours, 384 minutes (performance 300 ÷ 384 = 78.1%), and 285 are good (quality 95%). OEE = 0.80 × 0.781 × 0.95 = 59.4%.

The number alone doesn't say what to fix; the three factors do. Collecting them needs stops, counts and scrap recorded at the machine, the same shop-floor data [scheduling](/manufacturing/production-scheduling-software) and [job costing](/manufacturing/quickbooks-job-costing) depend on.
