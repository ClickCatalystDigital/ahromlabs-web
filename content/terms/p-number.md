title: "P-number"
answer: >
  ASME Section IX's grouping of base metals with similar welding behaviour, so one procedure or welder test
  covers every material in the group: P-No. 1 for carbon steel, P-No. 8 for austenitic stainless.
domain: [pressure]
order: 8
published: 2026-11-01
updated: 2026-11-07
---

It matters because it decides how many procedure and welder tests a shop needs: a qualification on one P-No. 1 carbon steel covers the others, but welding P-No. 1 to P-No. 8 stainless is a dissimilar joint needing its own procedure. Group numbers inside a P-number matter when impact testing applies.

Common materials in each group, with F-numbers and A-numbers for filler metals, are in [P-numbers, F-numbers and A-numbers](/asme/p-numbers). The P-number on a WPS is only as good as the record of which plate is in which seam; see [material traceability](/services/material-traceability).
