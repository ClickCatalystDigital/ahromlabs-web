## What is the R stamp?

The R stamp is the National Board's Certificate of Authorization for work on pressure equipment already in service.
In the National Board's words, it "offers the Certificate of Authorization and R symbol stamp for the repair and/or
alteration of boilers, pressure vessels, and other pressure-retaining items." It is not an ASME stamp: ASME's
[stamps](/asme/stamps) cover new construction, and once an item is built, certified and in service, repairs and
alterations come under the National Board Inspection Code (NBIC), whose Part 3 provides guidance "to perform,
verify, and document acceptable repairs or alterations to pressure-retaining items regardless of code of
construction."

## Who needs the R stamp?

Organizations that weld or otherwise repair or alter boilers, pressure vessels and their pressure parts in service,
where the jurisdiction requires NBIC work by an authorized organization, which is most US states and Canadian
provinces that have adopted the NBIC. Typical holders: boiler service companies, field repair contractors, vessel
shops that also do repair work, plant maintenance contractors, and manufacturers who repair their own installed
equipment. A shop that holds an ASME U or S stamp still needs the R stamp to repair stamped equipment in service.

## What is the difference between a repair and an alteration?

| Work | NBIC category | Report |
|---|---|---|
| Restoring an item to a safe condition: replacing tubes in kind, weld repair of a crack, replacing a section of shell with the same material and thickness | Repair | Form R-1, Report of Repair (NB-66) |
| Changing the item's design: rerating to a higher pressure or temperature, adding a nozzle that needs reinforcement calculations, changing material or dimensions that affect pressure capability | Alteration | Form R-2, Report of Alteration (NB-229) |

The NBIC gives examples of each and the classification rules (2025 edition Supplement 10 covers classifying repairs
and alterations); when in doubt, the Inspector decides. An alteration needs design work and calculations to the
original construction code; a repair usually needs a procedure and records but not new design.

## What does a shop need to get the R stamp?

The National Board lists the requirements. The organization must:

- "have and maintain an inspection agreement with an Authorized Inspection Agency";
- "have a written quality system that complies with the requirements of the current edition of the National Board
  Inspection Code (NBIC), NB-415, and includes the expected scope of activities";
- have the current edition of all parts of the NBIC;
- "have available a copy of the code of construction appropriate for the intended scope of work"; and
- complete the NB-12 application.

"Prior to issuance of a National Board R Certificate of Authorization, the organization and its facilities are
subject to an onsite review of its quality system." The review can be held with an ASME joint review where the
shop is also seeking or renewing an ASME certificate.

## How much does the R stamp cost?

The National Board's page: "The certificate fee for the R Certificate of Authorization is $1150 USD for a three-year
period. For new issuances, there will be an additional one-time charge of $150 USD for the loan of the steel R symbol
stamp." Review fees or an advance deposit are set after the application, depending on who performs the review, and
the Authorized Inspection Agency's contract and the cost of writing the quality manual come on top.

## How does an R repair job run?

1. **Scope and classification.** The shop and the Inspector agree whether the work is a repair or an alteration, and
   the method.
2. **Acceptance by the Inspector before work starts**, and by the jurisdiction where its rules require it.
3. **Materials** with their [MTRs](/asme/mtr), welding to a qualified [WPS](/asme/wps-pqr-wpq) by qualified welders
   with current [continuity](/asme/welder-continuity), heat treatment where required.
4. **Examination and pressure test** as the NBIC and the Inspector require.
5. **Report**: Form R-1 (or R-2 for an alteration), signed by the shop and the Inspector; for registered items, filed
   with the National Board.
6. **Nameplate**: an R stamping or nameplate attached near the original nameplate.

Problems found along the way are handled as [nonconformances](/asme/nonconformance-report), the same as in new
construction.

## What records does an R holder keep?

Per job: the classification, the Inspector's acceptance, the procedure, materials and MTRs, welds with WPS and
welder, NDE reports, heat treatment and test records, the signed R-1 or R-2, and the nameplate. Per shop: the quality
manual, welder qualifications and continuity, calibration records, and the certificate and its renewal date. A
repair shop with a job and inspection system can keep all of this per job; our US boiler demo runs the same
inspection, NCR and record flow for new construction, and R-1 reports are something we would add for a repair
customer. See [ERP for boiler and pressure vessel shops](/industries/boiler-pressure-vessel-manufacturing) and
[National Board registration](/asme/national-board-registration).
