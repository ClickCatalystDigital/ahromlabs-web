## What is ASME Section IX?

ASME Section IX is the Code section that qualifies welding, brazing and fusing: the procedures a shop uses and the
people who use them. ASME's own description is that it "contains rules relating to the qualification of welding,
brazing, and fusing procedures as required by other BPVC sections". It answers two questions with test coupons: can
this procedure make a sound weld, and can this person make it. The Code text is ASME's and paid; this guide cites
paragraphs and explains them, and your copy of the [2025 Section IX](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/bpvc-ix-bpvc-section-ix-welding-brazing-fusing-qualifications) is the authority.

## What does Section IX not cover?

It doesn't say which joints are welded, the joint design, how much NDE a weld gets or when heat treatment is
needed. Those come from the construction code: [Section VIII](/asme/section-viii), [Section I](/asme/section-i-vs-iv)
or a [B31 piping code](/asme/b31-1-vs-b31-3), which then sends you to Section IX for qualification. That is why a
WPS cites Section IX for its qualification and the vessel's data report cites Section VIII for everything else.

## What are the parts of Section IX?

Section IX has four parts:

| Part | Covers |
|---|---|
| QG | General requirements shared by all processes, including the organization's responsibility for its qualifications |
| QW | Welding: procedure qualification (QW-200), performance qualification (QW-300), welding data and variables (QW-400) and standard welding procedure specifications (QW-500) |
| QB | Brazing |
| QF | Plastic fusing (polyethylene pipe) |

## What are a WPS, a PQR and a WPQ?

They are the three documents Section IX qualification produces:

- **WPS** (welding procedure specification): the written instruction a welder follows, with a range for each variable.
- **PQR** (procedure qualification record): the record of the test coupon that proves the WPS works: what was actually used, and the test results.
- **WPQ** (welder or welding operator performance qualification): the record that a person passed their test, and what it qualifies them for.

How they link and what goes in each is in [WPS vs PQR vs WPQ](/asme/wps-pqr-wpq); which changes need a new PQR is in
[essential variables](/asme/essential-variables); what a welder's test covers is in
[welder qualification](/asme/welder-qualification); and how a welder keeps a qualification alive is the
[continuity rule](/asme/welder-continuity).

## Where do I get Section IX forms?

ASME publishes suggested formats free on its [data report forms page](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/publications-information/asme-data-report-forms): QW-482 for a WPS, QW-483 for a PQR,
QW-484A for a welder's WPQ, QW-484B for a welding operator's, and QW-485 for demonstrating a standard welding
procedure specification, with QB and QF equivalents for brazing and fusing. They are suggested formats: a shop may use
its own layout if it records everything Section IX requires.

## How does Section IX group materials and filler metals?

It groups base metals by **P-number** (with Group numbers where toughness matters) and filler metals by **F-number**
and, for steel weld metal, **A-number**, so one test covers a family of materials instead of one specification. See
[P-numbers, F-numbers and A-numbers](/asme/p-numbers).

## Can a shop use another company's PQR?

No: each organization qualifies its own procedures and welders and takes responsibility for them (Part QG). The
exception is the standard welding procedure specifications published by AWS that Section IX accepts in Article V
(QW-500): a shop may use one after demonstrating it, recorded on the QW-485 format.

## Does a shop need Section IX for an ASME certificate?

Yes, if it welds. ASME's [required code books](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/ASME-Certification-Process/Required-Code-Books) list Section IX for the U, S, H and other certificates, and note
that Section II Part C and Sections V and IX are not required "if welding, brazing and fusing are not within the
scope of their work".

## Do B31.1 and B31.3 use Section IX?

Yes. Both piping codes send welding procedure and welder qualification to Section IX, and B31.3 adds some
requirements of its own. A shop's WPSs and PQRs can serve its vessel and its piping work where the variables fit
both codes; see [B31.1 vs B31.3](/asme/b31-1-vs-b31-3).

## What changed in the 2025 Edition of Section IX?

The changes are listed in the "Summary of Changes" at the front of the book, and they apply to new qualifications from
1 January 2026: the American Welding Society's [Welding Journal summary](https://www.aws.org/magazines-and-media/welding-journal/2025/july/wj-july-25-feature-05-sperko/) says the changes "will become
mandatory for new qualifications on January 1, 2026" (the article's detail is for AWS members). Procedures and
welders qualified under an earlier edition stay qualified; a new qualification follows the edition in force when it
is made. See [ASME Code editions](/asme/editions).
