## What does the ASME PP stamp mean?

PP stands for Pressure Piping. ASME lists it among the power boiler certificates under Section I of the Boiler and
Pressure Vessel Code, as "PP - Pressure Piping", beside S (power boilers), A (assembly of power boilers), E (electric
boilers) and M (miniature boilers). A shop that holds it is authorized to fabricate and assemble the pressure piping
that Section I covers and to apply the ASME Certification Mark with the PP designator to that piping.

"Stamp" is the everyday word. ASME's FAQ records that "The old Code Symbol Stamps for ASME BPVC Sections I, IV, VIII,
X, and XII (U, S, PP, etc.) were discontinued on January 31, 2013." Since then there is one Certification Mark, and
PP is the designator placed with it. See [ASME stamps](/asme/stamps) for the full list.

## What piping does the PP stamp cover?

Mainly boiler external piping: the piping that runs from the boiler proper out to the valve or valves that Section I
requires, such as the main steam line to its stop valve, the feedwater line back to its stop and check valves, and
the blowoff and drain lines to their valves. Section I holds the administrative control over this piping (who may
build it, inspection, stamping and the data report), and the technical rules for materials, design, fabrication and
testing come from [B31.1 Power Piping](/asme/b31-1-vs-b31-3).

Piping beyond those valves is non-boiler external piping. It is built to B31.1 (or B31.3 in a process plant) and
doesn't need a PP certificate under the ASME Code, although the owner, the insurer or the state may set their own
requirements for who can weld it.

## What is the difference between the PP, S and A stamps?

| Certificate | Covers | Typical holder |
|---|---|---|
| S | Power boilers, and their parts and piping | Boiler manufacturers |
| PP | Pressure piping for power boilers | Pipe fabrication shops and mechanical contractors |
| A | Assembly of power boilers in the field | Erectors and installers |
| [U](/asme/u-stamp) | Pressure vessels under Section VIII Division 1 | Vessel shops |

A boiler manufacturer with the S certificate can build the boiler's external piping under it. A piping fabricator or
mechanical contractor who doesn't build boilers holds PP alone. Many shops hold PP together with U and the National
Board [R certificate](/asme/r-stamp), because power plant and industrial customers ask for all three.

## What does a shop need to get the PP stamp?

The same route as the other boiler certificates: an application to ASME, a written quality control system, the code
books, an inspection agreement and a joint review of the shop.

- **Inspection agreement.** ASME's handbook says "the Applicant must have an inspection agreement in place with an
  ASME Accredited Authorized Inspection Agency (AIA)", and its table of certificates lists PP as valid for 3 years
  with the agreement required. See [Authorized Inspector](/asme/authorized-inspector).
- **Quality control manual** describing how the shop controls drawings, materials, welding, examination, heat
  treatment, calibration and records for piping. See the [QC manual](/asme/quality-control-manual).
- **Code books.** ASME lists PP and S together ("Pressure Piping and Power Boilers"): Section I, Section II Parts A,
  B, C and D, Section V, Section IX, B31.1 Power Piping and CA-1.
- **Welding qualifications** to Section IX: procedures, welders and their continuity. See
  [WPS, PQR and WPQ](/asme/wps-pqr-wpq).
- **A demonstration piece** for the joint review, so the team can see the system working on real piping.

## How much does the PP stamp cost?

ASME's price guide: "All other Certificates: $4,500.00 per certificate and Advance Deposit: $11,000.00", and
"Certification Mark: $400.00 per stamp plus applicable tax and shipping". The deposit goes toward the cost of the
review. On top of that come the Authorized Inspection Agency's contract, the code books, and the time to write the
manual and qualify welding procedures. The certificate is renewed every three years with another review.

## Can an S stamp holder add PP without another review?

Yes. ASME's FAQ says "A full ASME Joint Review is not required for: A holder of an ASME S Certificate who applies for
an additional A or PP Certificate(s) of Authorization." ASME asks the team leader of the last review and the shop's
inspection agency to confirm in writing that the QC manual and system now cover the added work, and issues the
certificate once the fees are received. A shop that holds only U needs a joint review to add PP.

## Which data report goes with PP piping?

ASME's Section I forms include P-4A, the "Manufacturers' Data Report for Fabricated Piping", and P-4B, the
"Manufacturers' Data Report for Field Installed Mechanically Assembled Piping". The fabricator completes the report
for the piping it built, the Authorized Inspector signs it, and it goes to the boiler manufacturer or assembler to
join the boiler's own [data reports](/asme/data-reports). The piping carries the Certification Mark with the PP
designator, on the pipe or on a nameplate, as Section I allows for the size.

## Can you show a PP job from order to data report?

A made-up example: a fabricator receives an order for the main steam spool between a watertube boiler's outlet and
its stop valve.

1. **Contract review.** Confirm the piping is boiler external piping, the code edition, and who will file the report.
2. **Materials.** Pipe, fittings and flanges are received against their [MTRs](/asme/mtr) and marked with heat
   numbers.
3. **Welding.** Each joint is welded to a qualified procedure by a qualified welder, with the welder's identity
   recorded per joint.
4. **Examination and heat treatment** as B31.1 requires for the material and thickness; see [PWHT](/asme/pwht).
5. **Hold points.** The Authorized Inspector is notified at the points on the
   [inspection and test plan](/asme/inspection-and-test-plan).
6. **Pressure test**, witnessed where required; see [hydrostatic test](/asme/hydrostatic-test).
7. **Stamping and report.** The mark is applied and the P-4A is signed by the shop and the Inspector.

## What records should a PP shop keep?

Per job: the purchase order and code edition, drawings and revisions, MTRs by heat number, a weld map with the
procedure and welder for each joint, examination and heat treatment reports, the pressure test record, any
[nonconformance reports](/asme/nonconformance-report) and the signed data report. Per shop: the QC manual, welder
qualifications and continuity, calibration records, the inspection agreement, and the certificate's expiry date.
