## Why groups exist

There are thousands of material specifications. Without grouping, every pairing would need its own procedure test. Section IX assigns each base metal a **P-number** (and, for ferrous metals where toughness matters, a **Group number**), so a PQR on one material qualifies the others in its P-number. The full assignments are in Section IX (QW/QB-422) and Section II Part D; the common ones are below.

## Common P-numbers

| P-No. | Materials | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Carbon and carbon-manganese steels | SA-516 Gr 70, SA-106 Gr B, SA-105 |
| 3 | C–½Mo and low-chromium steels | SA-204, SA-335 P1 |
| 4 | 1–1¼Cr–½Mo steels | SA-387 Gr 11, SA-335 P11 |
| 5A | 2¼Cr–1Mo steels | SA-387 Gr 22, SA-335 P22 |
| 5B | 5Cr to 9Cr steels | SA-335 P5, P9 |
| 7 | Ferritic stainless | SA-240 Type 405, 409, 430 |
| 8 | Austenitic stainless | SA-240 Type 304, 316, 321 |
| 10H | Duplex stainless | SA-240 S31803, S32205 |
| 15E | Creep-strength enhanced ferritic steels | SA-335 P91 |
| 21–26 | Aluminum alloys | SB-209 5083 |
| 31–35 | Copper alloys | SB-171 |
| 41–49 | Nickel alloys | SB-168 Alloy 600, SB-443 Alloy 625 |
| 51–53 | Titanium | SB-265 Grade 2 |

Welding a P-No. 1 to a P-No. 8 is a dissimilar-metal joint and needs a procedure qualified for that combination.

## F-numbers: filler metal usability

| F-No. | Filler | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Heavy iron-powder steel electrodes | E7024 |
| 2 | Rutile steel electrodes | E6013 |
| 3 | Cellulosic steel electrodes | E6010, E6011 |
| 4 | Low-hydrogen steel electrodes | E7018 |
| 5 | Stainless steel electrodes | E308L-16 |
| 6 | Solid and metal-cored steel wires and rods (GTAW, GMAW, SAW) | ER70S-6 |
| 43 | Nickel alloy filler | ERNiCrMo-3 |

F-numbers matter most for **welders**: they reflect how hard the electrode is to handle, so a welder qualified with F-4 is generally also qualified for F-1 to F-3 in the same process, but not the other way round. Section IX's QW-433 table has the exact pairings, including the effect of welding with or without backing.

## A-numbers: weld metal chemistry

A-numbers classify the deposited weld metal of steels by composition (A-1 carbon steel, A-2 carbon–molybdenum, up to A-8 austenitic chromium–nickel and others). A change of A-number is a procedure [essential variable](/asme/essential-variables) for most processes.

## Using them well

- Write the P-number, Group number, F-number and A-number on every WPS and PQR; reviewers check them first.
- Check the material's P-number in the current edition: new materials are added and occasionally regrouped.
- The base metal's specification and grade come from its [MTR](/asme/mtr); see [Section II](/asme/section-ii) for SA vs A.

The documents these numbers appear on are explained in [WPS vs PQR vs WPQ](/asme/wps-pqr-wpq).
