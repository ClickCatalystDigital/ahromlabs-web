## Where the line is

| | Section IV (heating boilers) | Section I (power boilers) |
|---|---|---|
| Steam boilers | Up to 15 psi | Above 15 psi |
| Hot water boilers | Up to 160 psi and 250 °F | Above 160 psi or above 250 °F |
| Typical use | Space heating, domestic hot water | Process steam, power generation, high-temperature hot water |
| Certification designator | H (HLW for lined potable water heaters) | S, with A for assembly, E for electric, M for miniature, PP for boiler piping |
| Main data reports | H-2, H-3 (H-5 and H-5A for cast iron and cast aluminum) | P-2, P-3, P-4 |

Section IV allows cast iron and cast aluminum sectional boilers, which Section I does not, and its rules are simpler throughout because the energy stored at low pressure is much smaller.

## What changes for the shop

- **Design**: Section I has its own formulas (Part PG) and sets thicknesses for drums, headers and tubes in the hot zone; Section IV's are simpler and include minimum thicknesses.
- **Examination**: more volumetric examination of welds in Section I.
- **Testing**: a Section I boiler is hydrostatically tested to at least 1.5 times its maximum allowable working pressure (PG-99); the [hydrostatic test guide](/asme/hydrostatic-test) has a calculator.
- **Piping**: boiler external piping, from the boiler to the first stop valve and beyond as the Code defines it, is part of the Section I boiler and is built to [B31.1](/asme/b31-1-vs-b31-3) by a holder of a Section I certificate that covers it (S, A or PP).
- **Inspection**: both need an [Authorized Inspector](/asme/authorized-inspector), except Section IV cast iron and cast aluminum boilers, whose certificate needs no inspection agency agreement (see [ASME stamps](/asme/stamps)).

## In India

Boilers in India are registered under the Indian Boiler Regulations, 2026, whichever ASME section they were designed to. How the two fit together is in [ASME vs IBR 2026](/asme/asme-vs-ibr).
