## What does an ASME Authorized Inspector do?

An Authorized Inspector (AI) independently checks that a Code boiler, vessel or part was built to the Code, then
certifies the data report so the item can be stamped. They review the design, check materials and welding, review
NDE and heat treatment, witness the pressure test and sign the [data report](/asme/data-reports). ASME's handbook
says that for most certificates "an agreement with an ASME Accredited Authorized Inspection Agency (AIA) is
required", which is why there is an Inspector on nearly every stamped job.

## Who employs the Authorized Inspector?

An Authorized Inspection Agency (AIA) accredited by ASME. ASME's [accreditation page](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/Authorized-Inspection-Agency-Accreditation) says an AIA is an
organization that meets the criteria of ASME's QAI-1 standard, "Qualifications for Authorized Inspection", and that
AIAs are responsible for inspections of ASME pressure equipment during manufacture. Most are insurance companies
that write boiler and machinery cover; some are state or provincial jurisdictions acting as their own agency. ASME
publishes the list of accredited agencies with its downloadable resources.

## What commission does an Authorized Inspector hold?

The National Board Authorized Inspector Commission (AI). The National Board's [commissioned inspectors page](https://www.nationalboard.org/Index.aspx?pageID=392)
describes two commissions: the AI, for inspections required by the ASME Code during construction, and the Inservice
Inspector Commission (IS), for periodic inspections of equipment in operation. Each is earned by meeting the
education, experience, employment and examination requirements of NB-263, RCI-1, Rules for Commissioned Inspectors,
and the commission card is renewed every year.

## Can a shop hire an Authorized Inspector directly?

No. A shop signs an inspection agreement with an AIA, and the agency assigns the Inspector. ASME's handbook says the
AIA representative "is appointed by the Authorized Inspection Agency", and ASME won't schedule a shop's joint review
until the agency confirms the agreement. If a certificate holder changes agency, ASME's FAQ says it must tell ASME's
Conformity Assessment Department straight away, or risk losing its certification. See
[how to get the U stamp](/asme/u-stamp).

## Which certificates don't need an inspection agency?

The Section IV H certificate for cast iron and cast aluminum boilers, and the Section XIII pressure relief device
certificates (V, HV, UV, UD, UV3, UD3, TV, TD), per the table in ASME's handbook. The UM certificate needs one
indirectly, because it is only issued alongside a U or S certificate. Every other boiler and vessel certificate needs
an AIA agreement. See [ASME stamps](/asme/stamps).

## Do inspection agencies work outside the US?

Yes. ASME's FAQ says most AIAs operate worldwide and points to its list of agencies for local offices and contacts.
That is how a shop in India or elsewhere gets an Inspector for U or S work; see
[buying from India](/asme/buying-from-india).

## What does the Inspector check on a job?

On a stamped job the Inspector, in outline:

- reviews the design calculations and drawings for Code compliance;
- checks material against its [MTR](/asme/mtr) and its marking;
- checks that every weld is made to a qualified [WPS](/asme/wps-pqr-wpq) by a qualified welder in [continuity](/asme/welder-continuity);
- reviews NDE results and heat treatment records;
- witnesses the [hydrostatic or pneumatic test](/asme/hydrostatic-test);
- signs the data report and authorizes stamping.

A common check is to pick one weld and walk it back: weld number on the weld map, the welder's stamp, that welder's
qualification and continuity, the WPS, the PQR behind it, and the filler and base metal certificates.

## What are hold points?

Hold points are the steps in the shop's [quality control manual](/asme/quality-control-manual) where work stops until
the Inspector has seen it: typically material review, fit-up of critical joints, NDE review, the PWHT chart and the
hydro test. The manual and the inspection plan for the job name them, and the Inspector signs each one off.

## Is the Authorized Inspector the shop's quality control?

No. The Inspector checks that the shop's system worked; the shop runs it. If the shop's own inspection is missing,
the Inspector raises it as a nonconformity rather than doing it for them. The Inspector also sits on the review team
at the shop's ASME joint review, alongside the ASME Designee.

## Who inspects repairs to equipment in service?

A National Board commissioned inspector, for work done by a holder of the National Board's R certificate under the
National Board Inspection Code. The National Board's [R certificate page](https://www.nationalboard.org/Index.aspx?pageID=115&ID=160) requires an R holder to keep an
inspection agreement with an Authorized Inspection Agency too. See
[National Board registration and the R stamp](/asme/national-board-registration).

## What should a shop's records give the Inspector?

Everything the Inspector checks should come from records the shop already keeps, linked once: each weld to its
welders, WPS and NDE report, each part to its heat number and MTR. Our US boiler demo prints that as one ASME record
package, with the weld map, WPS and PQR, QW-322 continuity, MTRs by heat number, NDE, PWHT and hydro records, and the
Inspector's name and agency on the data report layout. See [ERP for boiler and pressure vessel shops](/industries/boiler-pressure-vessel-manufacturing).
