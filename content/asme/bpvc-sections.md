## What are the sections of the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code?

Thirteen, numbered I to XIII, and easiest to read as two kinds of book. A **construction section** is the rulebook
for one kind of equipment: what it may be made of, how it is designed, welded, examined, tested and stamped. A
**reference section** holds rules several construction sections point to, so they aren't written out in each.

| Section | Title, in short | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| I | Power boilers | Construction |
| II | Materials (Parts A to D) | Reference |
| III | Nuclear facility components | Construction |
| IV | Heating boilers | Construction |
| V | Nondestructive examination | Reference |
| VI | Care and operation of heating boilers | Recommended practice |
| VII | Care of power boilers | Recommended practice |
| VIII | Pressure vessels (Divisions 1, 2 and 3) | Construction |
| IX | Welding, brazing and fusing qualifications | Reference |
| X | Fiber-reinforced plastic pressure vessels | Construction |
| XI | In-service inspection of nuclear plants | Inspection |
| XII | Construction and continued service of transport tanks | Construction |
| XIII | Overpressure protection | Reference |

The whole Code is republished every two years; the current one is the [2025 Edition](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/bpvc-standards/bpvc-2025).

## What is the difference between a construction and a reference section?

A construction section is what a product is built and stamped to; a reference section is what it points to for one
topic. Section IX, for example, "contains rules relating to the qualification of welding, brazing, and fusing
procedures as required by other BPVC sections", in ASME's words, and Section XIII "sets requirements for design,
materials, inspection, testing, and marking of pressure relief valves, rupture disks, and related devices". A shop
working to Section VIII Division 1 therefore also works to Sections II, V, IX and XIII.

## Which books does a pressure vessel shop need?

For the U certificate, ASME's [required list](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/ASME-Certification-Process/Required-Code-Books) is Section II Parts A, B, C and D, Section V,
Section VIII Division 1, Section IX and CA-1, ASME's Conformity Assessment Requirements. Section II Part C and Sections
V and IX may be left out only if welding, brazing and fusing aren't in the shop's scope. Division 2 work (U2) needs
Division 2 instead. See [Section VIII](/asme/section-viii).

## Which books does a boiler shop need?

For the S certificate: Section I, Section II Parts A to D, Section V, Section IX, B31.1 Power Piping and CA-1. For an H
heating boiler certificate (except cast iron): Section II Parts A, B and C, Section IV, Section IX and CA-1; for cast
iron H, Section IV and CA-1. B31.1 is on the S list because boiler external piping is part of the Section I boiler.
See [Section I vs Section IV](/asme/section-i-vs-iv).

## Can a shop use photocopies or older books?

No. ASME's handbook says subscription to the required sections "is required for certification to assure that Code
users have the latest applicable Code rules", and that "Reproduced/unauthorized documents from any source are not
accepted for ASME Certification". Electronic editions from a licensed reseller are accepted. Each new edition becomes
mandatory six months after publication; see [ASME Code editions](/asme/editions).

## Which sections are not construction rules?

Sections VI and VII are recommended practice for owners caring for heating and power boilers: nothing is built or
stamped to them. Section XI is for in-service inspection of nuclear plants. In-service rules for other boilers and
vessels are in the National Board Inspection Code, not the BPVC; see [National Board registration](/asme/national-board-registration).

## How are ASME paragraph numbers read?

Each construction section uses letter prefixes for its parts. In Section VIII Division 1, UG paragraphs are general
requirements for every vessel, UW is welded construction, UCS is carbon and low-alloy steel and UHA is high-alloy
steel. Section I uses PG for general requirements and PW for welding; Section IX uses QW for welding. A reference such
as UG-27 therefore reads "Section VIII Division 1, general requirements, paragraph 27": the
[shell thickness rules](/asme/shell-thickness).

## How many shops hold ASME certificates?

ASME's certification page says "Today there are more than 6,800 Certificate Holders in the ASME BPVC Certification
Program", across power boilers, heating boilers, pressure vessels, plastic vessels, transport tanks and valves. Its
Certificate Holder Search lists them; see [ASME stamps](/asme/stamps).
