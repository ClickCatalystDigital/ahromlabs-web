## Construction sections and reference sections

The Code is easiest to read as two kinds of book. A **construction section** is the rulebook for one kind of equipment: what it may be made of, how it is designed, welded, examined, tested and stamped. A **reference section** holds rules that several construction sections point to, so they aren't written out again in each.

A pressure vessel shop working to Section VIII Division 1 therefore also works to Section II (materials), Section V (nondestructive examination), Section IX (welding) and Section XIII (relief devices), because Division 1 sends it there.

## The thirteen sections

| Section | Title, in short | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| I | Power boilers | Construction |
| II | Materials (Parts A to D) | Reference |
| III | Nuclear facility components | Construction |
| IV | Heating boilers | Construction |
| V | Nondestructive examination | Reference |
| VI | Care and operation of heating boilers | Recommended practice |
| VII | Care of power boilers | Recommended practice |
| VIII | Pressure vessels (Divisions 1, 2 and 3) | Construction |
| IX | Welding, brazing and fusing qualifications | Reference |
| X | Fiber-reinforced plastic pressure vessels | Construction |
| XI | Inservice inspection of nuclear plants | Inspection |
| XII | Transport tanks | Construction |
| XIII | Overpressure protection | Reference |

Sections VI and VII are recommendations for owners, not construction rules, and nothing is stamped to them. Section XI applies to nuclear plants in service.

## Which sections a shop needs

- **Pressure vessels**: [Section VIII](/asme/section-viii) (usually Division 1), plus II Part A or B for the material, II Part C if it buys filler metal to an SFA specification, II Part D for allowable stresses, V, [IX](/asme/section-ix) and XIII.
- **Power boilers** (steam above 15 psi (1 bar), hot water above 160 psi (11 bar) or 250 °F (121 °C)): Section I with the same reference sections. Below those limits, a heating boiler is built to Section IV; see [Section I vs Section IV](/asme/section-i-vs-iv).
- **Relief valves and rupture disks**: [Section XIII](/asme/section-xiii).

To hold an ASME certificate, a shop must own current copies of the sections its scope uses (ASME lists them per certificate as its "required code books"); photocopies are not accepted at the joint review.

## How the sections are numbered inside

Each construction section uses letter prefixes for its parts, which is why paragraph numbers look the way they do. In Section VIII Division 1, UG paragraphs are general requirements for every vessel, UW is welded construction, UCS is carbon and low-alloy steel and UHA is high-alloy steel. Section I uses PG for general requirements and PW for welding; Section IX uses QW for welding. A reference such as UG-27 therefore reads "Section VIII Division 1, general requirements, paragraph 27": the [shell thickness rules](/asme/shell-thickness).
