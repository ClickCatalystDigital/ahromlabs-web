## The two-year cycle

ASME revises the Code continuously in committee and publishes all the changes together every two years, in odd years. There are no addenda between editions any more: the cycle replaced them. Each edition becomes mandatory six months after it's published, which gives shops time to update procedures and their QC manual.

| Edition | Published | Mandatory |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 1 July 2023 | 1 January 2024 |
| 2025 | 1 July 2025 | 1 January 2026 |
| 2027 | Due July 2027 | Six months after |

The National Board applied the same date to data report forms: items built to the 2025 Code must use the 2025 forms, and reports on outdated forms are sent back. See [data reports](/asme/data-reports).

## Which edition applies to a vessel

The edition is fixed per job, not per day. In general, a vessel is built to the edition agreed for it (usually the one mandatory when the contract was signed), and the jurisdiction where it will be installed may require a particular one. Write the edition into the purchase order and the design basis, and make sure the QC manual tells staff how a job's edition is recorded.

Welding qualifications don't expire with an edition: a PQR or WPQ qualified under an earlier edition stays valid, and new qualifications follow the edition in force when they are made. The [Section IX guide](/asme/section-ix) covers the 2025 changes.

## Code cases

A **code case** is an approved alternative or addition published between editions: a new material, a new method, a relaxation in a defined situation. Using one is optional, needs the jurisdiction's acceptance, and must be recorded on the data report. Many are later written into the Code itself; some are annulled.

## Interpretations

An **interpretation** is ASME's written answer to a question about what the Code says. It isn't a change to the Code, and it doesn't approve a design; it tells you how the committee reads a paragraph. Interpretations are published by ASME and searchable online.

## Owning the Code

A certificate holder must own current editions of the sections its scope uses, bought from ASME or a licensed reseller. That's also why these guides cite paragraph numbers instead of reprinting them; see [ASME stamps](/asme/stamps) and [how to get the U stamp](/asme/u-stamp).
