## When does the ASME 2025 Edition become mandatory?

It became mandatory on 1 January 2026, six months after it was published on 1 July 2025. ASME's handbook states the
rule: "The edition becomes mandatory six months after the date of publication." The six months give shops time to
buy the new books and update procedures and their quality control manual.

## How often is the ASME Code published?

Every two years, in odd years, with all the changes made in committee since the last edition. There are no addenda
between editions any more; the two-year cycle replaced them.

| Edition | Published | Mandatory |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 1 July 2023 | 1 January 2024 |
| 2025 | 1 July 2025 | 1 January 2026 |
| 2027 | Due July 2027 | Six months after |

The National Board's inspection code (NBIC) follows the same rhythm: a new edition each July of an odd year.

## Do data report forms change with the edition?

Yes. The National Board says the 2025 revisions to the ASME and NBIC report forms became mandatory on 1 January
2026; items built to the 2025 Code must be documented on the current forms, and reports on outdated forms are sent
back. See [data reports](/asme/data-reports).

## Which edition applies to my vessel?

The edition agreed for the job, normally the one mandatory when the contract was signed, unless the jurisdiction where
it will be installed fixes another. The edition is fixed per job, not per day: write it into the purchase order and
the design basis, and make sure the quality control manual says how a job's edition is recorded.

## Do states require a specific edition?

Many do, and they lag. The National Board's 2026 Synopsis table of editions lists, for example: Ohio 2023, Illinois
2023, Michigan 2019, Pennsylvania 2010, Texas "Current" and Minnesota "Current". The rules themselves say:

- **Ohio** (OAC 4101:4-3-01, effective 1 July 2024) adopts the Code sections at the 2023 edition, "as well as
  amendments, supplements, and addenda subsequently published prior to issuance of the next edition".
- **Michigan** (R 339.5204, effective 3 May 2023) requires boilers, blowdown vessels and expansion tanks to be built to
  the 2019 edition.
- **Texas** (16 TAC §65.2) defines the ASME Code without a year, as the Code, code cases and interpretations adopted by
  ASME.
- **Pennsylvania** (34 Pa. Code §3a.1) still defines the ASME Code as the 2004 edition with its published cases and
  interpretations, older than the Synopsis entry.

Ask the jurisdiction's chief inspector before building to an edition newer than its rule names. See
[US and Canadian boiler laws](/asme/state-boiler-laws).

## Do I have to buy new books each edition?

Yes, if you hold an ASME certificate. ASME's FAQ answers "Yes, the latest edition/addenda becomes mandatory six months
after publication", and its handbook says reproduced or unauthorized copies aren't accepted. Electronic editions from
a licensed reseller are accepted. See [BPVC sections](/asme/bpvc-sections).

## Do welding qualifications expire with an edition?

No. A PQR or WPQ qualified under an earlier edition stays valid; new qualifications follow the edition in force when
they are made. The [Section IX guide](/asme/section-ix) covers the 2025 changes.

## What is a code case?

An approved alternative or addition published between editions: a new material, a new method, a relaxation in a
defined situation. ASME's [code cases page](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/publications-information/code-cases) says code cases "are effective immediately upon ASME approval and do not
expire", and are published in two books, Boiler and Pressure Vessels (CC-BPV) and Nuclear (CC-NUC), with seven
supplements through each two-year cycle. Using one is optional, must be recorded on the data report, and in many
states needs the jurisdiction's acceptance: the National Board's Synopsis has a code case column for each state.

## What is an interpretation?

ASME's written answer to a question about what the Code says. It isn't a change to the Code and doesn't approve a
design; it tells you how the committee reads a paragraph. Interpretations are published by ASME and searchable
online.
