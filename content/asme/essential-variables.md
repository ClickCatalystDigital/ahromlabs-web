## Why variables are classified

A procedure test proves one set of conditions. Section IX's variable tables (QW-250 onwards, one per process) say which conditions matter to the weld's mechanical properties and how far you can move from the test before you have to test again.

## The three kinds, for procedures

| Kind | If you change it beyond the PQR | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| Essential | Qualify a new PQR | Base metal P-number; filler metal F-number and A-number; thickness beyond the qualified range; adding or removing post-weld heat treatment; a drop in preheat of more than 100 °F (56 °C); a change of process; adding or removing shielding gas or a backing gas in some processes |
| Supplementary essential | New PQR only if the construction code requires impact testing | Group number within a P-number; heat input increase; single to multi-pass changes in some processes |
| Nonessential | Revise the WPS; no new test | Groove design, backing type, technique details, cleaning methods |

The exact list differs for each process (SMAW, GTAW, GMAW, SAW, FCAW), so always read the table for the process on your WPS.

## Thickness ranges

A groove weld PQR qualifies a range of base metal thickness and of weld metal thickness based on the coupon's thickness. As a general picture, a coupon of moderate thickness qualifies from a minimum up to twice its thickness, with thicker coupons qualifying up to the maximum allowed. Where impact testing applies, the lower limit changes too. Section IX's thickness table (QW-451) gives the numbers.

## For welders

Welders have their own essential variables (QW-350 onwards): process, position, backing, the F-number of the filler, and diameter and thickness ranges. Changing one beyond the WPQ means a new welder test. See [welder qualification](/asme/welder-qualification).

## Practical rules

- Keep a **PQR register** with each PQR's qualified ranges worked out once. Most WPS errors are ranges copied wrong.
- When a WPS cites two PQRs (for example to cover two thickness ranges), make sure every essential variable is covered by at least one.
- Impact-tested work is where supplementary essentials catch shops out. See [MDMT and impact testing](/asme/impact-testing).

The documents themselves are explained in [WPS vs PQR vs WPQ](/asme/wps-pqr-wpq), and the material grouping in [P-numbers, F-numbers and A-numbers](/asme/p-numbers).
