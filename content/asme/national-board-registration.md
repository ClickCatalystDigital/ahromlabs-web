## Who the National Board is

The National Board of Boiler and Pressure Vessel Inspectors is made up of the chief boiler inspectors of US states and Canadian provinces. ASME writes the construction code; the National Board commissions inspectors, keeps the registry of built items, and writes the code for equipment once it's in service, the **National Board Inspection Code (NBIC)**.

## Registration and the NB mark

Registration is the last step after design and construction and inspection: the manufacturer sends the signed [data report](/asme/data-reports) to the National Board, which keeps it on permanent record, and the nameplate carries the **NB** mark with a National Board number.

- A manufacturer becomes **authorized to register** by meeting the National Board's criteria (its document NB-264). Authorization is free; each registered item has a fee.
- Reports are filed on paper or electronically, within 30 days of certification, on current forms.
- Registration is required by most US jurisdictions for pressure equipment to be installed. Check the jurisdiction for the place of installation; see [US and Canadian boiler laws](/asme/state-boiler-laws).

For an owner, registration means a lost data report can always be replaced, which matters at every later inspection, repair or sale.

## The NBIC

The NBIC has four parts: installation, inspection, repairs and alterations, and pressure relief devices. It is the rulebook for the equipment's life after the ASME stamp.

## R, VR, NR and T/O

| Stamp | For |
|---|---|
| R | Repairs and alterations to boilers and pressure vessels |
| VR | Repair of pressure relief valves |
| NR | Repairs and replacements of nuclear components |
| T/O | Testing of pressure relief valves |

A company gets one by having its quality system manual evaluated and showing it works, much like an ASME [joint review](/asme/u-stamp). R-stamp work is accepted by a National Board commissioned inspector, and the holder keeps an agreement with an inspection agency. Repairs are recorded on NBIC forms (R-1 for a repair, R-2 for an alteration) and, for registered items, filed with the National Board too.
