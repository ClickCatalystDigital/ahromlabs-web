## How the Code becomes law

ASME is a standards body, not a regulator. The Code gets legal force when a **jurisdiction** (a state, a province, sometimes a city) adopts it in its boiler and pressure vessel law. The law then says which equipment is covered, which Code edition, who inspects it in service, and what has to be registered or permitted before it's operated.

What varies between jurisdictions:

- **Scope**: some regulate boilers and pressure vessels; some only boilers; many exempt small or low-pressure vessels.
- **Registration**: most US jurisdictions require the item's data report to be registered with the [National Board](/asme/national-board-registration) before installation.
- **Inspection in service**: by state inspectors or by insurance company inspectors holding National Board commissions, on set intervals.
- **Installation permits and operating certificates**.

## Who leads ASME reviews where

Jurisdictions also take part in certifying shops. ASME's handbook lists the states where the jurisdiction appoints the team leader for a [joint review](/asme/u-stamp) (Alaska, Delaware, Hawaii, Louisiana, Minnesota, Nebraska, Oklahoma, Texas and Washington), and says ASME appoints for Idaho, Wyoming, Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands and all Canadian provinces, and the National Board for the remaining states.

## Canada

In Canada each province or territory regulates pressure equipment, generally by adopting CSA B51, which in turn uses the ASME Code. A design is registered with the province before construction and gets a **Canadian Registration Number (CRN)**, and fittings need one too. A vessel stamped to ASME but without a CRN can't be installed.

## How to check a jurisdiction

1. Find the state or provincial boiler and pressure vessel office for the installation site; the National Board keeps a listing of every jurisdiction's requirements and contacts.
2. Confirm: is this equipment in scope, which edition, is NB registration required, what inspection before first use.
3. Put the answers in the purchase order, so the [data report](/asme/data-reports) and nameplate come out right the first time.

This page doesn't list each state's rules: they change, and the jurisdiction's own office is the source. For buying from a shop outside the US, see [buying from India](/asme/buying-from-india).
