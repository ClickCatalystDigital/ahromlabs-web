## Is the ASME Code required by law in the US?

Not federally: the Code gets legal force when a state, a city or a Canadian province adopts it in its own boiler and
pressure vessel rules. Pennsylvania's rule is typical in form: "The Department adopts and incorporates by reference
the following codes", with the ASME Code on the list. The adopting rule then says which equipment is covered, which
edition applies, who inspects it in service, and what must be stamped, registered or permitted before it runs.

The National Board adds its own caveat: "Synopsis data is subject to change", and users should consult the
jurisdiction's chief inspector. Most of these rules govern what may be installed and inspected; a new item built to a
later edition is often accepted, but confirm it in writing before you build. Code cases have their own column in the
Synopsis, with some states accepting them only "on a conditional basis".

## What do the state rules themselves say?

Five read in full on 6 November 2026:

- **Ohio**: OAC 4101:4-3-01 (effective 1 July 2024) adopts Sections I to XI and the code case book at the 2023
  edition, plus "amendments, supplements, and addenda subsequently published prior to issuance of the next edition".
- **Michigan**: R 339.5204 (effective 3 May 2023) requires "A boiler, blow down vessel, and expansion tank" to be built
  to the 2019 edition and B31.1 2018. Its scope names those items, not pressure vessels in general.
- **Texas**: 16 TAC §65.2 defines ASME as the Code, code cases and interpretations "adopted by the council of the
  society", with no year: effectively the current edition. The Texas rules are the Board of Boiler Rules', for boilers.
- **California**: 8 CCR §453 defines the ASME Code with no edition, and §454(a) requires new air tanks to be
  "constructed, inspected and stamped in compliance with the ASME Code" and, except UM vessels, registered with the
  National Board.
- **Pennsylvania**: 34 Pa. Code §3a.1 still defines the ASME Code as the 2004 edition with its cases and
  interpretations, older than the Synopsis entry, and §3a.21(b) requires the ASME stamp and the National Board
  registration number on every ASME boiler and unfired pressure vessel, plus a Pennsylvania keystone serial number.

## Which states don't require the U stamp?

Those that don't regulate unfired pressure vessels. The National Board's stamping table (2024 Synopsis) lists the ASME
stamps each jurisdiction requires: Florida, Louisiana, Montana, New Mexico, South Dakota and West Virginia list
boiler stamps without U; Idaho lists none; Wyoming lists only U, U2, U3 and UV. A buyer in those states may still
want the U stamp for insurance, resale or a plant standard.

## Which states require National Board registration?

Many, in different ways. The stamping table lists the NB mark as a requirement for Arizona, North Dakota, Washington
and West Virginia among others; Pennsylvania requires the National Board number on the stamping; California requires
registration of new air tanks. ASME's handbook says registration "may be required by the legal authority of a state
or municipality". See [National Board registration](/asme/national-board-registration).

## Do cities have their own boiler rules?

Some large ones do. The Synopsis lists separate entries for cities and counties including Chicago, Detroit, Los
Angeles, Miami and Miami-Dade, Milwaukee, New Orleans, New York City, Omaha, Seattle, Spokane, St. Louis and
Washington, D.C., several with their own editions; New York City's entry is the 1986 edition. Check the city as well
as the state.

## Who leads a shop's ASME joint review in each state?

ASME's handbook says the jurisdiction appoints the team leader in Alaska, Delaware, Hawaii, Louisiana, Minnesota,
Nebraska, Oklahoma, Texas and Washington; ASME appoints for Idaho, Wyoming, Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands and all
Canadian provinces; and the National Board appoints for the remaining states. See [how to get the U stamp](/asme/u-stamp).

## How does Canada regulate pressure equipment?

Province by province, with designs registered and given a Canadian Registration Number (CRN) under CSA B51. Alberta's
ABSA handles national applications for "Boilers, Pressure Vessels or Fittings to be Registered in Multiple Provincial
or Territorial Jurisdictions in Canada", and Ontario's TSSA guidelines for fittings (January 2026, citing CSA B51:24)
require that "The Canadian Registration Number (CRN) shall be identified whenever physically possible." The National
Board's tabulation shows most provinces requiring the ASME construction sections by law, with Québec the exception for
most sections. Register the design in each province where the equipment will go before it is built.

## How do I check a jurisdiction before ordering?

1. Find the jurisdiction's chief inspector on the National Board's [Synopsis page](https://www.nationalboard.org/ViewAllSynopses.aspx), which has an entry per state, province and city.
2. Confirm: is this equipment in scope, which edition, is NB registration required, are code cases accepted, what inspection or permit before first use.
3. Put the answers in the purchase order, so the [data report](/asme/data-reports) and nameplate come out right the first time.

The National Board's members are those chief inspectors, which is why its Synopsis is the starting point. For buying
from a shop outside the US, see [buying from India](/asme/buying-from-india).
