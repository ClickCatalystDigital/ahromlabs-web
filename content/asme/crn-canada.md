## What is a CRN?

A Canadian Registration Number (CRN) is the number a Canadian province or territory gives a pressure equipment
design once its safety authority has reviewed it: ABSA in Alberta, TSSA in Ontario and the equivalents elsewhere.
Registration is required by CSA B51, the Canadian boiler, pressure vessel and piping code that the provinces adopt;
CSA B51 in turn calls up the ASME Code for construction. The CRN registers the design. The ASME stamp and the
[Authorized Inspector](/asme/authorized-inspector) cover how one item was built.

## Does an ASME-stamped vessel need a CRN?

Yes, to be installed in Canada. The [U stamp](/asme/u-stamp) shows the vessel was built and inspected to the Code;
the CRN shows its design is registered in the province where it will operate. A US shop selling into Canada submits
the drawings and calculations for registration before shipping, and the CRN goes on the nameplate and the
[data report](/asme/data-reports). Building to ASME without a CRN means the vessel waits at the border of the
customer's permit, not at customs.

## How do you register in more than one province?

Submit once to the first jurisdiction, asking for the others you need. ABSA's national form lets the applicant tick
"All Jurisdictions (all of Canada)" or pick provinces, and notes that "ACI Central (www.acicrn.com) facilitates
registrations for PE, NS, NB, NL, NT, YT and NU." Each jurisdiction accepts the registration in turn, and the CRN
shows which have. For a one-off, site-specific vessel the form says: "To register a design in a single jurisdiction,
for example site specific installations, please contact the required jurisdiction directly."

## What do you submit for a CRN?

From ABSA's application form:

- the submitter, the registrant (the manufacturer, who "assumes full legal responsibility") and the billing client;
- the type of design (boiler, pressure vessel, heat exchanger or fitting) and the code and edition it is designed to,
  such as Section VIII Division 1 or B31.3;
- drawings or the fitting catalogue, technical specifications, and calculations or proof test reports;
- a "Copy of valid quality program certificate(s)", typically the ASME certificate;
- for fittings only, the signed statutory declaration and the scope of registration (products, sizes and variables);
- for an amendment or renewal, the original documents with the changes clearly marked.

Plan for the review in the schedule: a vessel can't be shipped to its Canadian site before the CRN is issued.

## How are fittings registered?

By category, under CSA B51's fitting categories A to H: pipe fittings, flanges, valves, expansion joints and hoses,
strainers and traps, measuring devices, pressure relief devices, and other. TSSA's guidelines say "Each category of
fitting manufactured shall be separately registered by the manufacturer with the AHJ" (the authority having
jurisdiction), and that the CRN "shall be identified whenever physically possible" on the fitting. Some provinces
exempt standard fittings: per ABSA's form, "BC, SK, MB and QC: standard fittings for categories A, B, and C".

## How long does a CRN last?

A vessel design registration stays valid for that design and code edition; a change to the design is an amendment.
Fitting registrations are renewed: ABSA's form offers renewal for fitting registrations only. Check the period with
the registering jurisdiction, and note it in the quality records so renewal isn't missed.

## What does a CRN number look like?

A letter-and-number code followed by a decimal suffix: the suffix records which provinces and territories have
accepted the registration, so the same design registered in more places shows more jurisdictions after the point.
Read the suffix before shipping: a CRN accepted in Alberta and Ontario does not cover a vessel going to Quebec until
Quebec has accepted it too.

## How long does registration take?

It depends on the jurisdiction's workload and how complete the submission is. A clean package (calculations that
follow the code paragraph by paragraph, drawings that match them, the code edition stated, and the quality program
certificate attached) moves fastest; most delays are questions from the reviewer. Build the review into the
schedule before material is ordered, and answer questions the same day.

## What documents does a reviewer usually question?

- Calculations that don't state the code edition and the paragraphs used.
- Material specifications on the drawing that differ from the calculation.
- Nozzle loads, flanges or relief device sizing not shown.
- Fittings in the design that don't have their own CRN in that province.
- Drawings without a revision, or a revision that differs from the calculation's.

## Do fittings in a vessel need their own CRN?

Often, yes. Valves, flanges, expansion joints and relief devices that are pressure-retaining fittings under CSA B51
need their own registration in the province of installation unless an exemption applies, as noted above for
standard fittings in some provinces. Ask suppliers for the fitting's CRN with the quotation, not after the vessel is
built.

## How should a shop track CRNs?

Against the design and the job: which drawing revision was registered, in which provinces, the CRN, and its expiry
for fittings. When a customer orders a vessel for Canada, the order should check the design's CRN covers the
installation province before material is bought. Our US boiler demo keeps each job's drawings, data report and
quality records together; CRN fields are something we'd add for a Canadian customer. See
