## What are the steps to get a U stamp?

A shop gets the U stamp by preparing its quality system, applying to ASME, and passing an on-site joint review; ASME
then issues a Certificate of Authorization for Section VIII, Division 1 pressure vessels and the Certification Mark
with the U designator. ASME's [certification process](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/asme-certification-process) and [informational handbook](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/cb8f63c5-3aa2-45ee-a2d1-b999d37733f7/bpv-gui-03-informational-handbook_1.pdf) set out the route. In the order a shop
usually does it:

1. **Decide the scope.** U for Division 1 vessels, shop only or field work too, and any limits. The scope drives everything else; see [ASME stamps](/asme/stamps).
2. **Buy the Code books.** ASME lists the books required per certificate (below). Copies and unauthorized reproductions aren't accepted.
3. **Sign an inspection agreement** with an ASME-accredited Authorized Inspection Agency. The agency's [Authorized Inspector](/asme/authorized-inspector) joins the review and then inspects every stamped job.
4. **Write the quality control manual** and train the people who will follow it. In English, covering every element the Code requires for the scope. See [the quality control manual](/asme/quality-control-manual).
5. **Apply** through ASME's CA Connect, return the signed ASME Accreditation/Certification Agreement, and pay the certificate fee, stamp fee and any deposit.
6. **ASME checks the file.** It asks your agency to confirm the inspection agreement and checks the application is complete; only then does it schedule the joint review.
7. **Prepare the demonstration.** Real Code work, a mock-up, or both, taken through your system from drawing to data report.
8. **The joint review.** A team leader (the ASME Designee) and a representative of your inspection agency audit the manual and watch the demonstration, then report to ASME with a recommendation.
9. **Certificate and stamp issued** once ASME accepts the report. Valid for three years.

## Which Code books does a U certificate need?

ASME's [required list](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/asme-certification-process/required-code-books) for the U certificate is Section II Parts A, B, C and D (materials), Section V
(nondestructive examination), Section VIII Division 1, Section IX (welding, brazing and fusing qualifications) and
CA-1, ASME's conformity assessment requirements. Section II Part C, Section V and Section IX can be left out only if
welding, brazing and fusing aren't in the shop's scope. Electronic editions bought from a licensed reseller are
accepted; a new edition becomes mandatory six months after it is published. See [BPVC sections](/asme/bpvc-sections)
and [Code editions](/asme/editions).

## Why do you need an Authorized Inspection Agency first?

ASME won't schedule the joint review until an accredited Authorized Inspection Agency confirms it has an inspection
agreement with the shop. The handbook's table of certificates marks the U certificate as requiring an agency
agreement. The agency is an organization accredited by ASME; its Authorized Inspector sits on the review
team, then inspects and signs off each stamped vessel, so the agreement covers the review and every job after it.
ASME publishes the [list of accredited agencies](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/Boiler-and-Pressure-Vessel-Certification), and its FAQ notes most work worldwide.

When comparing agencies, ask for the review fee, the hourly or per-visit rate for job inspections, travel charges,
and how often the Inspector will be in the shop at your volume.

## What goes in the quality control manual?

The manual is the shop's written description of how it meets each Code requirement in its scope: organization and
authority, drawings and design calculations, material receipt and control, welding, NDE, heat treatment,
calibration, examination and inspection, nonconformities, records and the Inspector's access. ASME's review teams
use a "General" checklist for the U certificate, and the shop must complete it, cross-referencing each element to
the paragraph of its manual that addresses it. The manual doesn't have to follow the checklist's order, but every
element must be findable. See [the quality control manual](/asme/quality-control-manual).

## What is the demonstration item?

The demonstration item is the work the shop puts through its quality system during the joint review to show the
manual is really used. ASME's FAQ says the demonstration must cover the administrative functions and the
manufacturing, fabrication and testing of the product, and that every element of the system must be demonstrated;
fabrication can be shown on current work, a mock-up built to current Code rules, or both. ASME gives no required
size or number of items.

Most shops choose a small vessel they can take all the way through: design calculations, material receipt with
[MTRs](/asme/mtr), [WPS and PQR](/asme/wps-pqr-wpq), welder qualifications, NDE, a
[hydrostatic test](/asme/hydrostatic-test) and a completed [data report](/asme/data-reports). Any Code work in the shop
during the review can be examined too.

## Who leads the joint review?

The ASME Designee who leads the review is appointed by ASME, by the jurisdiction, or by the National Board,
depending on where the shop is. The handbook says the jurisdiction appoints in Alaska, Delaware, Hawaii, Louisiana,
Minnesota, Nebraska, Oklahoma, Texas and Washington; ASME appoints in Idaho, Wyoming, Puerto Rico, the Virgin
Islands, all Canadian provinces, and wherever the jurisdiction is itself the inspection agency; the National Board
appoints in the other US states. Who appoints changes what
the shop pays for the review (below). See [US and Canadian boiler laws](/asme/state-boiler-laws).

## How much does the ASME U stamp cost?

ASME's own fee is $4,500 per certificate plus $400 per Certification Mark stamp (plus tax and shipping), from its
[Price Guide](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/asme-certification-process/price-guide) on 5 November 2026. Where ASME appoints the Designee, ASME also takes an $11,000 advance review deposit
that is applied to the cost of the review; the guide names reviews in Idaho, Wyoming, Puerto Rico and the Virgin
Islands, and any review where the state or province is itself the inspection agency. The shop pays the Designee's
travel and ASME's administrative fees from it, and the final invoice settles the difference either way. Where the
National Board or a state appoints, it tells the shop its own review charges. ASME wants all fees and the deposit
before it schedules the review. Prices are in US dollars and change; check the current guide.

| Item | Paid to | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| U certificate | ASME | $4,500 |
| Certification Mark (stamp), each | ASME | $400 plus tax and shipping |
| Advance review deposit, where ASME appoints the Designee | ASME | $11,000, applied to the review cost |
| Expedited joint review, if wanted | ASME | $2,750 |
| Manual or document review, if needed | ASME | $1,500 each |
| Joint review, where the National Board or a state appoints | National Board or the state | Set by them |
| Inspection agency: review attendance, then every job | The agency | Set by the agency |
| Code books | ASME or a licensed reseller | Per ASME's catalog |
| Manual writing, training, demonstration item, any consultant | Your own cost | |

For example, a shop in Ohio (National Board appoints) pays ASME $4,900 for one certificate and one stamp, and the
National Board and its inspection agency separately. A shop in Idaho (ASME appoints) pays ASME $15,900 before the
review is scheduled, $4,500 plus $400 plus the $11,000 deposit, and the final invoice refunds what the review and
travel don't use or bills any balance. Withdrawing after paying costs a $1,100 administrative fee, and cancelling
dates already accepted costs $2,200 or more.

## How long does it take to get a U stamp?

Plan on several months after the application, plus the time the shop needs to be ready. [ASME's FAQ](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/resources-and-events/frequently-asked-questions) says reviews are
generally scheduled about four months after it receives the completed application and fees; ASME emails proposed
dates, which must be accepted in CA Connect within seven business days, and sends review details four to six weeks
before. The certificate is issued after ASME approves the team leader's report.

The rest of the pace is set by the shop: writing a manual that matches how it really works, signing the agency
agreement, and getting a demonstration item through every step. Apply when the manual is done and the demonstration
job is planned, and book the review only when a full job can be shown end to end.

## How do you renew a U stamp?

The certificate lasts three years, and renewal is another joint review. ASME sends a reminder ten months before
expiry, and the renewal application, fees and any deposit must reach ASME at least six months before the expiry
date, or the certificate may lapse. A shop with work in progress at expiry can ask for one six-month extension to
finish the listed work: the request must reach ASME 30 days before expiry, with a $2,200 fee per certificate and a
report from the inspection agency.

## What should a shop's system keep for the review?

Whatever runs the shop, check that it can show the review team, for the demonstration job and every job after:

- The drawing and calculation revision used, and who approved it.
- Each material receipt with its MTR, heat number and the parts it went into; see [MTRs](/asme/mtr).
- The WPS used on each weld, the welder's stamp and their qualification and [continuity](/asme/welder-continuity).
- NDE, heat treatment and test records, linked to the job.
- Nonconformities and how each was resolved, with the Inspector's acceptance.
- Hold points the Inspector signed, and the data report.

## Can a shop in India get the U stamp?

Yes. The route is the same for a shop in India, and many Indian fabricators hold U and S certificates for export
work. IBR 2026 lets an Indian Inspecting Authority inspect an export boiler to a foreign code; see
[buying ASME equipment from India](/asme/buying-from-india) and [ASME vs IBR 2026](/asme/asme-vs-ibr).
