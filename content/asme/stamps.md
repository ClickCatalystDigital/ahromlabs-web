## What do the ASME stamp letters mean?

The letters are the **designator** stamped under the ASME Certification Mark (the cloverleaf), and they say which
Code section and what kind of work the item was built to: U for a Section VIII Division 1 vessel, S for a Section I
power boiler, H for a heating boiler, and so on. ASME's handbook says the mark "can only be used by a company that
holds a valid Certificate of Authorization", so the letters also tell you the maker held that certificate. People
still say "U stamp" for the mark with the U designator, and this guide does too.

## What happened to the old Code symbol stamps?

ASME replaced its 28 separate Code symbol stamps with one Certification Mark used together with a designator. Its
[FAQ](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/resources-and-events/frequently-asked-questions) says the old stamps for Sections I, IV, VIII, X and XII were discontinued on 31 January 2013 and must be
returned to ASME, and that the designator must appear directly below the mark, though it may be printed rather than
stamped. An older nameplate with a U inside the cloverleaf means the same as the new mark with U below it.

## Which ASME certificates and designators are there?

ASME's [certification page](https://www.asme.org/certification-accreditation/Boiler-and-Pressure-Vessel-Certification) lists these for the boiler and pressure vessel Code; how long each lasts and whether
it needs an agreement with an Authorized Inspection Agency are from ASME's handbook (Exhibit A):

| Section | Designator | For | Valid | Inspection agency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | S | Power boilers | 3 years | Yes |
| I | A | Assembly of power boilers | 3 years | Yes |
| I | E | Electric boilers | 3 years | Yes |
| I | M | Miniature boilers | 3 years | Yes |
| I | PP | Pressure piping (boiler external piping) | 3 years | Yes |
| IV | H | Heating boilers (not cast iron or aluminum) | 3 years | Yes |
| IV | H | Cast iron and cast aluminum heating boilers | 1 year | No |
| IV | HLW | Lined potable water heaters | 3 years | Yes |
| VIII-1 | U | Pressure vessels | 3 years | Yes |
| VIII-1 | UM | Miniature pressure vessels (needs a U or S too) | 1 year | Through the U or S |
| VIII-2 | U2 | Pressure vessels, alternative rules | 3 years | Yes |
| VIII-3 | U3 | High-pressure vessels | 3 years | Yes |
| X | RP | Fiber-reinforced plastic vessels | 3 years | Yes |
| XII | T | Transport tanks | 3 years | Yes |
| I, IV, VIII-1, VIII-2, XII | PRT | Parts, without design responsibility | 3 years | Yes |
| XIII | V, HV, UV, UD, UV3, UD3, TV, TD | Relief valves and other pressure relief devices | 3 years | No |

Every certificate also needs the Code books ASME lists for it; since the CA-1 standard (ASME's Conformity Assessment
Requirements) appears on every list, a shop buys that too. See [how to get the U stamp](/asme/u-stamp).

## What is the difference between the U and U2 stamp?

U is for vessels built to Section VIII Division 1 and U2 for Division 2, the alternative rules with a smaller design
margin and more analysis. They are separate certificates with separate joint reviews, and a U holder can't stamp a
Division 2 vessel. See [Section VIII Division 1 vs 2](/asme/section-viii).

## What is a PRT certificate?

PRT is for a maker of parts who builds to someone else's design: in the handbook's words, for "Manufacturers of parts
who do not perform or assume any design responsibility for the parts they manufacture." The certificate holder who
buys the part takes design responsibility, and the part comes with a partial data report.

## What are the UM, V and UV stamps?

UM is for miniature pressure vessels and can only be held alongside a valid U or S certificate; it is issued for one
year. The Section XIII letters are for pressure relief devices: V for boiler relief valves, HV for heating boiler
safety valves, UV for pressure vessel relief valves, UD for nonreclosing devices such as rupture disks, UV3 and UD3
for high-pressure vessels, TV and TD for transport tanks. Their reviews are run through the National Board and their
capacities certified by test; see [Section XIII](/asme/section-xiii).

## What is the R stamp?

The R stamp is not ASME's: it is the National Board's certificate for repairs and alterations to boilers, pressure
vessels and other pressure-retaining items already in service, done under the National Board Inspection Code (NBIC).
The National Board's [R certificate page](https://www.nationalboard.org/Index.aspx?pageID=115&ID=160) says a holder must keep an inspection agreement with an Authorized
Inspection Agency, a written quality system to the NBIC and the current NBIC, and that the certificate costs $1,150
for three years. Its sister programs are:

- **VR**: repair of pressure relief valves;
- **NR**: repair and replacement of nuclear components;
- **T/O**: in-service testing of pressure relief valves.

The National Board's [accreditation page](https://www.nationalboard.org/Index.aspx?pageID=115) notes that authorized repair organizations are issued an NBBI stamp applied
to the nameplate.

## What does NB on a nameplate mean?

NB means the item's data report is registered with the National Board, which keeps it permanently; the nameplate
carries the NB mark and a National Board number. The National Board's [registration page](https://www.nationalboard.org/Index.aspx?pageID=9) says the NB mark is
used by manufacturers to identify items registered with it. Many US jurisdictions require registration; see
[National Board registration](/asme/national-board-registration).

## Which stamps does my state require?

Each state sets its own list. The National Board's [stamping table](https://www.nationalboard.org/SiteDocuments/Synopsis/asme_requirements.pdf) (2024 Synopsis) gives, per jurisdiction, the
National Board and ASME stamps it requires. Most states list all ASME stamps or a long list that includes U and S;
Florida, Louisiana and Montana list boiler stamps without U; Wyoming lists only U, U2, U3 and UV; Idaho lists none.
NB registration is listed for Arizona, North Dakota, Washington and West Virginia, among others. Confirm with the
state's chief inspector; see [US and Canadian boiler laws](/asme/state-boiler-laws).

## How do I check that a company really holds a stamp?

Use ASME's [Certificate Holder Search](https://access.asme.org/caconnect/certified-companies), which ASME's FAQ points to for exactly this: it lists certified companies
with their certificates, filterable by country and certificate. Ask the supplier for a copy of the certificate too,
and check the designator, scope (shop or field) and expiry date cover your order. For repair firms, the National
Board's Manufacturer and Repair Directory lists R, VR and NR holders.

## What does a stamp tell a buyer?

A stamp says the item was built under an ASME-accepted quality system, inspected by an
[Authorized Inspector](/asme/authorized-inspector), and documented on a [data report](/asme/data-reports). It doesn't
say the design suits your process: that is set by the design conditions you specify in the order.
