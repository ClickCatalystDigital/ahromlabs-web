## What is the difference between ASME B31.1 and B31.3?

B31.1 is for power piping and B31.3 for process piping. ASME describes B31.1 as covering "piping systems typically
found in electric power generating stations, industrial plants, geothermal heating systems, and heating and cooling
systems", and B31.3 as setting "process piping requirements typically found in petroleum, chemical, pharmaceutical,
hydrogen, paper & pulp, and power generation industries". Both are ASME standards outside the Boiler and Pressure
Vessel Code, and both are paid; this guide cites their paragraphs.

| | B31.1 Power Piping | B31.3 Process Piping |
|---|---|---|
| Current edition | 2026 | 2024 |
| Typical plants | Power stations, industrial steam, district heating and cooling | Refineries, chemical, petrochemical, pharmaceutical, hydrogen, food |
| Special scope | Boiler external piping (BEP) | Fluid service categories: Normal, Category D, Category M, High Pressure, High Purity |
| Hazard approach | One set of rules, conservative | Rules scale with the fluid's hazard |
| Hydrostatic test | 1.5 × design pressure | 1.5 × design pressure, adjusted by the stress ratio at temperature |
| Welding qualification | Section IX | Section IX, with B31.3's own additions |
| Certification | BEP by a Section I certificate holder; CC-1 and CC-2 certificates of compliance | None |

## What are the current editions of B31.1 and B31.3?

B31.1-2026 and B31.3-2024, the latest editions ASME lists on its store as of 6 November 2026. Piping codes have their
own cycles, separate from the BPVC's two-year cycle, so check which edition your contract or jurisdiction names; Ohio's
rule, for example, adopts B31.1 at 2022 and Michigan's at 2018.

## What is boiler external piping?

The piping from a power boiler out to and including the valves Section I requires. Texas's boiler rules define it as
starting where the Section I or Section VIII boiler proper ends (at the first circumferential joint, flange face or
threaded joint) "and which extends up to and including the valve or valves required by ASME". It is designed to B31.1
but is part of the Section I boiler: built by a holder of a Section I certificate (S, A or PP) and inspected by the
[Authorized Inspector](/asme/authorized-inspector). That is why ASME lists B31.1 among the required books for the S and
PP certificates. See [Section I vs Section IV](/asme/section-i-vs-iv).

## Is there an ASME form for B31.1 piping?

Yes: ASME publishes two certificates of compliance for B31.1, CC-1 for the complete piping system and CC-2 for
fabricated components, subassemblies and assemblies, free on its [data report forms page](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/publications-information/asme-data-report-forms). B31.3 has no
equivalent ASME form; the owner's records and the examination and test reports carry the evidence.

## What are B31.3 fluid service categories?

- **Normal**: most services.
- **Category D**: non-flammable, non-toxic fluids at low pressure and moderate temperature; lighter rules.
- **Category M**: fluids where a single small exposure can cause serious harm; stricter rules.
- **High Pressure** and **High Purity**: chapters of their own.

The owner assigns the category, and with it the examination, testing and design rules that apply.

## What hydrostatic test does each require?

B31.1: at least 1.5 × design pressure (paragraph 137). B31.3: at least 1.5 × design pressure, multiplied by the ratio
of allowable stress at test temperature to that at design temperature (paragraph 345.4.2), the same logic as a
Division 1 vessel's stress ratio. See the [hydrostatic test guide](/asme/hydrostatic-test).

## Do B31.1 and B31.3 use Section IX?

Yes. Welding procedures and welders are qualified to [Section IX](/asme/section-ix), so a shop's
[WPSs and PQRs](/asme/wps-pqr-wpq) can serve its vessel and piping work where the variables fit both codes, and the
same continuity rule (QW-322) applies to its welders.

## Where do piping NDE and materials come from?

NDE methods from [Section V](/asme/section-v), with the piping code setting how much and the acceptance criteria;
materials are mostly ASME or ASTM specifications, see [Section II](/asme/section-ii).
