## Can an Indian manufacturer build an ASME U-stamped pressure vessel?

Yes. The Code and the certificate are the same wherever the shop is: an Indian fabricator with a U certificate builds
Section VIII Division 1 vessels to the same rules, under the same quality control system review and with an
Authorized Inspector from an ASME-accredited agency, as a shop in Texas. ASME's FAQ notes there are now 74 countries
in which companies have been certified, and many Indian fabricators hold U, U2 and S certificates for export work.

## How do I check an Indian shop's ASME certificate?

On ASME's [Certificate Holder Search](https://access.asme.org/caconnect/certified-companies), which ASME's FAQ points to for exactly this and which filters by
country and certificate. Ask the supplier for a copy of the certificate too, and confirm:

- the designator covers the work (U for Division 1, U2 for Division 2, S for power boilers, PP for boiler external piping);
- the scope (shop only, or field work too) and any limits;
- the expiry date is after your job finishes, or that renewal is in hand.

See [ASME stamps](/asme/stamps).

## Who is the Authorized Inspector for a shop in India?

An inspector employed by the ASME-accredited inspection agency the shop has its agreement with; ASME's FAQ says most
agencies operate worldwide. Ask which agency it is: their [Authorized Inspector](/asme/authorized-inspector) witnesses
the hydro test and signs your [data report](/asme/data-reports).

## Can an Indian shop register the vessel with the National Board?

Yes, if it is authorized to register. The National Board says any manufacturer meeting NB-264 may become authorized,
and NB-264 then requires registration forms to be sent within 30 days of the Inspector's certification. If your state
requires National Board registration, make authorization a condition of the order. See
[National Board registration](/asme/national-board-registration).

## Which US rules apply to an imported vessel?

The rules of the state, and sometimes the city, where it will be installed: the Code edition the jurisdiction names,
whether it regulates unfired vessels at all, National Board registration, and inspection before first use.
Pennsylvania, for example, requires the ASME stamp, the National Board number and its own keystone serial number on
every new boiler and unfired vessel. Put the jurisdiction's answers in the purchase order; see
[US and Canadian boiler laws](/asme/state-boiler-laws).

## What should I check before ordering?

| Check | How |
|---|---|
| The certificate covers the work | Designator, scope and expiry, on ASME's Certificate Holder Search and a copy from the shop |
| Who the inspection agency is | The agency named on their agreement; its Inspector signs your data report |
| National Board registration | Required by your state? Then the shop must be authorized to register (NB-264) |
| The Code edition | Name it in the order; see [editions](/asme/editions) |
| Your jurisdiction's own rules | Edition, scope, stamping and registration; see [state boiler laws](/asme/state-boiler-laws) |
| Materials | SA specifications with mill test reports by heat number; see [MTRs](/asme/mtr) |
| Documents | The full package in English: calculations, drawings, MTRs, WPS/PQR/WPQ, welder continuity, NDE, PWHT charts, test record, data report |

## Will the documents be in English?

They must be, for the ASME side. ASME's handbook requires a certificate holder's quality control documentation to be
in English, and the data report, calculations and records the Inspector reviews follow it. Ask for the data report
package as a draft before the vessel ships, not with it.

## What does IBR add for a boiler built in India?

A step at home. Regulation 4 of IBR 2026 lets an Indian Inspecting Authority inspect a boiler meant for export,
through its stages of construction, against a foreign code such as ASME, and certify it in Form I Part A. That
certificate is for India's records: it doesn't replace the ASME stamp or the Authorized Inspector. See
[ASME vs IBR 2026](/asme/asme-vs-ibr) and [IBR Form I Part A](/ibr/forms#form-i-part-a).

## How should I manage the order?

- **Inspection and test plan**: agree the hold points (material review, fit-up, NDE, PWHT, hydrostatic test) and who witnesses each, yours and the Inspector's.
- **Document review before shipping**: get the data report package as a draft before the vessel ships.
- **Logistics**: plan oversized vessels' shipping early; the data report and nameplate must match what arrives.
