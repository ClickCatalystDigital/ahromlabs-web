## Which steam pipes come under IBR?

Steam pipes and their fittings connected to a boiler registered under the Boilers Act, together with the mountings
and fittings on them. They are designed, made, inspected and approved under IBR's standard conditions for steam
pipes, and approved for a pressure and temperature like the boiler itself.

## When are the plans submitted?

Twice: "Plans of steam pipes including mountings and fittings shall be submitted with the prescribed fee to the
Inspecting Authority before construction and to the Chief Inspector at the time of registration of the boiler for his
decision whether the pipes and their arrangement comply with the regulations." The plans carry the layout, pipe
sizes and thicknesses, materials, supports, drains and fittings. The fee for examining them is set in regulation 323
by the size of the work.

## How are the pipes inspected before use?

"Pipes shall be inspected and hydraulically tested before use in place", at the test pressure in the standard
conditions for steam pipes. A certificate from the Inspecting Authority in [Form IV Part B](/ibr/forms/form-iv-part-b)
that this was done may be accepted; pipes made at a well-known pipe maker's works carry their own certificate. If the
Competent Person is satisfied, the pipes are erected, and "The Chief Inspector shall intimate his approval to the
owner regarding the suitability of the pipe line for use at the pressure and temperature to be specified."

## What is recorded once the line is approved?

A memorandum of inspection book for the steam pipe and fittings, in which the Inspector enters every particular and
dimension with the calculations, the hydraulic test and inspection notes; later inspections, repairs, replacements and
any accident are entered too. The Chief Inspector checks it and approves the maximum allowable working pressure and
temperature for each part, and "The pressure and temperature once approved for the particular steam pipe line shall
not be altered except on a written authority from the Chief Inspector."

## Is there a Form XIV in IBR 2026?

Regulation 322(3)(a) says "A memorandum of Inspection Book shall be prepared in respect of steam pipe and fittings in
Form-XIV", but the forms printed in the 2026 Gazette run from Form XIII to Form XV with no Form XIV. Under IBR 1950,
Form XIV was the steam pipe form. Until a correction is notified, ask your Inspecting Authority or Chief Inspector
which format they accept. See the [IBR 2026 forms list](/ibr/forms).

## How are steam pipes inspected later?

At a boiler inspection, or at any time, the Competent Person may inspect the steam pipes externally and have lagging
removed at the flanges. If the pipes look unsatisfactory, the Chief Inspector may have all the lagging removed and
order a hydraulic test of "not less than one and half times, the design pressure of the boiler or the maximum
allowable working pressure of pipe as the case may be".

## How does this compare with ASME piping?

In the US the equivalent split is ASME Section I for boiler external piping and B31.1 for power piping beyond it; see
[B31.1 vs B31.3](/asme/b31-1-vs-b31-3). Under IBR, the pipe line has its own approval and book, kept with the boiler's
records; see [stage inspection](/ibr/stage-inspection) and [certificate renewal](/ibr/renewal-inspection).
