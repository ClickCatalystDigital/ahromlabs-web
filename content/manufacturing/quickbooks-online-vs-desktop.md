## The short comparison

From Intuit's own pages, read on 3 November 2026:

| | QuickBooks Online Plus / Advanced | QuickBooks Desktop Enterprise |
|---|---|---|
| Where it runs | Browser, from anywhere | Installed on your PCs or hosted |
| Pitched for | Advanced: companies "needing industry workflows" and working from anywhere | Complex inventory, "like manufacturers and wholesalers" |
| Users | By plan | Up to 40 |
| Inventory | Quantity, FIFO or average cost, reorder points (Plus and Advanced) | Plus location tracking down to the bin or pallet with Advanced Inventory (Platinum and Diamond) |
| Assemblies and BOMs | Not in Online; Intuit lists BOM management for Advanced as coming soon | Assemblies and builds, with subassemblies |
| Manufacturing orders | In Intuit Enterprise Suite, not Online | Desktop's own assembly builds |

Details of each are in [QuickBooks Online inventory](/manufacturing/quickbooks-online-inventory) and
[does QuickBooks work for manufacturing](/manufacturing/quickbooks-for-manufacturing).

## When Online is the better choice

- People need the books from more than one place, and nobody wants to look after a server.
- You resell finished goods and don't build assemblies.
- You're coming off an unsupported Desktop version anyway (see
  [Desktop 2023 end of support](/manufacturing/quickbooks-desktop-end-of-support)).

## When Desktop Enterprise is still the better choice

- You build assemblies from components today and need it to keep working.
- You track stock by location or bin in QuickBooks itself.
- You have large lists or many users at once.

## The third option

Many shops don't need QuickBooks to do inventory at all: they keep QuickBooks Online for the books and run stock,
jobs and the floor in a system built for that, which sends QuickBooks the accounting result. Then the Online vs
Desktop choice is only about accounting. See [connecting QuickBooks to a shop system](/manufacturing/quickbooks-integration).
