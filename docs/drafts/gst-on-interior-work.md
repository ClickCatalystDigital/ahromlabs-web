## Draft page text

### Which GST applies to an interior job

An interior job usually carries three different supplies, and a BOQ should keep them on separate lines because
they can be taxed differently:

1. **The design fee**: a professional service. *(Rate and SAC to confirm: understood as SAC 998391, 18%.)*
2. **Fixed work on the building**: false ceilings, flooring, built-in carpentry, wiring. The CGST Act defines a
   works contract as a contract for building, construction, fabrication, completion, erection, installation,
   fitting out, improvement, modification, repair, maintenance, renovation, alteration or commissioning of any
   immovable property where property in goods passes in the course of the contract (section 2(119)).
   *(Rate to confirm: understood as 18% under heading 9954.)*
3. **Loose furniture supplied and installed**: chairs, freestanding units. If it can be removed without damage it
   may be a supply of goods with installation rather than a works contract. *(To confirm: rate for the furniture
   heading after the September 2025 rate changes; and how the CA treats modular kitchens and wardrobes fixed to
   walls.)*

### Works contract or goods: the modular kitchen question

Whether a modular kitchen or wardrobe is a works contract or a supply of goods turns on whether it becomes part of
the building. A unit screwed to the wall that comes off without damage points one way; one built into a niche
and finished with the walls points the other. Advance rulings have gone both ways on furniture with installation,
and an advance ruling binds only the business that asked for it. *(To confirm: which rulings the CA relies on;
the Madhya Pradesh ruling for Methodex (2020) was reported as treating installed furniture as goods, but its text
has not been read for this draft.)*

### CGST and SGST, or IGST

For services directly related to immovable property, the IGST Act places the supply where the property is, and it
names interior decorators, architects and engineers among them (section 12(3)(a)). So a studio registered in
Gujarat charges CGST + SGST on a site in Gujarat and IGST on a site in Maharashtra, whatever the client's
address. *(Checked against secondary copies of the Act; confirm against the official text.)*

### The client's input tax credit

A business client fitting out its own office generally can't take credit for the GST on it: section 17(5)(c)
blocks credit on works contract services for construction of immovable property (other than plant and machinery),
and 17(5)(d) blocks goods or services used to construct immovable property on the client's own account; the
explanation counts renovation, additions, alterations and repairs as construction to the extent they are
capitalised. *(Read from CBIC's CGST Act text updated to 30 Sept 2020. Clause (d) was amended retrospectively
by the Finance Act 2025 after the Supreme Court's Safari Retreats judgment; confirm the current wording.)*

### What this means for the BOQ and the quotation

- Keep the design fee, fixed works and loose furniture on separate lines, with their own SAC/HSN and rate.
- Record where the site is: it decides CGST + SGST or IGST, not where the client lives.
- Tell a business client up front that credit on fit-out work is usually blocked; it changes their real cost.

### Boundary

Not tax advice. Rates change by notification; check the current notification on the CBIC site, and settle the
treatment of your own contracts with your CA.
