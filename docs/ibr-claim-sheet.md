## Review, 17 Oct 2026 (Claude, against the Gazette, the 1950 forms index and the 1950 book scan)

Every item in section B was checked against the regulation or form template it describes. Corrected:

1. Form IX: was described as a general periodic-inspection form; it is for power-generation boilers and waste heat boilers in continuous process plants (regs 295(7)–(8), Appendices H and I).
2. Form XII: added the date and pressure of the last hydraulic test (reg 310(2)); removed "kept by the owner", which the text does not say.
3. Form I Part B, what changed: the 1950 form already used kg/cm² (g); the change is design pressure on the stamp where 1950 showed W.P.
4. Form V Part A, what changed: the signed-extract proviso existed under 1950 too; moved to "when filed". Tons to kg/mm² confirmed from the 1950 Form IV.
5. Form V Part B and Form XXII: also signed by the Competent Person and the Inspecting Authority.
6. Form I Parts C, D, E; IV Parts A, B, C; X: contents rewritten from the form templates.
7. Forms I Part A, II, XIII, XX: summaries tightened to the text.
8. Guides: an unchecked "same path as 1950" comparison removed; creep-range rule scoped to the furnace and superheater zone; a "where originals come from" sentence removed; "when a Chief Inspector asks" replaced with reg 8(d)(ii); steam test (reg 300(4)) added to registration.
9. Infographics: registration image redone (registry plate GT over 1234, steam test step); record-keeping image owner text corrected to reg 308(2)–(3). All four images carry alt text repeating every label.

Quotes: 143/143 found in the official text.

Judgement calls left for Pujan: Shanti is named on every IBR page ("Keeping a boiler folder without retyping it"), and Form IV Part B notes that Shanti's "III A" sheet is a pipe table.

## B. Written in our own words (checked by Claude, 17 Oct 2026)

### Form pages (src/lib/ibr-forms.ts)

- [x] **Form I Part A** (reg 4; was Form II-A): The Inspecting Authority's certificate of inspection during construction for a boiler made to a foreign code for export.
- [x] **Form I Part B** (reg 8(a); was Form II(1)): The Inspecting Authority's certificate that a shop-assembled boiler was built under its supervision, inspected at each stage and hydrostatically tested.
  - Issued/signed by: The Inspecting Authority, signed by its Competent Person and the Inspecting Authority, with date and seal.
  - Filed: Goes to the Chief Inspector with the registration application, alongside Form IV Part A, the drawings and the material certificates (regulation 8).
  - Records: The boiler type, maker and maker's number; That it was built under supervision and inspected at stages by the Competent Person; The stamp on the shell plate: maker's name and number, year of make, test pressure and date, design pressure in kg/cm² (g); The hydrostatic test pressure and the date it was witnessed; That welded seams passed destructive and non-destructive examination where applicable; The maker's drawing number, and that Form IV Part A was countersigned
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form II(1). The wording is nearly the same; the stamp now shows the design pressure where the 1950 form showed the working pressure (W.P.), and the form refers to the Boilers Act, 2025 and to Form IV Part A instead of Form III.
- [x] **Form I Part C** (reg 8(a); was Form II(2)): The Inspecting Authority's certificate for the parts of a site-assembled boiler made and inspected at the maker's works.
  - Issued/signed by: The Inspecting Authority, signed by its Competent Person and the Inspecting Authority.
  - Filed: Goes with the registration application in place of Form I Part B when the boiler is assembled at site (regulation 8). Form I Part E follows once erection is complete.
  - Records: The boiler, maker and maker's number, and that it was built under supervision and inspected at stages by the Competent Person; Each component's name, drawing number and stamping details; Test pressure and design pressure in kg/cm² (g); That material samples were tested in the Competent Person's presence, and welded seams passed destructive and non-destructive examination; The maker's drawing number, and that Form IV Part A was countersigned
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form II(2).
- [x] **Form I Part D** (reg 8(a)(iv); was Form II-B): Replaces Form I Part B or C for a boiler built with variations from the standard conditions that the Board or the Inspecting Authority permitted.
  - Issued/signed by: The Inspecting Authority.
  - Filed: Filed in place of Form I Part B or Part C (regulation 8(a)(iv)).
  - Records: The boiler type, length, diameter, design pressure, maker and shop number; That it was built under supervision, in accordance with the permitted variations from the standard conditions; The stamp on the front end plate; The water pressure test of the drum and header; Material and weld tests, struck out where a Well-known steel maker's Form V Part A is furnished instead; The maker's drawing number, and that Form IV Part A was countersigned
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form II-B.
- [x] **Form I Part E** (reg 8(a)(v); was Form II-C): The Inspecting Authority's certificate once a boiler is erected at site and passes its hydrostatic test there.
  - Issued/signed by: The Inspecting Authority, signed by its Competent Person and the Inspecting Authority.
  - Filed: Issued after erection and the site hydrostatic test, for boilers assembled at site (regulation 8(a)(v)).
  - Records: The boiler type, design pressure, maker and maker's number; That it was erected under supervision and inspected at stages of erection by the Competent Person; That welded seams had post-weld heat treatment and non-destructive examination where applicable; The water pressure test after erection, its pressure and date
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form II-C.
- [x] **Form II** (reg 7(1)(b)(iii) and 541; was Form XIII): The certificate a welder must hold to weld boilers, boiler components, economisers, feed pipes, steam pipes and superheaters (regulation 7(1)(b)(iii)).
- [x] **Form III** (reg 7(1)(b)(v); new in 2026): The questionnaire a firm answers to be recognised as a manufacturer of boilers or boiler components. New in 2026; recognition lasts three years.
- [x] **Form IV Part A** (reg 8(b); was Form III): The boiler maker's own certificate describing the boiler, its parts, materials, construction, mountings and tests.
  - Issued/signed by: The maker of the boiler or its representative; the Inspecting Authority countersigns it.
  - Filed: Goes with the registration application, with Form I Part B or C, the drawings, and Form V Part B listing every material certificate used (regulation 8).
  - Records: Description: constructor, contract, type of boiler, length and diameter, design pressure, maker's number and year of make, heating surface, evaporation capacity, steam temperatures; Parts made at the constructor's works, and parts made outside it, with drawing numbers and makers; Construction: the chapter it complies with, numbers of longitudinal and circumferential seams, weld repairs, heat treatment, radiography; Drums and shells; headers and boxes; Mountings: main and auxiliary stop valves, safety valves, blow-down valves, feed check valves; Safety valve details and test results; The maker's certification that the listed parts were used, backed by the makers' certificates
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form III. Under IBR 2026 the extract of all material test certificates (Form V Part B) is filed with it.
- [x] **Form IV Part B** (reg 10; was Form III-A): The certificate of manufacture and test for steam and feed pipes.
  - Issued/signed by: The pipe maker and the Inspecting Authority. Well-known pipe makers may issue it online with a digital signature.
  - Filed: Kept with the boiler's records by the Inspecting Authority, the owner and the manufacturer (regulation 308).
  - Records: The part and quantity, drawing number, maker and customer; The steel: process of manufacture, fully killed or rimmed, chemical composition, heat number, size, test certificate number and date, steel maker; The pipe: process of manufacture, main dimensions, tolerances, specification; Tests: bend test on pipe or weld, flattening, tensile strength, chemical composition, heat treatment, hydraulic test in kg/cm² (g); The identification mark of the Inspecting Authority or well-known pipe maker
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form III-A, which is the name many folders still use.
- [x] **Form IV Part C** (reg 11; was Form III-B): The certificate of manufacture and test for boiler tubes.
  - Issued/signed by: The tube maker and the Inspecting Authority. Well-known tube makers may issue it electronically with a digital signature.
  - Filed: Kept with the boiler's records by the Inspecting Authority, the owner and the manufacturer (regulation 308).
  - Records: The part and quantity, drawing number, maker and customer; The steel: process of manufacture, fully killed or rimmed, chemical composition, heat number, size, test certificate number and date, steel maker; The tube: process of manufacture, main dimensions, tolerances, specification; Tests: tensile strength, elongation, bend, flattening, crushing, flare and flange tests, heat treatment, hydraulic test in kg/cm² (g)
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form III-B.
- [x] **Form IV Part D** (reg 12; was Form III-C): The certificate for boiler mountings and fittings such as valves.
- [x] **Form IV Part E** (reg 13 and 50 to 57; was Form III-F): The certificate for castings used in a boiler.
- [x] **Form IV Part F** (reg 14 and 58 to 62; was Form III-G): The certificate for forgings used in a boiler.
- [x] **Form IV Part G** (reg 15; was Form III-H): The certificate for headers and similar standalone pressure parts, such as a steam header.
- [x] **Form IV Part H** (reg 16; was Form III-I): The certificate for dished ends and end covers.
- [x] **Form V Part A** (reg 8(d); was Form IV): The steel maker's certificate for plates and bars: charge numbers, chemical analysis and mechanical test results.
  - Issued/signed by: The steel maker. A Well-known steel maker recognised by the Central Boilers Board can issue it in place of an Inspecting Authority certificate.
  - Filed: Goes with the registration application (regulation 8(d)); the owner keeps it for the boiler's life (regulation 308). Where the original certificate also covers plates used elsewhere, an extract signed by the boiler maker and countersigned by the Inspecting Authority is accepted instead.
  - Records: Charge numbers, plate or bar numbers, and the number and dimensions of plates tested; Chemical analysis; Ultimate tensile strength in kg/mm², percentage elongation and gauge length; Bend and other tests, their results and dates
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form IV, which gave tensile breaking strength in tons; IBR 2026 asks for ultimate tensile strength in kilograms per square millimetre.
- [x] **Form V Part B** (reg 8(d); was Form IV-A): The extract that lists every material test certificate used in a boiler and its components, filed with the maker's Form IV Part A.
  - Issued/signed by: The boiler maker and its representative, from the original material test certificates, with the Competent Person and the Inspecting Authority also signing.
  - Filed: Filed with Form IV Part A in the registration application (regulation 8(d)(iv)). The manufacturer keeps it for at least five years (regulation 308(4)).
  - Records: The boiler's maker's number and drawing number; For each component: quantity, size, cast or heat number and plate number, steel-making process, specification; The steel or part maker, certificate number and date, heat treatment; Chemical analysis (C, Mn, P, S, Si and alloying elements), yield strength and UTS in kg/mm², elongation, bend test; The name of the inspecting authority
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form IV-A. IBR 2026 makes it an explicit part of the registration documents, filed with Form IV Part A.
- [x] **Form VI** (reg 17(2); was Form XV-A to XV-G): Questionnaires for firms seeking recognition as an Inspecting Authority, Competent Authority, well-known steel maker, foundry or forge, tube or pipe maker, material testing laboratory or remnant life assessment organisation.
- [x] **Form VII** (reg 19; was Form XVI-A to XVI-I): The Central Boilers Board's certificates of approval for the bodies and makers recognised under Form VI.
- [x] **Form VIII** (reg 293; new in 2026): The maker's final inspection report for boiler components, approved by the Competent Person; components are erected at site on its strength. New in 2026.
- [x] **Form IX** (reg 295(7) and 295(8); was Form XIX): The details furnished with an application to inspect a boiler used for electric power generation (Appendix H) or a waste heat boiler in a continuous process plant (Appendix I), twelve or thirty-six months after certification.
- [x] **Form X** (reg 300(2); was Form V): The order that lets a newly inspected boiler be used before its registration and certificate are complete.
  - Issued/signed by: The Inspector, after inspecting the boiler and conducting the hydraulic test.
  - Filed: Issued during registration. If its requirements aren't met, fresh orders can be issued for up to six months more, and not beyond twelve months from the first (regulation 300(3)).
  - Records: Who may use the boiler, its registry number and rating, maker and maker's number; The design pressure in kg/cm² (g) at which it may be used; That it lapses six months from its date, pending the issue or refusal of a certificate
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 and the Boilers Act, 1923 this was Form V, under section 9 of that Act.

  - Records: The boiler's particulars and dimensions; Hydraulic and steam tests; Inspection notes, general condition and repairs; Reports of casual visits, main steam pipe inspections and accidents; The design pressure approved by the Chief Inspector
  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form I, which is why older folders call the registration book 'Form I'.

  - Changed: Under IBR 1950 this was Form VI.

### Guides (content/ibr/*.md)

#### /ibr/2026-changes

- [x] **When did IBR 2026 replace IBR 1950?** The Indian Boiler Regulations, 2026 replaced the Indian Boiler Regulations, 1950 on 1 October 2026, the day they were published in the Gazette of India (Extraordinary, No. 596). The Central Boilers Board made them under section 40 of the Boilers Act, 2025, which itself repealed the Boilers Act, 1923. The supersession is "except as respects things done or omitted to be done before such supersession", so what was done under IBR 1950 before 1 October 2026 is not undone by the change.
- [x] **Were the IBR forms renumbered?** Yes. The IBR 2026 forms run from Form I to Form XXIII, and most folder documents changed number. The maker's certificate of manufacture and test, Form III under IBR 1950, is now Form IV Part A. The Inspecting Authority's certificate, Form II(1) or II(2), is now Form I Part B or Part C. The steel maker's certificate, Form IV, is now Form V Part A. Every form, with its old name, is on [the IBR 2026 forms list](/ibr/forms).
- [x] **Can IBR certificates be filed electronically now?** Yes. Under regulation 8 of IBR 2026, the certificates, drawings and specifications that go with a boiler's registration application may be submitted electronically with digital signatures. Well-known pipe and tube makers may also issue their certificates (Form IV Part B and Part C) online with a digital signature.
- [x] **How long must boiler records be kept under IBR 2026?** Regulation 308 sets three periods. The Inspecting Authority keeps a boiler's certificates and approved drawings for at least three years after registration. The owner keeps them for the entire life of the boiler, and hands them to any new owner. The manufacturer keeps them, including Form V Part B, for at least five years. See [boiler record keeping under IBR 2026](/ibr/record-keeping).
- [x] **What changed for boiler manufacturers?** A firm seeking recognition as a manufacturer applies to an Inspecting Authority in its own state with the manufacturer questionnaire, Form III, which is new in IBR 2026 (regulation 7(1)(b)(v)). Recognition lasts three years, and renewal is applied for at least ninety days before it expires (regulation 7(4)). Components can be erected at site on the strength of a final inspection report in Form VIII approved by the Competent Person (regulation 293(2)). The steel maker's certificate, now Form V Part A, gives ultimate tensile strength in kilograms per square millimetre; the IBR 1950 Form IV gave it in tons.
- [x] **What changed in registration?** Under regulation 300 of IBR 2026, the Inspector inspects the boiler, conducts the hydraulic test and issues a provisional order in Form X, under section 14 of the Boilers Act, 2025; under IBR 1950 the provisional order was Form V. The boiler's record is the Memorandum of Inspection Book, now Form XI. See [documents for boiler registration](/ibr/registration-documents).

#### /ibr/record-keeping

- [x] **How long must a boiler manufacturer keep IBR records?** A boiler manufacturer keeps the boiler's records for at least five years under regulation 308(4) of IBR 2026. The same rule covers makers of economisers and steam pipes. The records are the Inspecting Authority's certificates (Form I Part B or C), the component certificates (Form IV Parts B, C and D), the material certificates (Form V Parts A and B), the economiser certificates (Forms XV and XVI) and copies of the approved drawings.
- [x] **How long must the boiler owner keep them?** The owner keeps the boiler's certificates and approved drawings for the entire life of the boiler under regulation 308(2). If the boiler is sold, the records go to the new owner, who keeps them on the same terms (regulation 308(3)).
- [x] **How long does the Inspecting Authority keep them?** The Inspecting Authority keeps a boiler's records, including Form IV Part A, for at least three years after registration, and may then send them to the owner (regulation 308(1)).
- [x] **Where is the boiler's inspection book kept?** The Memorandum of Inspection Book, [Form XI](/ibr/forms/form-xi), is kept in the Chief Inspector's office except when the Competent Person needs it for an inspection (regulation 306). The Chief Inspector also keeps a registration book with the same particulars (regulation 307). If a boiler moves to another state, both books go to that state's Chief Inspector on request (regulation 309).
- [x] **When can the original certificates be asked for?** Within three years of a boiler's registration, if a question arises about its fitness for the approved working pressure, the Chief Inspector can ask the owner to obtain and furnish the original documents (regulation 8(d)(ii)).
- [x] **Why does five years matter for a manufacturer?** Five years of certificates for every boiler made is a large archive. In Shanti Boilers' sample, each material test certificate was used across about 3.2 boilers, so one certificate is evidence in several folders. Shanti's system keeps them in one bank indexed by certificate, cast and plate number, rather than copied into each folder. See [how Shanti's system keeps test certificates](/notes/ibr-statutory-folder-from-bom).

#### /ibr/registration-documents

- [x] **Which documents go with a boiler registration application?** Regulation 8 of the Indian Boiler Regulations, 2026 lists what goes to the Chief Inspector with an application to register a boiler. They may be filed on paper or electronically with digital signatures. 1. **The Inspecting Authority's certificate:** [Form I Part B](/ibr/forms/form-i-part-b) for a shop-assembled boiler, or [Form I Part C](/ibr/forms/form-i-part-c) for one assembled at site. [Form I Part D](/ibr/forms/form-i-part-d) replaces either when permitted variations apply. 2. **The maker's certificate of manufacture and test:** [Form IV Part A](/ibr/forms/form-iv-part-a), signed by the maker. 3. **Drawings:** for a shop-assembled boiler, drawings showing principal dimensions, sections, maker's number, stamp position, bill of material, welding details and design parameters. 4. **Material certificates:** the steel maker's [Form V Part A](/ibr/forms/form-v-part-a), and the [Form V Part B](/ibr/forms/form-v-part-b) extract of every material test certificate used.
- [x] **What else is needed for some boilers?** Fusion-welded drums need a diagram of weld repairs and the heat-treatment temperature charts (regulation 8(d)(v)). Alloy steels used in the creep range in the furnace or superheater zone need two 1,000-hour stress rupture tests for each grade (regulation 8(d)(vi)). Boilers under 20 tons per hour carry all their mountings and fittings certificates; at 20 tons per hour and above, mountings for site-assembled boilers may be supplied separately (regulation 8(d)(vii)).
- [x] **What happens after the application is filed?** Under regulation 300, the Inspector inspects the boiler at site for transit damage and checks the maker's certificate against the boiler. The Inspector then conducts the hydraulic test and issues a provisional order in [Form X](/ibr/forms/form-x), so the boiler can be used while registration completes. The particulars and test results go into the Memorandum of Inspection Book, [Form XI](/ibr/forms/form-xi), which goes to the Chief Inspector with the maker's papers. Once the registry number is issued, the Inspector conducts a steam test and enters it in Form XI (regulation 300(4)); the certificate for use, [Form XII](/ibr/forms/form-xii), follows (regulation 310).
- [x] **When is the registry number engraved?** Within one month of receiving it, the registry number is cut into the boiler's front plate or wherever the Inspector points out, and the engraving is reported to the Chief Inspector within the same month (regulation 301). The number sits below the state's letters, for example GT for Gujarat and MR for Maharashtra; a small industrial boiler's number carries an "S" prefix.

#### /ibr/small-industrial-boilers

- [x] **What counts as a small industrial boiler under IBR 2026?** A small industrial boiler, under regulation 546 of IBR 2026, is one of two kinds. A shell type boiler working up to 7 kg/cm² (g), with a volumetric capacity of more than 25 litres and up to 500 litres, measured from the feed check valve to the main steam stop valve. Or a coil type, once-through or water tube boiler working up to 12 kg/cm² (g), with a capacity of up to 150 litres. A boiler combining both kinds is held to 7 kg/cm² (g) and 500 litres in total.
- [x] **Which stages are inspected for a small shell boiler?** An Inspecting Authority inspects a small industrial boiler during construction (regulation 550). For a shell type boiler, regulation 551 sets four stages: material identification and inspection; each component after welding and drilling; assembly of the boiler; and the hydraulic test. A welded boiler is tested at twice its design pressure, and the maker provides a test coupon for root and face bend tests.
- [x] **Which certificate does a small industrial boiler need?** A small industrial boiler's certificate of manufacture and test is [Form XXII](/ibr/forms/form-xxii) (regulation 555(2)). Under IBR 1950 this was Form XVII, which also covered small solar boilers; they now have their own Form XXIII. If the boiler's plate is too small to stamp every detail, the certificate carries them, but the name, works number and year of make are always stamped or shown on the boiler (regulation 553(2)).
- [x] **Which plates can a small industrial boiler use, and is a steel maker's certificate needed?** A small industrial boiler's plates are IS 2062 fully killed steel or equivalent, at least 6 mm thick for the shell and heads, and at least 8 mm where heads serve as tube sheets for rolled-in tubes (regulation 548(2)(a)). The steel maker's certificate in Form V Part A is not required for IS 2062 plates, provided every other certification requirement is met (regulation 548(2)(b)).
- [x] **How is a small industrial boiler's registry number marked?** A small industrial boiler's registry number carries an "S" prefix below the state's letters, for example "GT" over "S-" and the number for a boiler registered in Gujarat (regulation 301(1)(c)).
